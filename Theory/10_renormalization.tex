\chapter{Multivariate-Gaussian Operator Renormalization}
\label{ch:gaussian-renormalization}

\Cref{ch:renormalization} separates a genuine scale diagram from a sequence
of differently sized objects.  This chapter instantiates that diagram on the
multivariate-Gaussian (MVG) interaction sector of
\Cref{ch:gaussian-coarsegraining}.  The flowed object is an operator
parameter, not automatically a normalized Gaussian law: a law-level flow
also needs a coarse reference measure, an information-vector rule, and a
finite normalizer at every scale.  Likewise, the hard aggregation below is
only one admissible coarse arrow.  The effective-support, sheaf, and Kron
reductions of \Cref{ch:gaussian-coarsegraining} give other arrows on narrower
domains. Unless a channel index is displayed, this chapter flows the
belief/recognition operator sector; a model-channel flow is a separate typed
construction with its own dimension, links, and parameters. \status{DEFINITION}

\section{The MVG operator arrow}
\label{sec:grg-operator-arrow}

At level \(\ell\), let
\(\boldsymbol\Xi_\ell^{\mathrm{MVG}}
=((A_i^{(\ell)}),(W_{ij}^{(\ell)}))\) with
\(A_i^{(\ell)},W_{ij}^{(\ell)}\in\PSD^K\), and assemble
\begin{equation}
(\Lambda_\ell)_{ii}
 =A_i^{(\ell)}+\sum_{j\ne i}W_{ij}^{(\ell)},
\qquad
(\Lambda_\ell)_{ij}=-W_{ij}^{(\ell)}.
\label{eq:grg-family}
\end{equation}
For a hard partition, write
\(S_\ell=\widehat S_\ell\otimes I_K\).  Its unrescaled aggregation map is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{S_\ell}(\Lambda_\ell)
 =S_\ell^\top\Lambda_\ell S_\ell,
\qquad
\mathcal A_{S_\ell}(\boldsymbol\Xi_\ell^{\mathrm{MVG}})
 =\left(
 \left(\sum_{i\in I}A_i^{(\ell)}\right)_I,
 \left(\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\j\in J}}W_{ij}^{(\ell)}\right)_{I\ne J}
 \right).
\label{eq:grg-aggregation}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

The second identity is exact on the hard-aggregation domain proved in
\Cref{sec:cg-gaussian-aggregation}; nonflat data must first satisfy the
effective-support or rectangular endpoint conditions of
\Cref{sec:cg-effective-support,sec:cg-sheaf-closure}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Aggregation is a positive semigroup action}{prop:grg-aggregation-semigroup}
If \(S_1\) maps fine nodes to intermediate clusters and \(S_2\) maps those
clusters to coarse clusters, then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A_{S_2}\mathcal A_{S_1}
 =\mathcal A_{S_1S_2}.
\label{eq:grg-semigroup}
\end{equation}
Each \(\mathcal A_S\) is linear and positive on the product cone of node and
edge weights.  It is generally noninjective because every edge internal to a
cluster is annihilated. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Congruence gives
\(S_2^\top(S_1^\top\Lambda S_1)S_2
=(S_1S_2)^\top\Lambda(S_1S_2)\).
The parameter formula follows by associativity of finite sums.  Sums preserve
positive semidefiniteness, so the map is positive.  Changing an internal
weight while holding every cut weight and self term fixed leaves
\(\mathcal A_S(\pi)\) unchanged, proving noninjectivity.

An RG arrow additionally declares a positive rescaling:
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{\ell+1}
 =\zeta_\ell^{-1}\mathcal A_{S_\ell}(\Lambda_\ell),
\qquad
\zeta_\ell>0.
\label{eq:grg-flow}
\end{equation}
This is the MVG component of the comparison data required by
\Cref{sec:rg-scale-category}.  The source and target usually have different
dimensions.  A fixed ray is therefore typed only after comparison maps
identify a repeated hierarchy with one fixed parameter space.  Without that
identification, \eqref{eq:grg-flow} is a cocycle of positive maps, not an
autonomous dynamical system.

\paragraph{Equal-block spectral bounds.}
If every cluster has \(b\) nodes, then \(S^\top S=bI\).  For every symmetric
\(\Lambda\),
\begin{equation}
b\lambda_{\min}(\Lambda)
\leq\lambda_{\min}(S^\top\Lambda S),
\qquad
\lambda_{\max}(S^\top\Lambda S)
\leq b\lambda_{\max}(\Lambda).
\label{eq:grg-spectral-bounds}
\end{equation}
Indeed, congruence preserves the Loewner inequalities
\(\lambda_{\min}(\Lambda)I\preceq\Lambda
\preceq\lambda_{\max}(\Lambda)I\).
These are finite-level bounds.  Divergence or convergence as
\(\ell\to\infty\) additionally requires an infinite hierarchy and a declared
normalization. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{The biadditive closed family}
\label{sec:grg-biadditive}

Let each node carry \(x_i\geq0\), and fix \(A,M\in\PSD^K\).  The family
\begin{equation}
A_i=x_iA,\qquad W_{ij}=x_ix_jM
\label{eq:grg-biadditive}
\end{equation}
is exactly closed under every hard partition: with
\(x_I=\sum_{i\in I}x_i\),
\begin{equation}
A_I=x_IA,\qquad W_{IJ}=x_Ix_JM.
\label{eq:grg-biadditive-coarse}
\end{equation}
This follows from distributivity and proves closure, not attraction.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Positive additive rules are forced to be linear}{prop:grg-positive-additive-linear}
Suppose
\(\alpha:\mathbb R_+\to\PSD^K\) is additive and
\(w:\mathbb R_+^2\to\PSD^K\) is symmetric and separately additive.
Then there are \(A,M\in\PSD^K\) such that
\begin{equation}
\alpha(x)=xA,\qquad w(x,y)=xyM.
\label{eq:grg-additive-classification}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:grg-biadditive} is the maximal positive family among rules
depending only on additive scalar node masses and obeying those additivity
hypotheses. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
If \(y\geq x\), additivity gives
\(\alpha(y)-\alpha(x)=\alpha(y-x)\succeq0\); hence \(\alpha\) is Loewner
monotone.  For every \(v\), the scalar function
\(x\mapsto v^\top\alpha(x)v\) is additive, nonnegative, and therefore
monotone.  A monotone additive function on \(\mathbb R_+\) is linear, so
\(v^\top\alpha(x)v=xv^\top\alpha(1)v\).  Polarization yields
\(\alpha(x)=x\alpha(1)\).  For fixed \(y\), the same argument gives
\(w(x,y)=xw(1,y)\); applying it to the second argument gives
\(w(1,y)=yw(1,1)\).  Take \(A=\alpha(1)\) and \(M=w(1,1)\).

On a homogeneous complete graph with equal blocks of size \(b\), the
unrescaled map acts on the common pair \((A,W)\) by
\begin{equation}
A\longmapsto bA,\qquad W\longmapsto b^2W.
\label{eq:grg-homogeneous-map}
\end{equation}
The dense normalization \(\zeta=b^2\) fixes the coupling sector and contracts
the self sector by \(1/b\).  Consequently the candidate limiting ray has
\(A=0\).  It is a boundary operator: its consensus directions lie in the
kernel, so it does not define a full-space normalized Gaussian density.
Pinning, quotienting the kernel, and retaining an external positive mass are
different repairs and define different probability problems.  A
pseudoinverse inserted into the original Lebesgue density is not a fourth
repair.

The exponent \(2\) is not topology independent. For a typical block
\(B(b)\) and coarse cut \(\partial B(b)\), define the weighted sums
\[
W_{\partial}(b)=\sum_{e\in\partial B(b)}W_e,
\qquad
A_B(b)=\sum_{i\in B(b)}A_i.
\]
If, in a declared matrix norm,
\(\lVert W_{\partial}(b)\rVert\asymp b^{s_W}\) and
\(\lVert A_B(b)\rVert\asymp b^{s_A}\), then a scalar normalization that keeps
the coupling sector nondegenerate scales as \(b^{s_W}\), while the normalized
self sector scales as \(b^{s_A-s_W}\). Edge count alone does not determine
either exponent when the fine weights vary with \(b\).

The count-based exponents follow only under explicit nonzero-average
assumptions. If \(n_{\mathrm{cut}}(b)\asymp b^s\) and
\[
\frac{W_{\partial}(b)}{n_{\mathrm{cut}}(b)}\longrightarrow\overline W\ne0,
\qquad
\frac{A_B(b)}{b}\longrightarrow\overline A\ne0,
\]
then \(s_W=s\), \(s_A=1\), and the coupling and normalized-self exponents are
respectively \(s\) and \(1-s\). For the homogeneous complete-graph scheme
above, a cut between two \(\Theta(b)\)-sized blocks has \(s=2\); consecutive
blocks in a nearest-neighbor chain have \(s=0\). Relevance of the self term is
therefore a property of the declared
weighted graph family and normalization scheme, not of
\eqref{eq:grg-flow} alone. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Cone dynamics: what Birkhoff does and does not prove}
\label{sec:grg-cones}

Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_N
=\left(\prod_i\PSD^K\right)
 \times\left(\prod_{i<j}\PSD^K\right)
\label{eq:grg-full-cone}
\end{equation}
be the full MVG parameter cone.  On the interior of a proper cone
\(\mathcal K\), Hilbert's projective metric is
\begin{equation}
d_{\mathrm H}(x,y)
=\log\left(
\frac{\inf\{\beta>0:x\preceq_{\mathcal K}\beta y\}}
{\sup\{\alpha>0:\alpha y\preceq_{\mathcal K}x\}}
\right).
\label{eq:grg-hilbert}
\end{equation}
A positive endomorphism of finite projective diameter contracts this metric;
a primitive finite-dimensional positive matrix is the familiar special
case \citep{Birkhoff1957,nussbaum1988hilbert}.  The hypotheses fail for
sum-only MVG aggregation on the full cone.

\propositionheading{The full-cone route is blocked}{prop:grg-full-cone-blocked}
After a hierarchical identification makes \(\mathcal A_S\) an endomorphism,
sum-only aggregation is not primitive on \(\mathcal K_N\).  Moreover, the
dense candidate ray with \(A=0\) lies on \(\partial\mathcal K_N\), whereas a
full-cone Birkhoff contraction has an interior Perron ray.  Hence Birkhoff's
full-cone theorem cannot prove attraction to that candidate. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
An absent coarse cut remains a zero edge coordinate, so the map can meet the
boundary even from positive data on the represented fine edges.  More
sharply, define for a subspace \(U\subseteq\mathbb R^K\)
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F_U
=\{(W_e)_e:\operatorname{ran}W_e\subseteq U\ \text{for every }e\}.
\label{eq:grg-common-range-face}
\end{equation}
Each \(\mathcal F_U\) is an invariant face because the range of a sum of
positive semidefinite matrices contained in \(U\) is still contained in
\(U\).  Proper invariant faces rule out primitivity.  Finally, \(A=0\) is a
boundary coordinate of \(\prod_i\PSD^K\), while the Perron ray of a strict
interior contraction is interior.

The coupling cone alone removes the vanishing self coordinate but not the
matrix obstruction.  Suppose an identified spatial map \(B\geq0\) has a
positive eigenvector \(c=(c_e)_e\).  Because aggregation only adds weights,
for every \(M\in\PSD^K\)
\begin{equation}
W_e=c_eM
\label{eq:grg-inert-fiber}
\end{equation}
is an eigenray with the same spatial eigenvalue.  Different matrix rays are
not mixed; on the raw product cone their projective separation can be
infinite.  Thus sum-only aggregation cannot select a fiber direction.  It
can at most contract a spatial cone after the fiber ray has been declared.

\conjectureheading{Fixed-\(B\) scalarized attraction}{conj:grg-fixed-b-attraction}
Fix \(M_0\succ0\) and restrict to
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K^W_{M_0}
=\{(c_eM_0)_e:c_e\geq0\}.
\label{eq:grg-scalarized-cone}
\end{equation}
If a hierarchical identification yields one fixed spatial endomorphism
\(B\), some power \(B^q\) is strictly positive, and its Perron vector
factorizes as \(c_{ij}=x_ix_j\), then Birkhoff's theorem gives a unique
attracting spatial ray \(W_{ij}=x_ix_jM_0\) on this scalarized cone.
\status{CONJECTURE}

This statement freezes every matrix direction.  It is not a theorem about
the full coupling cone, a nonautonomous sequence \(B_\ell\), or the
common-principal RG state of \Cref{sec:rg-common-principal-state}. Alternating positive
maps may contract projective distances while approaching a periodic orbit,
as \Cref{sec:rg-dynamical-types} shows.  The missing obligations are a fixed
hierarchical identification, primitivity of the resulting \(B\), and the
factorization of its Perron vector.

\propositionheading{Residual fiber label}{prop:grg-residual-fiber-label}
For \(M\in\PSD^K\), the orbit under residual global reframing
\(M\mapsto h^\top Mh\), \(h\in\GL(K)\), is determined exactly by
\(\operatorname{rank}M\).  Hence the biadditive ansatz has \(K+1\) internal
congruence classes and no continuous internal modulus. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Sylvester's law of inertia classifies real symmetric matrices under
congruence \citep{HornJohnson2013}.  A positive semidefinite matrix has no
negative eigenvalues, so its inertia is determined by its rank.  In
particular, every \(M\succ0\) is congruent to \(I_K\), witnessed by
\(h=M^{-1/2}\).

Rank does not classify the spatial graph, the limiting spectral measure,
corrections to scaling, basin, or scheme.  Nor does the proposition extend
unchanged to a nonflat pair \((W,\Theta)\), whose residual action combines
congruence on \(W\) with conjugation on \(\Theta\).

\section{An exact finite-sector gate}
\label{sec:grg-numerical-gate}

The homogeneous calculation
\eqref{eq:grg-homogeneous-map} is exact.  On
\(\Sym^K\oplus\Sym^K\), its eigenvalues are \(b^2\) on the coupling sector
and \(b\) on the self sector, each with multiplicity \(K(K+1)/2\).  The
spectral ratio outside the dominant eigenspace is therefore
\begin{equation}
\frac{b}{b^2}=\frac1b,
\label{eq:grg-sector-gap}
\end{equation}
equal to \(0.5\) at \(b=2\).  Comparing the two largest entries of the sorted
spectrum would incorrectly return one because the dominant eigenvalue is
degenerate. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Equation~\eqref{eq:grg-sector-gap} is the complete finite-sector result.
Attraction, uniqueness, and scheme independence remain open because they
require a declared invariant cone, hierarchy, and limiting protocol.
\status{OPEN}

\section{Gauge-invariant spectral data}
\label{sec:grg-spectra}

Let \(L\) be the interaction Laplacian obtained from
\eqref{eq:grg-family} by setting all \(A_i=0\).  Both \(L\) and
\(\Lambda=L+A\) are bilinear forms.  Represent a local reframing by the
invertible new-to-old coordinate map
\[
G:\mathbb R_{\mathrm{new}}^{NK}\xrightarrow{\sim}
\mathbb R_{\mathrm{old}}^{NK},
\qquad
G=\operatorname{diag}(g_1,\ldots,g_N).
\]
Pullback to the new coordinates sends both forms by congruence,
\((L,\Lambda)\mapsto(G^\top LG,G^\top\Lambda G)\).
Ordinary eigenvalues of either matrix are therefore frame dependent.  The
generalized spectrum of the pair is invariant when the pencil is regular.

\propositionheading{Regular-pencil invariance}{prop:grg-pencil-invariance}
Suppose \(\det(L-d\Lambda)\) is not the zero polynomial.  Then
\((L,\Lambda)\) and
\((G^\top LG,G^\top\Lambda G)\) have the same generalized eigenvalues with
the same multiplicities.  If \(\Lambda\) is invertible, there are exactly
\(NK\) finite roots. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
\begin{equation}
\det(G^\top LG-dG^\top\Lambda G)
=\det(G)^2\det(L-d\Lambda).
\label{eq:grg-pencil-congruence}
\end{equation}
The nonzero factor does not change roots or regularity.  When
\(\Lambda\) is invertible, the coefficient of \(d^{NK}\) is
\((-1)^{NK}\det\Lambda\ne0\).

\propositionheading{Regularity criterion}{prop:grg-pencil-regularity}
For \(L,A\succeq0\) and \(\Lambda=L+A\), the pencil
\((L,\Lambda)\) is regular if and only if
\begin{equation}
\ker L\cap\ker A=\{0\}.
\label{eq:grg-regularity}
\end{equation}
In that case \(\Lambda\succ0\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Positive semidefiniteness gives
\(\ker(L+A)=\ker L\cap\ker A\).  A nonzero common-kernel vector annihilates
\(L-d\Lambda\) for every \(d\), so the determinant is identically zero.
Conversely, if the intersection is trivial then
\(L+\Lambda=2L+A\succ0\); evaluating at \(d=-1\) proves that the determinant
polynomial is not identically zero.  The same intersection identity gives
\(\ker\Lambda=\{0\}\).

At the dense candidate ray \(A=0\), one has \(\Lambda=L\) and
\(\det(L-dL)\equiv0\).  Passing to a complement of \(\ker L\) produces the
constant generalized spectrum \(\{1\}\), not a graph-sensitive label.
Pinning and quotienting therefore do not preserve the same spectral object.

\propositionheading{Mass-pencil transfer}{prop:grg-mass-pencil-transfer}
Let \(R\succ0\), \(a>0\), and \(\Lambda=L+aR\).  Generalized eigenvalues of
\((L,R)\) and \((L,\Lambda)\) are related by
\begin{equation}
d=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+a},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{ad}{1-d}.
\label{eq:grg-mass-transfer}
\end{equation}
The correspondence preserves eigenvectors, multiplicities, and ordering.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
N_d(t)=N_\lambda\left(\frac{at}{1-t}\right),
\qquad 0\leq t<1.
\label{eq:grg-counting-transfer}
\end{equation}
If \(N_\lambda(s)-N_\lambda(0)\sim Cs^\alpha\) near zero, then at fixed
\(a>0\),
\(N_d(t)-N_d(0)\sim Ca^\alpha t^\alpha\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The equation \(Lv=d(L+aR)v\) is equivalent, for \(d\ne1\), to
\(Lv=(ad/(1-d))Rv\).  The case \(d=1\) would require \(Rv=0\), which is
impossible.  Monotonicity of the fractional map gives the counting identity
and its asymptotic consequence.

Mass removal and the infrared limit do not commute:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{a\downarrow0}\lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+a}=0,
\qquad
\lim_{\lambda\downarrow0}\lim_{a\downarrow0}
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+a}=1.
\label{eq:grg-noncommuting-mass}
\end{equation}
Thus a positive mass regularizes each finite problem but does not supply a
mass-independent fixed-ray spectrum.

\paragraph{Thermodynamic spectral labels.}
For a declared sequence \((V_n,L_n,R_n)\), define
\begin{equation}
N_n(t)
=\frac{1}{|V_n|K}
\#\{k:\lambda_k^{(n)}\leq t\}.
\label{eq:grg-ids}
\end{equation}
A cumulative exponent
\(N(t)-N(0)\sim Ct^\alpha\) is meaningful only after proving convergence
\(N_n\to N\) in a stated mode.  A fixed finite graph has an atomic measure,
so a density exponent is not defined there.  RG depth \(\ell\) and
thermodynamic size \(n\) form the two-index family of
\Cref{sec:rg-two-index-limits}; neither the existence nor the commutation of
those limits follows from the finite congruence identity. \status{OPEN}

Equality of one exponent is not equality of universality classes.  The full
limiting measure, amplitudes, corrections to scaling, invariant faces,
basin, and blocking scheme may still differ.  Projective eigenvalue ratios,
block-normalized scaling dimensions, and matched heat/IDS exponents are
candidate invariant observables; the raw running matrices are
scheme-dependent coordinates.

\section{Finite stochastic refinement on matrix cones}
\label{sec:grg-stochastic-refinement}

\Cref{sec:rg-projective-refinement} proves the general typing statement:
a stochastic right section samples a fine preimage and is not an inverse of
the realized coarse map.  The matrix cone sharpens both the obstruction and
the available finite construction.

The central Wishart family has Laplace transform
\(\det(I+\sigma\Theta)^{-p}\). Apart from the degenerate law
\(\delta_0\), it is infinitely divisible exactly when
\(\operatorname{rank}\sigma=1\) \citep{Mayerhofer2010Wishart}. Therefore a
full-rank Wishart prior cannot provide convolution roots of every order for
\(K\geq2\); the surviving rank-one case is an embedded scalar Gamma law on
a proper face.  This does not mean full-cone convolution semigroups are
absent. Define the trace-one cone
\[
\mathbb S_{+,1}^K
=\{U\succeq0:\operatorname{tr}U=1\}.
\]
Let \(\alpha\) be a finite positive Borel measure on
\(\mathbb S_{+,1}^K\), and let
\(\beta:\mathbb S_{+,1}^K\to(0,\infty)\) be measurable with
\[
\int_{\mathbb S_{+,1}^K}
\log\left(1+\beta(U)^{-1}\right)\alpha(dU)<\infty.
\]
For \(\Theta\succeq0\), the corresponding matrix-Gamma law on
\(\PSD^K\) has Laplace transform
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\alpha,\beta}(\Theta)
=\exp\left[
-\int_{\mathbb S_{+,1}^K}
\log\left(
1+\frac{\operatorname{tr}(U\Theta)}{\beta(U)}
\right)\alpha(dU)
\right],
\label{eq:grg-matrix-gamma}
\end{equation}
and, for fixed \(\beta\), replacing \(\alpha\) by \(t\alpha\), \(t\geq0\),
produces a convolution semigroup; \(t=0\) is \(\delta_0\)
\citep{PerezAbreuStelzer2014}. Generic full-cone draws need not lie in one
Kron-closed chart, however.

\theoremheading{Congruence-diagonal Gamma--Dirichlet bridge}{thm:grg-gamma-dirichlet-bridge}
Fix the Kron-closed cone
\(\mathcal C_H\) from \Cref{sec:cg-kron}, channel parameters
\(a_r,\beta_r>0\), and intensities \(t_e>0\).  Independently draw
\begin{equation}
X_{e,r}\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(t_ea_r,\text{ rate }\beta_r),
\qquad
W_e=H\operatorname{diag}(X_{e,1},\ldots,X_{e,K})H^\top.
\label{eq:grg-gamma-weights}
\end{equation}
For a coarse coordinate \(f\) formed from constituents \(E_f\),
\(\sum_{e\in E_f}W_e\) has the same form with intensity
\(\sum_{e\in E_f}t_e\).  Conditioned on
\[
W_f=H\operatorname{diag}(c_1,\ldots,c_K)H^\top,
\]
sample independently over channels
\begin{equation}
(P_{e,r})_{e\in E_f}
\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}((t_ea_r)_{e\in E_f}),
\qquad
X_{e,r}=c_rP_{e,r}.
\label{eq:grg-dirichlet-bridge}
\end{equation}
Then every \(W_e\in\mathcal C_H\) and
\(\sum_{e\in E_f}W_e=W_f\) exactly.  Applied independently to coarse self
terms and cut edges, this is a stochastic right section of any fixed finite
aggregation map on the represented coordinates. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Scalar Gamma variables with a common rate add by addition of their shape
parameters.  Conditional on their sum, their proportions have the
Dirichlet law in \eqref{eq:grg-dirichlet-bridge}.  The identities hold
channelwise, and multiplication by the fixed congruence \(H(\cdot)H^\top\)
preserves both \(\mathcal C_H\) and addition.

Internal fine edges are absent from the coarse coordinates and require a
separate prior.  The theorem neither recovers the discarded realization nor
constructs a two-sided inverse.  An infinite graph-level construction still
owes compatible internal-edge priors, nested bridge kernels, gauge-covariant
chart data, cylinder consistency, and tightness or another projective-limit
theorem. \status{OPEN}

\section{Relations to Bayesian, Laplacian, network, and learned RG}
\label{sec:grg-literature-boundary}

\paragraph{Bayesian renormalization.}
For a Gaussian mean family with fixed precision \(\Lambda\), the Fisher
metric in the mean coordinates is \(\Lambda\), and the inverse Fisher
cometric is \(\Lambda^{-1}\).  This local identity makes an MVG comparison
with Bayesian renormalization natural.  Berman, Klinger, and Stapleton,
however, begin with a posterior over model parameters. In a late-data local
Gaussian regime, the delta method pushes an inverse Fisher cometric through a
forward map to obtain a sample-space diffusion tensor, exactly for an affine
map and to leading local order for a nonlinear map
\citep{BermanKlingerStapleton2023,BermanKlinger2024Inverse}. Howard,
Klinger, Maiti, and Stapleton subsequently apply Bayesian RG to neural
network field theories \citep{HowardKlingerMaitiStapleton2025}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Hard energy precomposition \(\Lambda\mapsto S^\top\Lambda S\) is not that
posterior flow. \status{ESTABLISHED}

No theorem identifies their scale orientation,
connection data, or global continuation with \eqref{eq:grg-flow}; the
comparison therefore supplies no ``opposite-flow'' theorem between this
operator coarsening and Bayesian learning. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\paragraph{Laplacian RG.}
With a positive reference form \(R\), the generalized heat generator
\(R^{-1}L\) transforms by similarity, so its spectrum and heat trace are
gauge invariant.  The scalar Laplacian-RG construction motivates the
entropy scale diagnostic and the positive susceptibility
\begin{equation}
C(\tau)=-\frac{dS}{d\log\tau}
\label{eq:grg-heat-capacity}
\end{equation}
\citep{villegas2023laplacian}.  Raw off-diagonal heat-kernel blocks transform
as \(K_{ij}\mapsto g_i^{-1}K_{ij}g_j\), however, and are not invariant scalar
node affinities. \status{ESTABLISHED}

A gauge-invariant real-space block selector, zero-mode
treatment, and entropy normalization remain open.  The heat scale diagnostic
does not by itself prove a unique or scheme-independent partition.
\status{OPEN}

\paragraph{Network RG.}
Exact closure occurs in the independent-edge multiscale family of Garuccio,
Lalli, and Garlaschelli, whose hidden variables aggregate additively
\citep{Garuccio2023Multiscale}. It is a characterized family, not a theorem
for arbitrary hidden-variable networks. Interaction closure after a
separately specified elimination is a different result. Interacting-network
RG can generate higher-order interactions
outside restricted coordination classes
\citep{CatanzaroGarlaschelliPatil2026}; it does not imply exact closure of
arbitrary matrix-weighted node aggregation.  Conversely,
\eqref{eq:grg-aggregation} does not integrate out edge variables and should
not be presented as that network transformation. The broader network-RG
taxonomy is reviewed by \citet{gabrielli2025network}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Graph learning and variational RG.}
The Galerkin identity
\(L_c=S^\top LS\) preserves the restricted quadratic energy.  It does not
imply spectral approximation, message-passing commutation, or a graph neural
network realization; those require the approximation and residual controls
separated in \Cref{sec:cg-galerkin}
\citep{Loukas2019,JolyKeriven2024}. DiffPool instead learns soft cluster
assignments end to end \citep{Ying2018DiffPool}; that modeling choice does
not by itself provide an exact RG semigroup or a closure theorem.
Oono and Suzuki prove conditional exponential information loss for deep
graph convolutional networks under spectral and weight hypotheses
\citep{OonoSuzuki2020}; their result is an oversmoothing boundary, not a
Galerkin-commutation theorem. Likewise,
Mehta and Schwab's exact variational-RG/deep-network correspondence assumes a
hidden-variable kernel satisfying its trace condition
\citep{MehtaSchwab2014}.  A deterministic identification \(y=S\widetilde y\)
does not supply that kernel or trace condition, so their exact mapping is a
precedent within its own construction, not a proof that
\eqref{eq:grg-flow} is a deep network. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Remaining MVG obligations}
\label{sec:grg-obligations}

The operator recursion is exact on its declared domain, but the following
extensions remain open.  Partial fixed-section fibers and rectangular
cellular-sheaf endpoint maps are already structurally closed under nested
blocking by \Cref{sec:cg-partial,sec:cg-sheaf-closure}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

This structural result does not supply a canonical
compression to one invertible link and one SPD weight or an RG rescaling
across variable-rank fibers.  A full MVG law flow must specify the information
vector, reference measure, and normalizer and prove that every iterate stays
in the Gaussian natural domain.  Attraction needs a typed autonomous
endomorphism or a cocycle theorem, a basin, and a matrix mixing mechanism or
an explicit scalarized restriction.  Universality needs scheme-invariant
observables and the two-index convergence required by
\Cref{sec:rg-two-index-limits}.  A gauge-invariant, nondegenerate block
selector remains open.  Stochastic inverse RG needs graph-level projective
priors, including priors for annihilated internal edges. \status{OPEN}

\paragraph{Physicist summary.}
The MVG interaction precision has a clean positive coarse map:
cut weights and self terms add, and a declared scalar restores the chosen
units.  On dense homogeneous graphs the coupling sector scales like \(b^2\)
while the self sector scales like \(b\), giving an exact relative factor
\(1/b\).  That algebra produces a boundary operator ray, not a normalized
Gaussian fixed law.  Birkhoff contraction becomes plausible only after
freezing the matrix direction and constructing one primitive spatial
endomorphism; sum-only aggregation preserves matrix faces and cannot select
that direction.  Generalized spectra and Gamma--Dirichlet finite refinements
are exact gauge-compatible tools, but neither solves scheme independence,
nonflat hierarchy, or the infinite projective-limit problem.
