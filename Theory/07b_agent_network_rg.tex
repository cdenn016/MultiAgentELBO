\chapter[Exact Agent-Network Renormalization]{Exact Renormalization of\\Agent Networks}
\label{ch:agent-network-rg}

This chapter constructs an effective variational theory for the interaction
model of \Cref{ch:local-collective-elbo}.  Its primary objects are normalized
measures and Markov kernels.  Actions, couplings, and differential beta
functions appear only after a reference and a scale identification have been
declared.  Exact closure is obtained by retaining induced hyperedges, marked
attention events, complete holonomy data, and path-space memory.  A pairwise,
memoryless model with one scalar attention row and one averaged group link is
therefore a truncation, not the general effective theory.

\section{Law-level coarse-graining and the VFE identity}
\label{sec:rg-law-level-vfe}

Let $P(do,dy)$ be the fixed normalized joint law on standard-Borel spaces, let
$o$ be regular, let $\Pi_o(dy)$ be its posterior, and let
$Q_o\ll\Pi_o$ be a recognition law.  A coarse channel between standard-Borel
latent spaces is a fixed Markov kernel
\begin{equation}
C:\mathsf Y\rightsquigarrow\mathsf Z
\label{eq:rg-coarse-channel}
\end{equation}
that does not read $Q_o$ and does not alter the observation coordinate.  Define
\begin{equation}
P^c(do,dz)=\int_{\mathsf Y}C(y,dz)P(do,dy),
\qquad
\Pi_o^c=\Pi_oC,
\qquad
Q_o^c=Q_oC.
\label{eq:rg-common-pushforward}
\end{equation}

\theoremheading{Exact coarse VFE and discarded conditional information}{thm:rg-exact-coarse-vfe}
Let the fine and coarse VFE expressions be defined as extended-real sums, with
the common finite evidence term separated from their KL gaps, and let
\begin{equation}
\widehat Q_o(dy,dz)=Q_o(dy)C(y,dz),
\qquad
\widehat\Pi_o(dy,dz)=\Pi_o(dy)C(y,dz).
\label{eq:rg-bridge-lifts}
\end{equation}
Then $P^c$ has the same observation evidence as $P$, and the extended-real
additive identity is
\begin{equation}
\Fenergy_P(Q_o)
=\Fenergy_{P^c}(Q_o^c)+\int_{\mathsf Z}
\KL\bigl(\widehat Q_o(dy\given z)
\Vert\widehat\Pi_o(dy\given z)\bigr)Q_o^c(dz)
.
\label{eq:rg-vfe-chain-rule}
\end{equation}
In particular, the fine VFE is at least the coarse VFE.  If the fine KL is
finite, their ordinary real-valued difference equals the displayed
conditional KL.
The corresponding coarse ELBO rises only because a conditional inference gap
has been discarded; the evidence has not increased.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The observation marginal is unchanged because $C(y,\mathsf Z)=1$.  Attaching
the same channel to both measures preserves the fine relative entropy,
$\KL(Q_o\Vert\Pi_o)=\KL(\widehat Q_o\Vert\widehat\Pi_o)$.  Disintegration on
$z$ and the relative-entropy chain rule split the latter into
$\KL(Q_o^c\Vert\Pi_o^c)$ plus the integral in
\eqref{eq:rg-vfe-chain-rule}.  Substitute the exact evidence identities at the
two scales.  $\square$

The theorem does not cover different generative and recognition channels, a
fitted rather than pushed-forward $P^c$, or a simultaneous coarse-graining of
the observation.  Such operations generally change the comparison target or
the evidence event.  An invertible or evidence-sufficient observation channel
is an exception, but its preserved event and posterior must be proved
explicitly.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Full pointwise probabilistic datum for a candidate parent}{thm:rg-pointwise-parent-datum}
Fix a nonempty finite child block $I$, a parent label $A$, the common domain
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U_A=\bigcap_{i\in I}\mathcal C_i,
\qquad r_*\in\mathcal U_A,
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-overlap}
\end{equation}
and structural data $X$ with $X_A=\chi_A(X)$. Let
$\mathsf O,\mathsf Y_I,\mathsf B_A,\mathsf M_A,\boldsymbol\Xi_A,$ and
$\mathsf H_A$ be nonempty standard-Borel spaces, and put
\begin{equation}
\mathsf Z_A=\mathsf B_A\times\mathsf M_A\times\boldsymbol\Xi_A\times\mathsf H_A.
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-space}
\end{equation}
Keep $X$ and $X_A$ outside the random channel. Supply the fixed normalized generative joint
\[
\mathbb P_I(Do,DY\mid X),
\]
and its selected measurable posterior-version kernel $o\mapsto\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}$.
At an admitted $o$ with a finite positive evidence representative, supply a normalized correlated recognition law
$\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}\ll\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}$. Declare one normalized measurable
Markov-kernel channel:
\begin{equation}
C_A:\mathsf Y_I\rightsquigarrow\mathsf Z_A.
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-channel}
\end{equation}
It is fixed independently of recognition, posterior, recognition
parameters, and realized observation. It acts only on the fine random variables and leaves the
observation coordinate in $\mathsf O$ unchanged. Define
\begin{align}
\mathbb P_A(Do,Dz\mid X)
&=\int_{\mathsf Y_I}C_A(Y,Dz)\mathbb P_I(Do,DY\mid X),
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-generative}\\
\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}&=\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}C_A,\\
\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}&=\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}C_A
\quad\text{(same normalized Markov-kernel channel).}
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-triple}
\end{align}
Then all three parent laws are normalized, the parent observation marginal equals the fine one,
$o\mapsto\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}$ is the selected parent posterior-version kernel, and
$\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}\ll\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}$.

Declare a jointly measurable normalized parent evaluator
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{ev}_A:\mathsf M_A\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Kern}(\boldsymbol\Xi_A,
\mathsf B_A\times\mathsf O\times\mathsf H_A),
\qquad m_A\longmapsto K^{X_A}_{A,m_A}.
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-evaluator}
\end{equation}
It is compatible with the parent generative law either when it is the family induced by a selected
standard-Borel disintegration, or, for a family declared independently of $\mathbb P_A$, under the
separate almost-sure compatibility hypothesis
\begin{equation}
\mathbb P_A(db_A,do,dh_A\mid\xi_A,m_A,X)
=K^{X_A}_{A,m_A}(\xi_A;db_A,do,dh_A),
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-evaluator-compatibility}
\end{equation}
for the parent $(M_A,\Xi_A)$ marginal almost surely. Disintegration proves existence of the induced
tier; it does not validate an arbitrary predeclared evaluator, select its null extension canonically,
make evaluation injective, or regularize a quotient of presentations.

With $\mathbb P_A^Z(D\mid X)=\mathbb P_A(\mathsf O\times D\mid X)$ and locally fixed $(o,X)$,
the parent recognition, prior, and posterior marginals are the coordinate pushforwards
\begin{align}
q_A^b&=(\operatorname{pr}_b)_\#\mathbb Q_{A,o,X},
&q_A^m&=(\operatorname{pr}_m)_\#\mathbb Q_{A,o,X},
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-recognition-marginals}\\
p_A^b&=(\operatorname{pr}_b)_\#\mathbb P_A^Z(\cdot\mid X),
&p_A^m&=(\operatorname{pr}_m)_\#\mathbb P_A^Z(\cdot\mid X),
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-prior-marginals}\\
\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}^b&=(\operatorname{pr}_b)_\#\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X},
&\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}^m&=(\operatorname{pr}_m)_\#\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}.
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-posterior-marginals}
\end{align}
Under the richness hypothesis of \Cref{prop:prob-marginals-do-not-determine-joint} --- that the
relevant parent coordinate space carry at least two nondegenerate coordinates --- no displayed
marginal pair reconstructs the corresponding full law. The hypothesis is necessary and not
decorative: if $\mathsf Z_A$ degenerates to a single point, its unique law is trivially determined by
its marginals, and the released finite witnesses exhibit nonreconstruction only in the nondegenerate
case.

Apply the same normalized Markov-kernel channel $C_A$ to the fine recognition and posterior laws and
disintegrate both lifts in
$Y$ conditional on $z$. The additive identity in $[0,+\infty]$ is
\begin{equation}
\KL(\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}\Vert\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X})
=\KL(\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}\Vert\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X})+\Delta_A(o,X),
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-kl-chain}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\Delta_A(o,X)=\int_{\mathsf Z_A}
\KL\left(
\widehat{\mathbb Q}_{I,o,X}(dY\given z)
\middle\Vert
\widehat{\boldsymbol\Pi}_{I,o,X}(dY\given z)
\right)\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}(dz)\geq0.
\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-defect}
\end{equation}
Adding the same finite real $-\log p_X(o)$ to both KL terms gives the extended-real identity
$\Fenergy_I=\Fenergy_A+\Delta_A$; when finite, a VFE may be negative. Without a finite-fine-KL
premise, $\Delta_A=0$ exactly when the two discarded conditional laws in
\eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-defect} agree $\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}$-almost surely. Finite fine KL is
required for the ordinary subtraction $\Fenergy_I-\Fenergy_A=\Delta_A$ and for the stated two-way
pairwise common-recovery equivalence. Family-wide common recovery requires simultaneous hypotheses
for every family member. A bare equality $+\infty=+\infty$ supplies neither the zero-defect
criterion nor a recovery consequence. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Normalization and the observation marginal.  Since $C_A(Y,\mathsf Z_A)=1$ for every
$Y\in\mathsf Y_I$, integrating \eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-generative} against the constant
function gives $\mathbb P_A(\mathsf O\times\mathsf Z_A\mid X)=1$, and the same computation applied
to $\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}$ and $\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}$ normalizes the other two laws in
\eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-triple}.  Testing
\eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-generative} with a bounded measurable function of $o$ alone gives
$\mathbb P_A^{\mathsf O}=\mathbb P_I^{\mathsf O}$, because $C_A$ leaves the observation coordinate
unchanged.

Posterior version.  Let $f$ be bounded measurable on $\mathsf Z_A$ and $g$ bounded measurable on
$\mathsf O$, and put $h(Y)=\int f(z)C_A(Y,dz)$, which is bounded and measurable because $C_A$ is a
kernel.  Then
\[
\int g\Bigl(\int f\,d\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}\Bigr)\mathbb P_A^{\mathsf O}(do)
=\int g\Bigl(\int h\,d\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}\Bigr)\mathbb P_I^{\mathsf O}(do)
=\int g(o)h(Y)\,\mathbb P_I(do,dY\mid X)
=\int g(o)f(z)\,\mathbb P_A(do,dz\mid X),
\]
using the preserved observation marginal, then the defining property of the selected fine version,
then \eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-generative}.  Hence $o\mapsto\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}$ is a selected
parent posterior version, including at its declared exceptional points.  The step uses that $C_A$
acts on the conditioned variable only; a channel that also coarsened $\mathsf O$ would not descend.

Absolute continuity.  If $\boldsymbol\Pi_{A,o,X}(N)=0$ then
$\int C_A(Y,N)\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}(dY)=0$, so $C_A(\cdot,N)=0$ holds
$\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}$-almost surely, hence $\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}$-almost surely by
$\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}\ll\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}$, and therefore
$\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}(N)=\int C_A(Y,N)\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}(dY)=0$.

Evaluator.  On standard-Borel spaces a selected disintegration of $\mathbb P_A(\cdot\mid X)$ over
$(M_A,\Xi_A)$ exists and is jointly measurable, which supplies the induced tier of
\eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-evaluator} together with
\eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-evaluator-compatibility} by construction.  For a family declared
independently of $\mathbb P_A$, \eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-evaluator-compatibility} is the stated
separate hypothesis; disintegration alone does not supply it.

Marginals.  Equations \eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-recognition-marginals} and the accompanying
prior and posterior displays are coordinate pushforwards by definition, obtained by testing each
parent law with functions of a single coordinate.

Chain identity and defect.  Form the joint lifts of
\eqref{eq:rg-bridge-lifts} with $C=C_A$, $Q_o=\mathbb Q_{I,o,X}$ and
$\Pi_o=\boldsymbol\Pi_{I,o,X}$.  Applying \Cref{thm:rg-exact-coarse-vfe} verbatim yields
\eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-defect} and the extended-real additive identity, every term lying in
$[0,+\infty]$ with no infinite quantity subtracted.  Adding the common finite real $-\log p_X(o)$ to
both KL terms gives $\Fenergy_I=\Fenergy_A+\Delta_A$ in the extended reals; the sum of a finite real
and a nonnegative extended real is no longer nonnegative, which is why a finite VFE may be negative.

Zero defect.  $\Delta_A$ is the integral of a nonnegative measurable integrand against
$\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}$, so it vanishes exactly when that integrand vanishes
$\mathbb Q_{A,o,X}$-almost surely; the integrand is a relative entropy, which vanishes exactly when
its two arguments agree.  No finiteness hypothesis enters here.

Finite tier.  If the fine KL is finite then both right-hand terms are finite, so the subtraction
$\Fenergy_I-\Fenergy_A=\Delta_A$ is an ordinary difference of reals.  On that tier the selected
posterior reverse kernel recovers both declared laws under $\Delta_A=0$; conversely, one normalized
kernel $R$ recovering both laws sandwiches the fine and coarse KL values by data processing through
$C_A$ and then through $R$, forcing equality and hence $\Delta_A=0$.  This is the equality case of
data processing, i.e.\ sufficiency of $C_A$ for the pair, and it is pairwise: a single kernel serving
an entire family requires the hypothesis for every member simultaneously.  If both KL values are
$+\infty$ the equality is vacuous and yields neither conclusion.  $\square$

This theorem constructs a full normalized, generally lossy pointwise probabilistic datum. The coarse
channel and child membership are declared inputs, not canonical selections. The theorem supplies no

\section{The exact effective likelihood and action}
\label{sec:rg-effective-action}

Fix one adjacent scale arrow and suppress its index temporarily.  Its fine
space is a finite product of standard-Borel agent spaces, so it is itself
standard Borel.  Let $\rho=P_0$ be a probability reference and let
\begin{equation}
m_o(dy)=L_o(y)\rho(dy),
\qquad
L_o(y)=\exp[-H_o(y)],
\qquad
0<M_o:=m_o(\mathsf Y)<\infty,
\qquad
\pi(dy)=M_o^{-1}m_o(dy)
\label{eq:rg-success-submeasure}
\end{equation}
be the unnormalized evidence submeasure, its normalized law, and its
strictly positive finite mass.  The action may be extended valued; the
displayed density convention is $e^{-\infty}=0$ and all equalities involving
actions are therefore almost-everywhere statements.  Push both measures
through the normalized, recognition-independent channel $C$:
\begin{equation}
\rho^c=\rho C,
\qquad m_o^c=m_oC,
\qquad
\pi^c=\pi C=M_o^{-1}m_o^c.
\label{eq:rg-reference-evidence-pair}
\end{equation}
Absolute continuity $m_o^c\ll\rho^c$ follows from $m_o\ll\rho$, and the
channel preserves the evidence mass.  Define
\begin{equation}
L_o^c(z)=\frac{dm_o^c}{d\rho^c}(z),
\qquad
H_o^c(z)=-\log L_o^c(z).
\label{eq:rg-effective-likelihood-action}
\end{equation}

\theoremheading{Conditional-partition formula and composition}{thm:rg-effective-action}
Let $R_\rho(z,dy)$ be a reverse conditional of $Y$ given $Z=z$ under
$\rho(dy)C(y,dz)$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\exp[-H_o^c(z)]
=\int\exp[-H_o(y)]R_\rho(z,dy)
\label{eq:rg-conditional-partition}
\end{equation}
$\rho^c$-almost everywhere, and
\begin{equation}
Z(o)=\int\exp[-H_o^c(z)]\rho^c(dz).
\label{eq:rg-effective-evidence}
\end{equation}
For a second coarse channel $D$, the direct and staged effective likelihoods
agree almost everywhere when their conditional versions are chosen from the
same joint tower.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For bounded measurable $f$,
\[
\int f(z)m_o^c(dz)
=\iint f(z)L_o(y)C(y,dz)\rho(dy)
=\int f(z)\E_\rho[L_o(Y)\given Z=z]\rho^c(dz).
\]
This identifies the Radon--Nikodym derivative and proves
\eqref{eq:rg-conditional-partition}.  Setting $f=1$ proves
\eqref{eq:rg-effective-evidence}.  The tower property proves composition.
$\square$

For binary interaction successes, $0\leq L_o\leq1$ implies
$0\leq L_o^c\leq1$, so one effective binary interaction record realizes the
entire coarse action.  For general record densities, $L_o^c$ remains a valid
density relative to the pushed probability reference, although it need not be
bounded by one.  An infinite Lebesgue reference cannot be pushed through an
arbitrary projection without checking sigma-finiteness; this is why the
probability pair $(\rho,m_o)$ is primary.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{Local bounded action chart and its derivatives}
\label{sec:rg-local-action-calculus}

The unnormalized pair tracks the evidence mass, while the local action
calculus is based at its normalized law.  Because the fine and coarse spaces
are standard Borel, fix a jointly measurable reverse kernel
\(\Pi(z,dy)\) for the normalized joint law:
\begin{equation}
\pi(dy)C(y,dz)=\pi^c(dz)\Pi(z,dy).
\label{eq:rg-normalized-reverse-kernel}
\end{equation}
Values of \(\Pi\) on a \(\pi^c\)-null set are not used.  Write
\(\mathfrak B=L^\infty(\pi;\mathbb R)\) and choose
\(0<\epsilon<\log 2\).  The local chart is the open ball
\begin{equation}
\mathcal U_\epsilon=
\{\varphi\in\mathfrak B:\Vert\varphi\Vert_\infty<\epsilon\}.
\label{eq:rg-bounded-action-chart}
\end{equation}
For every \(\varphi\in\mathfrak B\), a perturbation acts on the complete
measure pair by \(m_o^\varphi=e^{-\varphi}m_o\).  Its exact coarse action
increment is
\begin{equation}
Q(\varphi)(z)
=-\log\frac{d\bigl((e^{-\varphi}m_o)C\bigr)}{dm_oC}(z)
=-\log\int e^{-\varphi(y)}\Pi(z,dy).
\label{eq:rg-local-action-map}
\end{equation}
The last expression is finite and belongs to
\(L^\infty(\pi^c;\mathbb R)\): it lies between
\(-\Vert\varphi\Vert_\infty\) and
\(\Vert\varphi\Vert_\infty\).  Thus the Radon--Nikodym formula defines
\(Q:\mathfrak B\to L^\infty(\pi^c;\mathbb R)\) on the entire bounded
space.  The real-analytic theorem below is asserted on
\(\mathcal U_\epsilon\), not on a general \(L^p\) neighborhood.  Where both
actions are finite, it equals the literal
difference \(\mathcal R^H[H_o+\varphi;\rho]-
\mathcal R^H[H_o;\rho]\).  For an extended-valued base action, the
Radon--Nikodym ratio in \eqref{eq:rg-local-action-map} is the definition and
no expression of the form \(+\infty-(+\infty)\) is used. \status{DEFINITION}

\theoremheading{Exact bounded action calculus}{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus}
The map
\begin{equation}
Q:\mathcal U_\epsilon\longrightarrow
L^\infty(\pi^c;\mathbb R)
\label{eq:rg-local-action-map-typed}
\end{equation}
is real analytic.  If
\begin{equation}
(U\varphi)(z)=\int\varphi(y)\Pi(z,dy),
\label{eq:rg-reverse-conditional-expectation}
\end{equation}
then its first two Fr\'echet derivatives at the origin are the bounded typed
maps
\begin{align}
DQ(0)[\varphi]&=U\varphi,
\label{eq:rg-action-first-frechet}\\
D^2Q(0)[\varphi,\psi]
&=-\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi(z,\cdot)}(\varphi,\psi).
\label{eq:rg-action-second-frechet}
\end{align}
More generally, with
\begin{equation}
\Pi^\varphi(z,dy)=
\frac{e^{-\varphi(y)}\Pi(z,dy)}
{\int e^{-\varphi(u)}\Pi(z,du)},
\label{eq:rg-tilted-reverse-kernel}
\end{equation}
the derivatives at \(\varphi\in\mathcal U_\epsilon\) are
\begin{align}
DQ(\varphi)[h](z)&=\int h(y)\Pi^\varphi(z,dy),
\label{eq:rg-action-first-frechet-general}\\
D^2Q(\varphi)[h,k](z)&=
-\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi^\varphi(z,\cdot)}(h,k).
\label{eq:rg-action-second-frechet-general}
\end{align}
All covariance expressions are in
\(L^\infty(\pi^c;\mathbb R)\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For \(\Vert\varphi\Vert_\infty<\epsilon\), the exponential power series
converges in \(L^\infty(\pi)\), and positivity plus unitality of \(U\)
give
\begin{equation}
\Vert U(e^{-\varphi})-1\Vert_\infty
\leq e^\epsilon-1<1.
\label{eq:rg-action-log-series-radius}
\end{equation}
The Banach-algebra logarithm series therefore converges in
\(L^\infty(\pi^c)\), proving analyticity of
\(Q=-\log U(e^{-\varphi})\).  Differentiating the convergent series, or
using the ordinary Fr\'echet chain rule for the exponential, bounded linear
operator \(U\), and logarithm, gives
\eqref{eq:rg-action-first-frechet-general}.  Differentiating that normalized
integral in direction \(k\) gives
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}
\int h d\Pi^{\varphi+tk}
=-\int hk d\Pi^\varphi
+\left(\int h d\Pi^\varphi\right)
\left(\int k d\Pi^\varphi\right),
\label{eq:rg-action-covariance-derivation}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:rg-action-second-frechet-general}.  Setting
\(\varphi=0\) proves \eqref{eq:rg-action-first-frechet} and
\eqref{eq:rg-action-second-frechet}.  The uniform lower bound
\(U(e^{-\varphi})\geq e^{-\epsilon}\) and boundedness of \(h,k\) make
every displayed derivative a bounded map into the stated target.  \(\square\)

\propositionheading{Bounded recentering gives analyticity at every bounded action}{prop:rg-action-bounded-recentering}
For every \(\varphi\in\mathfrak B\), define the positive unital operator
\begin{equation}
 U^\varphi h
 :=\frac{U(e^{-\varphi}h)}{U(e^{-\varphi})}.
 \label{eq:rg-action-recentered-operator}
\end{equation}
The denominator lies between
\(e^{-\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty}\) and
\(e^{\lVert\varphi\rVert_\infty}\), so this is a bounded
\(L^\infty(\pi)\to L^\infty(\pi^c)\) operator.  For every bounded increment
\(h\),
\begin{equation}
 Q(\varphi+h)-Q(\varphi)
 =-\log U^\varphi(e^{-h}).
 \label{eq:rg-action-recentering-identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for each \(0<\epsilon<\log2\), the left side is real analytic
as a function of \(h\) on
\(\lVert h\rVert_\infty<\epsilon\), and
\begin{equation}
 DQ(\varphi)[h]=U^\varphi h.
 \label{eq:rg-action-everywhere-bounded-derivative}
\end{equation}
Thus \(Q\) is locally Fr\'echet analytic at every bounded action, even though
\eqref{eq:rg-bounded-action-chart} was the one origin-centered chart used in
\Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The bounds follow from positivity and unitality of \(U\).  Substitution in
\eqref{eq:rg-local-action-map} gives
\[
 -\log U(e^{-\varphi-h})+\log U(e^{-\varphi})
 =-\log\frac{U(e^{-\varphi}e^{-h})}{U(e^{-\varphi})},
\]
which is \eqref{eq:rg-action-recentering-identity}.  Positivity and
unitality of \(U^\varphi\) imply
\(\lVert U^\varphi(e^{-h})-1\rVert_\infty
\leq e^\epsilon-1<1\).  The same Banach-algebra exponential and logarithm
series used in \Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus} therefore converge on
this increment ball.  Their first derivative at \(h=0\) is
\(U^\varphi h\). \(\square\)

For \(\varphi,\psi\in\mathfrak B\), the nonlinear map is order preserving
and additively homogeneous.  Indeed,
\(\varphi\leq\psi+c\) implies
\(e^{-\varphi}\geq e^{-c}e^{-\psi}\), and positivity of \(U\) gives
\(Q(\varphi)\leq Q(\psi)+c\).  Applying this implication in both
directions with \(c=\Vert\varphi-\psi\Vert_\infty\) proves the exact
bounded-action estimate
\begin{equation}
\Vert Q(\varphi)-Q(\psi)\Vert_\infty
\leq\Vert\varphi-\psi\Vert_\infty.
\label{eq:rg-nonlinear-action-sup-contraction}
\end{equation}
Under isometric cross-scale identifications, this prevents relevance from
arising in the bounded measure-pair action sector.  It does not constrain an
extensive interaction lift or a separately proved weighted action space.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The full action space remembers the perturbed mass
\(m_o^\varphi(\mathsf Y)\).  If an application deliberately discards that
scalar, it instead uses the projective action space
\begin{equation}
\overline{\mathfrak B}=\mathfrak B/\mathbb R1.
\label{eq:rg-projective-action-space}
\end{equation}
Since \(Q(\varphi+c)=Q(\varphi)+c\) and \(U1=1\), both \(Q\) and \(U\)
descend to the quotient.  The quotient is not used to erase an evidence
constant in the normalized measure-pair theory. \status{ESTABLISHED}

For comparison, normalize the perturbed fine law itself:
\begin{equation}
\widehat\pi^\varphi
=\frac{e^{-\varphi}\pi}{\pi(e^{-\varphi})},
\qquad
\widehat Q(\varphi)
:=-\log\frac{d(\widehat\pi^\varphi C)}{d\pi^c}
=Q(\varphi)+\log\pi(e^{-\varphi}).
\label{eq:rg-normalized-perturbation-action}
\end{equation}
The two action increments therefore agree only modulo a constant.  Their
derivatives make that distinction explicit:
\begin{align}
D\widehat Q(0)[h]&=Uh-\pi(h),
\label{eq:rg-normalized-action-first-derivative}\\
D^2\widehat Q(0)[h,k]&=
-\operatorname{Cov}_{\Pi(z,\cdot)}(h,k)
+\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi}(h,k).
\label{eq:rg-normalized-action-second-derivative}
\end{align}
The score of the normalized path \(t\mapsto\widehat\pi^{th}\) is
\(-h+\pi(h)\), and its coarse score is
\(-Uh+\pi(h)\).  Consequently the defect in
\eqref{eq:rg-action-l2-conditional-defect}, applied to the centered score,
is exactly the Fisher-information loss under the fixed Markov channel.  This
score statement remains an \(L^2\) tangent result; it does not enlarge the
nonlinear bounded action chart. \status{ESTABLISHED}

At a boundary where an action representative is infinite, or outside the
bounded action space, neither a literal action difference nor a differential
beta functional is supplied by this calculus.  Within the bounded space,
\Cref{prop:rg-action-bounded-recentering} supplies local analyticity at every
center.  At an extended-valued boundary the primary object remains the
measure pair and its Radon--Nikodym action; an action beta requires the
separately declared finite-valued vector space and subtraction hypotheses of
\Cref{sec:rg-beta-function}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{Canonical contraction and the bounded Dobrushin certificate}
\label{sec:rg-action-contraction}

\theoremheading{Conditional-expectation contraction and exact defect}{thm:rg-action-lp-contraction}
For every \(1\leq p\leq\infty\), the operator in
\eqref{eq:rg-reverse-conditional-expectation} extends uniquely to a
contraction
\begin{equation}
U:L^p(\pi;\mathbb R)\longrightarrow L^p(\pi^c;\mathbb R),
\qquad
\Vert U\varphi\Vert_{L^p(\pi^c)}
\leq\Vert\varphi\Vert_{L^p(\pi)}.
\label{eq:rg-action-lp-contraction}
\end{equation}
For every \(1\leq p\leq\infty\), it preserves the mean and hence maps centered tangent
spaces to centered tangent spaces.  At \(p=2\), the loss is exactly
\begin{equation}
\Vert\varphi\Vert_{L^2(\pi)}^2
-\Vert U\varphi\Vert_{L^2(\pi^c)}^2
=\int\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi(z,\cdot)}(\varphi)\pi^c(dz)
\geq0.
\label{eq:rg-action-l2-conditional-defect}
\end{equation}
Equality holds exactly when there is a measurable \(g\) on the coarse space
such that \(\varphi(y)=g(z)\) for
\(\pi(dy)C(y,dz)\)-almost every \((y,z)\).  For a deterministic coarse map
\(z=c(y)\), this is precisely \(\varphi=g\circ c\) almost everywhere
under \(\pi\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Conditional Jensen gives
\(\lvert U\varphi\rvert^p\leq U\lvert\varphi\rvert^p\) for finite
\(p\); integrating the disintegration
\eqref{eq:rg-normalized-reverse-kernel} proves
\eqref{eq:rg-action-lp-contraction}.  The \(p=\infty\) case follows from
the pointwise bound by the essential supremum.  The same disintegration gives
\(\int U\varphi d\pi^c=\int\varphi d\pi\).  Finally, the conditional
variance identity
\begin{equation}
\int\varphi^2d\pi
=\int(U\varphi)^2d\pi^c
+\int\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi(z,\cdot)}(\varphi)\pi^c(dz)
\label{eq:rg-action-total-conditional-variance}
\end{equation}
proves \eqref{eq:rg-action-l2-conditional-defect}.  A nonnegative
conditional variance integrates to zero exactly when it is zero
\(\pi^c\)-almost everywhere, which is equivalent to the stated joint
measurability condition.  \(\square\)

On \(L^\infty(\pi)/\mathbb R1\), use
\begin{equation}
\Vert[\varphi]\Vert_{\mathrm{osc}}
=\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\Vert\varphi-c\Vert_\infty
=\frac12\left(
\operatorname*{ess sup}\varphi-
\operatorname*{ess inf}\varphi\right).
\label{eq:rg-action-oscillation-norm}
\end{equation}
For the fixed reverse-kernel version, define its Dobrushin coefficient by
\begin{equation}
\delta=
\operatorname*{ess sup}_{(z,z')\sim(\pi^c)^{\otimes2}}
\sup_{A\in\mathscr Y}
\left|\Pi(z,A)-\Pi(z',A)\right|.
\label{eq:rg-action-dobrushin-coefficient}
\end{equation}

\propositionheading{Dobrushin contraction is a sufficient cocycle certificate}{prop:rg-dobrushin-cocycle}
The quotient map satisfies
\begin{equation}
\Vert\overline U\Vert_{\mathrm{osc}\to\mathrm{osc}}
\leq\delta.
\label{eq:rg-action-dobrushin-contraction}
\end{equation}
For a nonautonomous sequence of such arrows with block factors
\(b_k>1\), set
\begin{equation}
B_{n\leftarrow\ell}=\prod_{k=\ell}^{n-1}b_k,
\qquad
\overline U_{n\leftarrow\ell}
=\overline U_{n-1}\cdots\overline U_\ell.
\label{eq:rg-action-cocycle-scale}
\end{equation}
If \(B_{n\leftarrow\ell}\to\infty\) and
\begin{equation}
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\sum_{k=\ell}^{n-1}\log\delta_k}
{\log B_{n\leftarrow\ell}}<0,
\qquad \log0=-\infty,
\label{eq:rg-action-dobrushin-irrelevance}
\end{equation}
then every bounded projective perturbation has strictly negative upper growth
rate in this oscillation norm.  With a fixed isometric cross-scale
identification, fixed \(b>1\), and \(\delta<1\), this gives the autonomous
bound \(\log\delta/\log b<0\).  The condition is sufficient only: failure
of \eqref{eq:rg-action-dobrushin-irrelevance} proves neither marginality nor
relevance. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For probability measures \(\mu,\nu\),
\(\left|\int f d\mu-\int f d\nu\right|\) is at most
\(\operatorname{osc}(f)\sup_A|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|\).  Apply this to the two
reverse laws in \eqref{eq:rg-action-dobrushin-coefficient}, divide by two,
and take the essential supremum to obtain
\eqref{eq:rg-action-dobrushin-contraction}.  Iteration gives
\begin{equation}
\Vert\overline U_{n\leftarrow\ell}\Vert_{\mathrm{osc}\to\mathrm{osc}}
\leq\prod_{k=\ell}^{n-1}\delta_k.
\label{eq:rg-action-dobrushin-product-bound}
\end{equation}
Taking logarithms with the stated zero convention proves the claimed upper
growth bound.  Two controls show why neither one-step behavior nor failure
of the displayed certificate can be promoted to a necessary condition.  For
the first, take reverse matrices
\begin{equation}
R_0=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0\\0&1\\1/2&1/2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
R_1=
\begin{pmatrix}
1/2&1/2&0\\0&0&1
\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:rg-dobrushin-nonnecessity-witness}
\end{equation}
with compatible laws
\(\pi_0=(1/2,1/2)\),
\(\pi_1=(1/3,1/3,1/3)\), and
\(\pi_2=(2/3,1/3)\).  Both one-step coefficients are one, but the two rows
of \(R_1R_0\) are both \((1/2,1/2)\), so the composed coefficient is zero.
Thus individual strict Dobrushin contraction is not necessary for complete
two-step forgetting.  For the opposite control, take
\(\pi_k=(1/2,1/2)\) at every scale and let \(J\) have both rows
\((1/2,1/2)\).  Each matrix below is then a reverse kernel compatible with
these laws:
\begin{equation}
R_k=(1-a_k)I+a_kJ,
\qquad a_k=2^{-k-2}.
\label{eq:rg-dobrushin-one-step-insufficient-witness}
\end{equation}
Then \(\delta_k=1-a_k<1\) at every step, but
\(\prod_k\delta_k>0\).  With fixed \(b_k=b>1\), the normalized accumulated
logarithmic rate can therefore be zero.  One-step strict contraction is not
sufficient for the nonautonomous conclusion.  The identity channel has
\(\delta=1\) and leaves every bounded mode marginal, while a two-cycle has
an eigenvalue \(-1\); these controls also prevent a phase-blind conclusion.
\(\square\)

\subsection{Score tangents, the extensive block lift, and an inhabited spectrum}
\label{sec:rg-score-lift}

Estimate \eqref{eq:rg-nonlinear-action-sup-contraction} shows that the bounded
measure-pair action sector is nonexpansive, so under isometric cross-scale
identifications no bounded action mode in that sector can grow.  A growing mode
must therefore come from somewhere else, and this subsection identifies the
first of the two places where it does: the score tangent carries an
\emph{extensive} replication factor that the one-arrow contraction theorem
cannot see.  The second place is the extensive interaction assembly of
\Cref{sec:rg-finite-network-interactions}.

\definitionheading{Quadratic-mean differentiability and score}{def:rg-dqm-score}
Let \((\mathsf W,\mathscr W,\mu)\) be a probability space and write
\begin{equation}
 L^2_0(\mu)
 =\left\{h\in L^2(\mu):\int h\,d\mu=0\right\}.
 \label{eq:rg-centered-tangent}
\end{equation}
A family \((\mu_t)_{\lvert t\rvert<t_0}\) of probability laws with
\(\mu_t\ll\mu\) and \(\mu_0=\mu\) is differentiable in quadratic mean at
\(t=0\) with score \(h\in L^2(\mu)\) when
\begin{equation}
 \left\lVert
 \sqrt{\frac{d\mu_t}{d\mu}}-1-\frac t2h
 \right\rVert_{L^2(\mu)}
 =o(\lvert t\rvert),
 \qquad t\to0,
 \label{eq:rg-dqm-definition}
\end{equation}
where both one-sided limits are required.  The Fisher information of the path
at \(t=0\) is \(\lVert h\rVert_{L^2(\mu)}^2\).  Nothing is proved by this
declaration. \status{DEFINITION}

Centering is forced rather than assumed.  Writing \(p_t=d\mu_t/d\mu\) and using
\(\int p_t\,d\mu=1\) gives the exact identity
\(\int(\sqrt{p_t}-1)\,d\mu=-\tfrac12\int(\sqrt{p_t}-1)^2\,d\mu\).  Inserting
\eqref{eq:rg-dqm-definition} makes the left side
\(\tfrac t2\int h\,d\mu+o(\lvert t\rvert)\) and the right side
\(-\tfrac{t^2}8\lVert h\rVert_2^2+o(t^2)\), so \(\int h\,d\mu=0\).  The
quadratic-mean tangent set is therefore contained in \(L^2_0(\mu)\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\lemmaheading{Every centered square-integrable score is realized two-sided}{lem:rg-dqm-realization}
Let \(h\in L^2_0(\mu)\) and put \(a=\lVert h\rVert_2^2/4\).  For every real
\(t\), define
\begin{equation}
 p_t=\frac{(1+th/2)^2}{1+at^2},
 \qquad
 \mu_t=p_t\mu.
 \label{eq:rg-quadratic-score-path}
\end{equation}
Then every \(\mu_t\) is a probability law, \(\mu_0=\mu\), and the path is
differentiable in quadratic mean at \(t=0\) with score \(h\), for
\(t\to0^+\) and for \(t\to0^-\).  No exponential moment, and no boundedness of
\(h\), is used. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Centering gives
\(\int(1+th/2)^2\,d\mu=1+t\mu(h)+\tfrac{t^2}4\mu(h^2)=1+at^2\), so \(p_t\geq0\)
integrates to one.  Put \(s_t=1+th/2\) and \(N_t=1+at^2\geq1\), so that
\(\sqrt{p_t}=\lvert s_t\rvert/\sqrt{N_t}\) and
\begin{equation}
 \sqrt{p_t}-s_t
 =\frac{\lvert s_t\rvert-s_t}{\sqrt{N_t}}
 +s_t\left(\frac1{\sqrt{N_t}}-1\right).
 \label{eq:rg-quadratic-path-split}
\end{equation}
On \(\{s_t<0\}\) one has \(th/2<-1\), hence \(\lvert h\rvert>2/\lvert t\rvert\)
and \(\lvert s_t\rvert=\lvert th/2\rvert-1\leq\lvert th\rvert/2\), so
\(\lvert s_t\rvert-s_t=2\lvert s_t\rvert\leq\lvert th\rvert\) there and the
difference vanishes elsewhere.  Since \(N_t\geq1\),
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{t^2}\left\lVert
 \frac{\lvert s_t\rvert-s_t}{\sqrt{N_t}}
 \right\rVert_2^2
 \leq\int h^2\mathbf1_{\{\lvert h\rvert>2/\lvert t\rvert\}}\,d\mu
 \longrightarrow0
 \label{eq:rg-quadratic-path-tail}
\end{equation}
by dominated convergence, because \(h^2\) is integrable.  For the second term,
\(\lVert s_t\rVert_2=\sqrt{N_t}\), so its norm is exactly
\(\lvert1-\sqrt{N_t}\rvert=O(t^2)\).  Adding the two bounds and using
\(s_t=1+\tfrac t2h\) gives \eqref{eq:rg-dqm-definition}. \(\square\)

\propositionheading{Independent block replication of a differentiable path}{prop:rg-score-block-lift}
Fix an integer \(b\geq1\).  Let \((\mu_t)\) be any path satisfying
\eqref{eq:rg-dqm-definition} with score \(h\), and form the product path
\(\mu_t^{\otimes b}\) at \(\mu^{\otimes b}\).  Then the product path is
differentiable in quadratic mean at \(t=0\) with score
\begin{equation}
 (\mathscr I_bh)(x_1,\dots,x_b)=\sum_{i=1}^bh(x_i),
 \label{eq:rg-score-replication}
\end{equation}
and the replication map is a bounded linear injection
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr I_b:L^2_0(\mu)\longrightarrow L^2_0(\mu^{\otimes b}),
 \qquad
 \lVert\mathscr I_bh\rVert_{L^2(\mu^{\otimes b})}^2
 =b\lVert h\rVert_{L^2(\mu)}^2,
 \label{eq:rg-score-replication-norm}
\end{equation}
so \(\lVert\mathscr I_b\rVert=\sqrt b\) and \(b^{-1/2}\mathscr I_b\) is an
isometry onto its range.  The symbol \(\mathscr I_b\) is the score replication
lift; it is not the Hoeffding assembly \(E_\ell\) of
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-assembly-extraction}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Write \(r_t=\sqrt{d\mu_t/d\mu}=1+\tfrac t2h+t\varepsilon_t\) with
\(\lVert\varepsilon_t\rVert_{L^2(\mu)}\to0\), which is
\eqref{eq:rg-dqm-definition}.  The product amplitude is
\(\prod_{i=1}^br_t(x_i)=\prod_{i=1}^b(1+u_i)\) with
\(u_i(x)=\tfrac t2h(x_i)+t\varepsilon_t(x_i)\).  Expanding the finite product,
\begin{equation}
 \prod_{i=1}^b(1+u_i)
 =1+\sum_{i=1}^bu_i
 +\sum_{\lvert S\rvert\geq2}\prod_{i\in S}u_i .
 \label{eq:rg-product-amplitude-expansion}
\end{equation}
Because the coordinates are independent and each factor depends on one
coordinate, \(L^2(\mu^{\otimes b})\) norms of products factor, so
\(\lVert\prod_{i\in S}u_i\rVert_2=\prod_{i\in S}\lVert u_i\rVert_2
=O(\lvert t\rvert^{\lvert S\rvert})\), and every term with
\(\lvert S\rvert\geq2\) is \(O(t^2)\).  The linear part is
\(\tfrac t2\mathscr I_bh+t\sum_i\varepsilon_t(x_i)\), whose remainder has norm at
most \(b\lvert t\rvert\lVert\varepsilon_t\rVert_2=o(\lvert t\rvert)\) by the
triangle inequality.  This proves quadratic-mean differentiability with score
\(\mathscr I_bh\).  Independence and centering give
\(\E(\mathscr I_bh)^2=\sum_i\E h(X_i)^2+\sum_{i\neq j}\E h(X_i)\E h(X_j)
=b\lVert h\rVert_2^2\), which is
\eqref{eq:rg-score-replication-norm}. \(\square\)

The replication is extensive.  Replacing \eqref{eq:rg-score-replication} by the
arithmetic mean without simultaneously rescaling the path parameter would change
the Fisher information from \(b\lVert h\rVert_2^2\) to
\(\lVert h\rVert_2^2/b\), which is a different theory.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Pushed score and the exact extensive Fisher defect}{thm:rg-score-pushforward-defect}
Let \(C_b:\mathsf W^b\rightsquigarrow\mathsf Z\) be a normalized,
parameter-independent Markov kernel between standard-Borel spaces, put
\(\mu^{(b)}=\mu^{\otimes b}\),
\(J_b(dx,dz)=\mu^{(b)}(dx)C_b(x,dz)\), and
\(\mu^{(b),c}=\mu^{(b)}C_b\).  Let
\(U_b:L^2(\mu^{(b)})\to L^2(\mu^{(b),c})\) be the reverse conditional
expectation of \Cref{thm:rg-action-lp-contraction} for this arrow.  Then the
pushed path \(\mu_t^{\otimes b}C_b\) is differentiable in quadratic mean at
\(\mu^{(b),c}\) with score
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr L_bh:=U_b\mathscr I_bh
 =\E_{J_b}\left[\sum_{i=1}^bh(X_i)\,\middle|\,Z\right],
 \label{eq:rg-score-renormalization-operator}
\end{equation}
so that \(\mathscr L_b:L^2_0(\mu)\to L^2_0(\mu^{(b),c})\) is linear with
\(\lVert\mathscr L_b\rVert\leq\sqrt b\).  Moreover the exact information budget
is
\begin{equation}
 b\lVert h\rVert_{L^2(\mu)}^2
 -\lVert\mathscr L_bh\rVert_{L^2(\mu^{(b),c})}^2
 =\int\operatorname{Var}_{J_b}\left(\mathscr I_bh\,\middle|\,Z=z\right)
 \mu^{(b),c}(dz)
 \geq0,
 \label{eq:rg-extensive-fisher-defect}
\end{equation}
with equality exactly when \(\mathscr I_bh\) agrees \(J_b\)-almost surely with a
measurable function of \(Z\).  Equivalently
\(b^{-1/2}\mathscr L_b\) is a contraction of \(L^2_0\) norms.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Suppose first that \(h\) is bounded and use the path
\eqref{eq:rg-quadratic-score-path}.  For \(\lvert t\rvert\) small,
\(s_t\geq1-\lvert t\rvert\lVert h\rVert_\infty/2>0\), so
\(p_t=s_t^2/N_t=1+th+O(t^2)\) uniformly, and the product density is
\(\prod_ip_t(x_i)=1+t\mathscr I_bh+O(t^2)\) uniformly.  Conditional expectation
of a uniformly \(O(t^2)\) remainder is uniformly \(O(t^2)\), and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{d(\mu_t^{\otimes b}C_b)}{d\mu^{(b),c}}(z)
 =\E_{J_b}\left[
 \frac{d\mu_t^{\otimes b}}{d\mu^{(b)}}(X)\,\middle|\,Z=z\right]
 =1+t\mathscr L_bh(z)+O(t^2)
 \label{eq:rg-pushed-density-expansion}
\end{equation}
uniformly, exactly as in \eqref{eq:rg-conditional-partition}.  Since
\(\sqrt{1+w}=1+w/2+O(w^2)\) uniformly for \(\lvert w\rvert\leq1/2\), the square
root of \eqref{eq:rg-pushed-density-expansion} is
\(1+\tfrac t2\mathscr L_bh+O(t^2)\) uniformly, hence in
\(L^2(\mu^{(b),c})\).  This is \eqref{eq:rg-dqm-definition} for the pushed path.

For general \(h\in L^2_0(\mu)\), choose bounded centered \(h_n\to h\) in
\(L^2(\mu)\) and let \(\mu_t^{(n)}\) be their paths
\eqref{eq:rg-quadratic-score-path}.  Squared Hellinger distance is an
\(f\)-divergence with the convex \(f(u)=(\sqrt u-1)^2\), so conditional Jensen
applied to the disintegration of the pushed joint law gives the data-processing
bound
\begin{equation}
 \left\lVert
 \sqrt{\frac{d(\mu_t^{\otimes b}C_b)}{d\mu^{(b),c}}}
 -\sqrt{\frac{d((\mu_t^{(n)})^{\otimes b}C_b)}{d\mu^{(b),c}}}
 \right\rVert_{2}
 \leq
 \left\lVert
 \sqrt{\frac{d\mu_t^{\otimes b}}{d\mu^{(b)}}}
 -\sqrt{\frac{d(\mu_t^{(n)})^{\otimes b}}{d\mu^{(b)}}}
 \right\rVert_{2}.
 \label{eq:rg-hellinger-contraction}
\end{equation}
Both input paths are differentiable in quadratic mean with scores
\(\mathscr I_bh\) and \(\mathscr I_bh_n\), so the right side divided by
\(\lvert t\rvert\) has limit superior \(\tfrac12\lVert\mathscr I_b(h-h_n)\rVert_2
=\tfrac{\sqrt b}2\lVert h-h_n\rVert_2\).  Adding and subtracting the bounded-case
expansion, and using
\(\lVert\mathscr L_b(h-h_n)\rVert_2\leq\sqrt b\lVert h-h_n\rVert_2\),
\begin{equation}
 \limsup_{t\to0}\frac1{\lvert t\rvert}
 \left\lVert
 \sqrt{\frac{d(\mu_t^{\otimes b}C_b)}{d\mu^{(b),c}}}
 -1-\frac t2\mathscr L_bh
 \right\rVert_2
 \leq\sqrt b\lVert h-h_n\rVert_2 .
 \label{eq:rg-pushed-score-approximation}
\end{equation}
The left side does not depend on \(n\), so letting \(n\to\infty\) makes it zero,
which is the general case.

Conditional Jensen gives \(\lVert U_b\rVert\leq1\) and preservation of the mean,
so \(\mathscr L_b\) has the stated type and
\(\lVert\mathscr L_b\rVert\leq\lVert\mathscr I_b\rVert=\sqrt b\).  Finally
\eqref{eq:rg-action-total-conditional-variance} applied to
\(\mathscr I_bh\), together with
\eqref{eq:rg-score-replication-norm}, is exactly
\eqref{eq:rg-extensive-fisher-defect}, and a nonnegative conditional variance
integrates to zero precisely when it vanishes almost everywhere.
\(\square\)

Two boundaries of \eqref{eq:rg-extensive-fisher-defect} deserve a sentence.  A
channel that maps every block to one point sends every score to zero, so a fixed
channel can annihilate the entire tangent.  In the opposite direction, Fisher
equality at one parameter is not recovery of the experiment: let \(A\) and \(B\)
be independent with \(\Pr_\theta(A=1)=\tfrac12+\tfrac\theta4\) and
\(\Pr_\theta(B=1)=\tfrac12+\tfrac{\theta^2}4\), and let the channel retain only
\(A\).  At \(\theta=0\) the \(B\)-score vanishes, so the full score is
\(A\)-measurable and equality holds, yet the conditional law of \(B\) varies with
\(\theta\) and no parameter-independent reverse kernel reconstructs the
experiment. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Bounded action classes embed isometrically in the Fisher tangent}{prop:rg-action-score-isometry}
For \(\varphi\in\mathfrak B=L^\infty(\pi;\R)\), the normalized exponential path
\(\widehat\pi^{t\varphi}\) of
\eqref{eq:rg-normalized-perturbation-action} is differentiable in quadratic mean
with score
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr S_\pi[\varphi]=-\varphi+\pi(\varphi),
 \label{eq:rg-action-score-map}
\end{equation}
which depends only on the class \([\varphi]\in\overline{\mathfrak B}\).  Equip
the action quotient with the Fisher norm
\begin{equation}
 \lVert[\varphi]\rVert_F
 =\inf_{c\in\R}\lVert\varphi-c\rVert_{L^2(\pi)}
 =\lVert\varphi-\pi(\varphi)\rVert_{L^2(\pi)}.
 \label{eq:rg-action-fisher-norm}
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathscr S_\pi:(\overline{\mathfrak B},\lVert\cdot\rVert_F)\to L^2_0(\pi)\)
is a linear isometry whose range is the bounded centered subspace; that range is
dense but generally proper, so the Fisher-norm completion of the bounded action
quotient is canonically \(L^2_0(\pi)\).  The two quotient norms are ordered by
\(\lVert[\varphi]\rVert_F\leq\lVert[\varphi]\rVert_{\mathrm{osc}}\).  Moreover
the centering square commutes,
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr S_{\pi^c}\bigl(\overline U[\varphi]\bigr)
 =U\mathscr S_\pi[\varphi],
 \label{eq:rg-score-action-square}
\end{equation}
and for the replicated block action
\(\Phi_\varphi(x_1,\dots,x_b)=\sum_i\varphi(x_i)\) one has
\(\mathscr S_{\mu^{(b)}}[\Phi_\varphi]=\mathscr I_b\mathscr S_\mu[\varphi]\) and,
after the block arrow, \(\mathscr L_b\mathscr S_\mu[\varphi]\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Boundedness gives the uniform expansions \(e^{-t\varphi}=1-t\varphi+O(t^2)\) and
\(\pi(e^{-t\varphi})=1-t\pi(\varphi)+O(t^2)\), so the density ratio of
\(\widehat\pi^{t\varphi}\) is \(1-t[\varphi-\pi(\varphi)]+O(t^2)\) uniformly and
its square root is \(1+\tfrac t2\mathscr S_\pi[\varphi]+O(t^2)\), which is
\eqref{eq:rg-dqm-definition}.  Adding a constant to \(\varphi\) leaves
\eqref{eq:rg-action-score-map} unchanged.  The \(L^2\) minimizer over constants
is the mean, giving \eqref{eq:rg-action-fisher-norm} and hence the isometry;
\(\inf_c\lVert\varphi-c\rVert_2\leq\inf_c\lVert\varphi-c\rVert_\infty\) gives the
norm ordering.  Bounded functions are dense in \(L^2(\pi)\) and centering is
continuous, so the range is dense; it is proper whenever \(L^2_0(\pi)\) contains
an unbounded element, as for \(\pi=N(0,1)\) and \(h(x)=x\).  Identity
\eqref{eq:rg-score-action-square} is mean preservation of \(U\) together with
linearity, and the replication statement is
\eqref{eq:rg-score-replication}. \(\square\)

The two norms on \(\overline{\mathfrak B}\) do different work and must not be
exchanged.  The oscillation norm of
\eqref{eq:rg-action-oscillation-norm} controls the nonlinear map \(Q\); the
Fisher norm controls the quadratic-mean tangent.  Completing in the Fisher norm
introduces unbounded scores on which the nonlinear bounded action map of
\Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus} is not defined.  A spectral statement
proved on \(L^2_0\) is therefore not a statement about the nonlinear bounded
action chart, and conversely. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Two further separations belong here, and each is stated with the witness that
makes it sharp.  First, every class in the Fisher completion is realized as a
two-sided quadratic-mean score by the \emph{quadratic} path of
\Cref{lem:rg-dqm-realization}, not by the exponential-action path
\(\widehat\pi^{t\varphi}\), which is defined only where its normalizer is
finite.  The witness that separates the two must therefore be a direction whose
exponential normalizer diverges on \emph{both} sides.  The correct witness is an
odd Hermite polynomial of degree at least three: for
\(\varphi=\mathrm{He}_3(x)=x^3-3x\in L^2_0(\gamma)\), the log integrand
\(-t\mathrm{He}_3(x)-x^2/2\) has an exact divergent tail on either side.
For \(t>0\), substitute \(x=-r\) to obtain
\[
-t\mathrm{He}_3(-r)-\frac{r^2}{2}
=tr^3-\frac{r^2}{2}-3tr\longrightarrow+\infty
\qquad(r\to+\infty),
\]
for \(t<0\), substitute \(x=r\) to obtain
\[
-t\mathrm{He}_3(r)-\frac{r^2}{2}
=|t|r^3-\frac{r^2}{2}-3|t|r\longrightarrow+\infty.
\]
The leading cubic is positive in both cases, so the exponential integrand is
eventually at least one on an infinite tail and \(N_3(t)=+\infty\) for
\emph{every} \(t\ne0\), while
\(\mathrm{He}_3\) is a perfectly good Fisher tangent direction with
\(\E_\gamma[\mathrm{He}_3]=0\) and \(\E_\gamma[\mathrm{He}_3^2]=6\).  The
degree-two direction \(\varphi=-x^2\) is \emph{not} such a witness and must not
be cited as one: its normalizer is \(\pi(e^{tx^2})=(1-2t)^{-1/2}\), which is
finite on the two-sided interval \((-\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\), taking the values
\(\sqrt6/3\) at \(t=-\tfrac14\) and \(\sqrt2\) at \(t=\tfrac14\).  This is the
classification already recorded in
\Cref{prop:ig-hermite-exponential-domain}.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

Second, the extensive replication lift is not a channel and no data-processing
statement applies to it in the coarse-to-fine direction.  The operator
\(\mathscr I_b\) of \Cref{prop:rg-score-block-lift} maps \(L^2_0(\pi)\) into
\(L^2_0(\pi^{\otimes b})\) with \(\lVert\mathscr I_b\rVert=\sqrt b>1\); it is a
lift between two different reference tangent spaces, not a pushforward along
any arrow.  No parameter-independent normalized Markov kernel realizes
\(b\)-fold replication for \(b\geq2\): if \(K\) satisfied
\(\mathcal N(x,1)K=\mathcal N(x\mathbf 1_b,I_{\mathbb R^b})\) for every \(x\), then
\Cref{thm:rg-score-pushforward-defect} would force the output Fisher
information \(b\) to satisfy \(b\leq1\).  Consequently the replication pair may
not be cited as a Markov-contraction counterexample or as evidence that Fisher
information increased along a coarse-graining arrow, because there is no such
arrow; the factor \(\sqrt b\) belongs to the reference identifications
\(I_\ell\) of \Cref{def:rg-interaction-beta} and to a declared
configuration-metric normalization, and to nothing else.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Gaussian block spectrum in the normalized Hermite basis}{thm:rg-gaussian-hermite-spectrum}
Let \(\gamma=N(0,1)\), let \(b\geq2\) be an integer, let \(X_1,\dots,X_b\) be
independent with law \(\gamma\), and let the block channel be the deterministic
normalized sum
\begin{equation}
 Z=b^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^bX_i,
 \label{eq:rg-gaussian-block-statistic}
\end{equation}
whose law is again \(\gamma\).  It is that normalization, and not a general
theorem, that makes source and target the same space and hence makes
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr L_b=U_b\mathscr I_b:L^2_0(\gamma)\longrightarrow L^2_0(\gamma)
 \label{eq:rg-gaussian-score-operator}
\end{equation}
an endomorphism.  Let \(\mathrm{He}_k\) be the probabilists' Hermite
polynomials and \(e_k=\mathrm{He}_k/\sqrt{k!}\).  Then \(\{e_k\}_{k\geq1}\) is a
complete orthonormal basis of \(L^2_0(\gamma)\) and
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr L_be_k=b^{1-k/2}e_k,
 \qquad k\geq1.
 \label{eq:rg-hermite-eigenvalues}
\end{equation}
Consequently \(\mathscr L_b\) is self-adjoint, positive, and diagonal in that
basis; it is Hilbert--Schmidt with
\(\sum_{k\geq1}b^{2-k}=b^2/(b-1)\), hence compact; its norm and spectral radius
are \(\sqrt b\); and
\begin{equation}
 \sigma(\mathscr L_b)
 =\left\{b^{1-k/2}:k\geq1\right\}\cup\{0\}.
 \label{eq:rg-hermite-spectrum}
\end{equation}
Every displayed nonzero value is a simple eigenvalue.  The value \(0\) is
\emph{not} an eigenvalue: it is the unique accumulation point of the spectrum
and belongs to the continuous spectrum.  This is a linear tangent-space
statement about the derivative at the Gaussian law; it is not a nonlinear
convergence theorem. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Completeness of the normalized Hermite system in \(L^2(\gamma)\) is standard,
and deleting \(e_0=1\) gives a complete orthonormal basis of the centered
subspace.  For each \(i\), the pair \((X_i,Z)\) is jointly centered Gaussian
with unit variances and \(\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,Z)=b^{-1/2}\), so
\(X_i\mid Z=z\sim N(z/\sqrt b,1-1/b)\).  Taking the conditional expectation of
the generating function \(\exp(tx-t^2/2)\) and completing the square,
\begin{equation}
 \E\left[e^{tX_i-t^2/2}\,\middle|\,Z=z\right]
 =\exp\left(\frac{tz}{\sqrt b}+\frac{t^2}2\left(1-\frac1b\right)
 -\frac{t^2}2\right)
 =\exp\left(sz-\frac{s^2}2\right),
 \quad s=\frac t{\sqrt b}.
 \label{eq:rg-mehler-generating}
\end{equation}
The generating identity
\(\exp(sz-s^2/2)=\sum_{k\geq0}\mathrm{He}_k(z)s^k/k!\) converges in
\(L^2(\gamma)\) locally uniformly in \(s\), and conditional expectation is
\(L^2\) continuous, so the two power series in \(t\) may be compared coefficient
by coefficient.  This gives the Mehler regression identity
\(\E[\mathrm{He}_k(X_i)\mid Z]=b^{-k/2}\mathrm{He}_k(Z)\).  Summing over the
\(b\) replicated coordinates and dividing by \(\sqrt{k!}\) proves
\eqref{eq:rg-hermite-eigenvalues}; the argument is uniform in \(b\) and is not an
induction from the binary case.

Since \(\mathscr L_b\) is bounded and agrees with the displayed diagonal action
on a complete orthonormal basis, it is that diagonal operator, hence
self-adjoint and positive with eigenvalues \(b^{1-k/2}\downarrow0\).  Square
summability of the eigenvalues is the stated geometric series, so
\(\mathscr L_b\) is Hilbert--Schmidt and compact, and its norm is the largest
eigenvalue \(b^{1/2}\).  The spectrum of a diagonal operator is the closure of
its eigenvalue set, which is \eqref{eq:rg-hermite-spectrum}, and the eigenvalues
are pairwise distinct so each is simple.  Every eigenvalue is strictly positive,
so \(\ker\mathscr L_b=\{0\}\) and \(0\) is not an eigenvalue.  The range contains
every finite Hermite expansion and is therefore dense, but it is proper: the
vector \(y=\sum_{k\geq1}b^{1-k/2}e_k\) lies in \(L^2_0(\gamma)\) because the
coefficients are square summable, while any preimage would have every Hermite
coefficient equal to one, which is not square summable.  An injective operator
with dense, nonclosed range has \(0\) in its continuous spectrum. \(\square\)

\definitionheading{Relevance conventions for the Gaussian block spectrum}{def:rg-hermite-relevance}
Fix the replication sum \eqref{eq:rg-score-replication}, the block statistic
\eqref{eq:rg-gaussian-block-statistic}, the same Fisher norm
\(\lVert\cdot\rVert_{L^2(\gamma)}\) at every scale, and the cumulative log block
scale \(\Delta s=\log b\) of \eqref{eq:rg-cumulative-log-scale}.  Then the
exponent of the degree-\(k\) mode is
\(y_k=\log_b b^{1-k/2}=1-k/2\).  Degree one is relevant with
\(y_1=\tfrac12\), degree two is marginal with \(y_2=0\), and every degree
\(k\geq3\) is irrelevant with \(y_k=1-k/2<0\).  The constant mode
\(k=0\) would have eigenvalue \(b\) on the full space \(L^2(\gamma)\) but is
absent from the normalized probability tangent, where scores are centered.
\status{DEFINITION}

This is the promised inhabited relevance spectrum, and it locates precisely
where the growth comes from.  The one-arrow operator \(U_b\) is a contraction by
\Cref{thm:rg-action-lp-contraction}, and the bounded action sector is
nonexpansive by \eqref{eq:rg-nonlinear-action-sup-contraction}.  The eigenvalue
\(b^{1-k/2}\) exceeds one for \(k=1\) only because the replication factor
\(\lVert\mathscr I_b\rVert=\sqrt b\) of
\eqref{eq:rg-score-replication-norm} is extensive while the norm is held fixed
across scales.  Relevance in this theory is therefore a joint property of the
operator, the extensive normalization, the declared norms, and the block scale,
in exactly the sense of \Cref{def:rg-mode-exponents}.  Removing any one of the
four removes the conclusion. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Convention dependence and forbidden extensions}{prop:rg-hermite-scope}
The spectrum \eqref{eq:rg-hermite-spectrum} is specific to the scalar
independent Gaussian realization and its declared normalization.  Four exact
boundaries hold.  First, if the coordinates are jointly centered Gaussian with
unit variances and common pairwise correlation \(\rho>-1/(b-1)\), and the
statistic is normalized to unit variance as
\(Z=(\sum_iX_i)/\sqrt{b[1+(b-1)\rho]}\), then
\(\operatorname{Corr}(X_i,Z)=\alpha=\sqrt{[1+(b-1)\rho]/b}\) and
\begin{equation}
 \mathscr L_{b,\rho}e_k=b\alpha^ke_k
 =b^{1-k/2}\left[1+(b-1)\rho\right]^{k/2}e_k,
 \label{eq:rg-hermite-correlated}
\end{equation}
which differs from \eqref{eq:rg-hermite-eigenvalues} for every \(\rho\neq0\);
keeping the uncorrelated normalization instead destroys the property that the
output law is \(\gamma\), so the operator is then not an endomorphism at all.
Second, for independent \(N(0,I_d)\) coordinates and componentwise normalized
sums the tensor Hermite functions satisfy
\(\mathscr L_be_\alpha=b^{1-\lvert\alpha\rvert/2}e_\alpha\), so degree \(k\) has
multiplicity \(\binom{d+k-1}{k}\) rather than one.  Third, a common orthogonal
action commutes with the componentwise normalized sum and preserves each degree
space, whereas a general \(\GL(d)\) transformation changes the reference
covariance and therefore requires an explicitly transported family of laws and
tangent norms; no gauge link, holonomy, or curvature datum enters the scalar
theorem.  Fourth, \eqref{eq:rg-score-replication} is the score of an independent
product perturbation and is a restricted direction inside
\(L^2_0(\gamma^{\otimes b})\); the theorem says nothing about interaction scores
or higher Hoeffding sectors, which are the subject of
\Cref{sec:rg-finite-network-interactions}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For the first statement, \((X_i,Z)\) is again jointly centered Gaussian with
unit variances and correlation \(\alpha\), so
\(X_i\mid Z=z\sim N(\alpha z,1-\alpha^2)\); the computation
\eqref{eq:rg-mehler-generating} with \(b^{-1/2}\) replaced by \(\alpha\) gives
\(\E[\mathrm{He}_k(X_i)\mid Z]=\alpha^k\mathrm{He}_k(Z)\), and summing over
\(i\) gives \eqref{eq:rg-hermite-correlated}.  Substituting
\(\alpha^2=[1+(b-1)\rho]/b\) gives the second form.  For the second statement,
tensor Hermite functions factor over coordinates and each factor obeys the
scalar identity with the same \(b^{-1/2}\), so the eigenvalue depends only on
the total degree \(\lvert\alpha\rvert\), and the number of multi-indices of
total degree \(k\) in \(d\) variables is \(\binom{d+k-1}{k}\).  For the third,
an orthogonal map preserves \(N(0,I_d)\) and commutes with the componentwise
sum, hence preserves each finite-dimensional degree space, while a general
invertible linear map sends \(N(0,I_d)\) to \(N(0,QQ^\top)\), which is a
different reference law.  The fourth statement is the definition of
\(\mathscr I_b\). \(\square\)

The linearization also does not by itself establish nonlinear convergence.  The
spectrum controls the derivative at the Gaussian law, while a basin of
attraction, a remainder estimate, and the behavior of the marginal direction are
separate obligations recorded in \Cref{app:claim-ledger}. \status{OPEN}

The same
qualification is made in the primary source: \citet{JonaLasinio2001} obtains the
binary case of \eqref{eq:rg-hermite-eigenvalues} and states that the nonlinear
terms required to complete a limit theorem are not pursued there.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Source scope.}
Section 2 of \citet{JonaLasinio2001} treats the binary normalized sum, linearizes
the induced map on laws around a centered unit-variance Gaussian under
normalization, centering, and variance constraints, and states the Hermite
eigenvalues \(2^{1-k/2}\); Section 5 identifies the linearized renormalization
at a self-similar Gaussian fixed point with a conditional expectation and states
the corresponding generalized Hermite eigenvalue equation; and Section 7 gives
the two-tangent-space form of that eigenvalue equation together with its
multiplicative composition law across scales.  Those four items are what the
citation supports.  The arbitrary-integer statement
\eqref{eq:rg-hermite-eigenvalues}, the quadratic-mean realization of
\Cref{lem:rg-dqm-realization}, the extensive Fisher budget
\eqref{eq:rg-extensive-fisher-defect}, the exact spectrum
\eqref{eq:rg-hermite-spectrum} including the status of \(0\), the correlated
boundary \eqref{eq:rg-hermite-correlated}, and the typed cocycle of
\Cref{sec:rg-generalized-modes} are proved here and are not attributed to that
source. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\subsection{Essential spectrum and topology dependence}
\label{sec:rg-action-spectrum-topology}

\theoremheading{Qualified positive-unital essential-spectrum theorem}{thm:rg-unital-essential-spectrum}
Let \(X\) be a complex Banach lattice with quasi-interior unit
\(\mathbf1\in X_+\), let \(U\in\mathcal L(X)\) be positive and unital,
and write \(q:\mathcal L(X)\to\mathcal L(X)/\mathcal K(X)\) for the
Calkin quotient by the compact-operator ideal.  Use the convention
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{ess}}(U)=r\bigl(q(U)\bigr).
\label{eq:rg-calkin-essential-radius}
\end{equation}
Assume that whenever \(r(U)>r_{\mathrm{ess}}(U)\), the value \(r(U)\) is a
pole of the resolvent and \(U^*\) has a nonzero positive eigenfunctional
\(\lambda\) satisfying \(U^*\lambda=r(U)\lambda\).  Then
\begin{equation}
r(U)>1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r(U)=r_{\mathrm{ess}}(U).
\label{eq:rg-unital-essential-spectrum-result}
\end{equation}
This does not assert quasi-compactness or a Perron theorem under weaker
hypotheses. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The quotient map is contractive, so
\(r_{\mathrm{ess}}(U)\leq r(U)\).  Suppose that \(r(U)>1\) and that the
inequality is strict.  The stated hypothesis gives \(\lambda\geq0\),
\(\lambda\ne0\), and \(U^*\lambda=r(U)\lambda\).  Quasi-interiority
implies \(\lambda(\mathbf1)>0\): otherwise positivity makes \(\lambda\)
zero on the principal ideal generated by \(\mathbf1\), hence on its dense
closure.  But unitality yields
\begin{equation}
r(U)\lambda(\mathbf1)
=(U^*\lambda)(\mathbf1)
=\lambda(U\mathbf1)=\lambda(\mathbf1),
\label{eq:rg-unital-functional-pairing}
\end{equation}
a contradiction.  Therefore strict inequality is impossible, and the two
radii agree.  \(\square\)

\propositionheading{Circle witness for norm-dependent radius}{prop:rg-circle-norm-witness}
Let \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) carry its geodesic metric and Haar
probability \(\lambda\), let \(D(z)=2z\bmod1\), and set
\begin{equation}
K(y,A)=\frac12\sum_{z\in D^{-1}\{y\}}\mathbf1_A(z)
\qquad(A\subseteq\mathbb T\text{ Borel}).
\label{eq:rg-circle-forward-kernel}
\end{equation}
The kernel preserves Haar measure and its reverse is
\(\Pi(z,dy)=\delta_{D(z)}(dy)\), so the conditional-expectation operator is
\(Uf=f\circ D\).  Its spectral radius is one on
\(L^\infty(\lambda)\).  For every \(0<\alpha\leq1\), its spectral radius
on complex periodic \(C^\alpha(\mathbb T)\) equipped with the sup-plus-H\"older
norm is \(2^\alpha\).  At \(\alpha=1\), this means the Lipschitz space
\(C^{0,1}\), not \(C^1\).  The H\"older space with this standard norm is
not the Banach lattice of \Cref{thm:rg-unital-essential-spectrum}; the
witness is a separate topology comparison.  Hence the same exact kernel has
distinct spectral conclusions in two declared topologies. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For bounded \(f\), Haar preservation gives
\(\Vert f\circ D^n\Vert_\infty=\Vert f\Vert_\infty\), so
\(\Vert U^n\Vert=1\) and the first radius is one.  With
\([f]_\alpha\) the H\"older seminorm,
\begin{equation}
[f\circ D^n]_\alpha\leq2^{\alpha n}[f]_\alpha,
\qquad
\Vert U^nf\Vert_\infty\leq\Vert f\Vert_\infty,
\label{eq:rg-circle-holder-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
so the second radius is at most \(2^\alpha\).  For the fixed Fourier mode
\(f(t)=e^{2\pi it}\), evaluate \(f\circ D^n\) at \(0\) and
\(2^{-(n+1)}\).  Their values differ by two while their circle distance is
\(2^{-(n+1)}\), whence
\begin{equation}
[f\circ D^n]_\alpha\geq2^{1+\alpha(n+1)}.
\label{eq:rg-circle-holder-lower-bound}
\end{equation}
After division by the fixed nonzero \(C^\alpha\) norm of \(f\), this gives
the matching lower spectral-radius bound.  \(\square\)

\section{Exact finite-network interaction coordinates}
\label{sec:rg-finite-network-interactions}

The following construction is an action-coordinate construction, not a
claim that an arbitrary coarse channel preserves a product law.  At every
scale \(\ell\), let \(V_\ell\) be an arbitrary finite set, let
\(\mathsf X_{\ell i}\) be standard Borel for \(i\in V_\ell\), and put
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf X_\ell=\prod_{i\in V_\ell}\mathsf X_{\ell i},\qquad
 \nu_\ell=\bigotimes_{i\in V_\ell}\nu_{\ell i},\qquad
 \pi_\ell\sim\nu_\ell.
 \label{eq:rg-interaction-product-reference}
\end{equation}
Here each \(\nu_{\ell i}\) is a probability law and \(\sim\) denotes
mutual absolute continuity.  Thus \(L^\infty(\pi_\ell)\) and
\(L^\infty(\nu_\ell)\) have the same equivalence classes and the same
essential-supremum norm, without requiring bounded Radon--Nikodym ratios.
The product structure of \(\nu_\ell\), rather than equivalence alone, makes
the coordinate integrals below representative-independent by Fubini.  For a scale arrow, the target premise
\(\pi_{\ell+1}=\pi_\ell K_\ell\sim\nu_{\ell+1}\) is separately required;
it does not follow from \(\pi_\ell\sim\nu_\ell\).  The evidence mass
\(M_\ell\) of \Cref{def:rg-measure-pair} is retained independently of
these normalized references. \status{DEFINITION}

For \(V_\ell=\varnothing\), the product is the one-point probability
space, \(\mathcal G_\ell=\{0\}\), and all assembly, extraction, and
interaction maps below are the unique zero maps.  The nontrivial estimates
that divide by \(3^{|V_\ell|}-1\) are stated only when
\(V_\ell\ne\varnothing\).

\propositionheading{Product equivalence is an admitted, not an automatic, scale premise}{prop:rg-product-equivalence-not-preserved}
Let \(\mathsf X_0=\{0,1\}\) with its uniform law \(\pi_0=\nu_0\), and
let \(K\) be the deterministic diagonal-cloning channel
\begin{equation}
 K(x,\{(x,x)\})=1
 \quad\hbox{from }\{0,1\}\hbox{ to }\{0,1\}^2.
 \label{eq:rg-diagonal-cloning-counterexample}
\end{equation}
Then \(\pi_1=\pi_0K\) is supported on \(\{(0,0),(1,1)\}\).  There is no
product probability \(\nu_1=\nu_{11}\otimes\nu_{12}\) on \(\{0,1\}^2\)
equivalent to \(\pi_1\).  In particular the uniform product reference gives
positive mass to all four points and is not equivalent.  No target Hoeffding
decomposition relative to a product reference is admitted at this target.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Equivalence would make both diagonal atoms positive under \(\nu_1\).  Thus
each marginal would charge both \(0\) and \(1\), forcing both off-diagonal
atoms to have positive product mass.  But \(\pi_1\) assigns them mass zero,
contradicting equivalence.  The uniform product is the stated special case.
\(\square\)

For \(A\subseteq V_\ell\), let \(C_{\ell,A}\) be conditional integration
against the product complement, lifted back to \(\mathsf X_\ell\):
\begin{equation}
 (C_{\ell,A}f)(x_A)
 =\int f(x_A,y_{A^c}) \nu_{\ell,A^c}(dy_{A^c}),
 \qquad C_{\ell,\varnothing}f=\nu_\ell(f).
 \label{eq:rg-coordinate-expectation}
\end{equation}
The operators commute and obey
\(C_{\ell,A}C_{\ell,B}=C_{\ell,A\cap B}\).  Their Boolean-lattice
Mobius projectors are
\begin{equation}
 P_{\ell,A}
 =\sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|A|-|B|}C_{\ell,B}
 =\prod_{i\in A}(I-C_{\ell,V_\ell\setminus\{i\}})
 C_{\ell,A},
 \qquad A\subseteq V_\ell.
 \label{eq:rg-hoeffding-mobius-projectors}
\end{equation}
The second expression only records the commuting-product identity; the
first is the definition.  Let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal H_{\ell,A}=P_{\ell,A}L^\infty(\nu_\ell),\qquad
 \mathcal G_\ell=
 \bigoplus_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V_\ell}^{\ell^1}
 \mathcal H_{\ell,A},\qquad
 \lVert g\rVert_{\mathcal G_\ell}
 =\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V_\ell}\lVert g_A\rVert_\infty.
 \label{eq:rg-full-interaction-banach-space}
\end{equation}
Thus \(g_A\) depends only on \(x_A\) and has zero product-reference mean
in every active coordinate.  This includes every nonempty hyperedge; it is
finite at each \(\ell\), with no bound imposed on \(\lvert V_\ell\rvert\).
Define the action quotient and its assembly/extraction maps by
\begin{equation}
 \overline{\mathfrak B}_\ell=L^\infty(\nu_\ell)/\mathbb R1,\qquad
 \phi_g:=\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V_\ell}g_A,\qquad
 E_\ell g=[\phi_g],\qquad
 \mathsf H_\ell[f]=(P_{\ell,A}f)_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V_\ell}.
 \label{eq:rg-interaction-assembly-extraction}
\end{equation}

\theoremheading{Hoeffding assembly is an exact finite-network action isomorphism}{thm:rg-hoeffding-action-isomorphism}
For every finite \(V_\ell\), \(E_\ell\) and \(\mathsf H_\ell\) in
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-assembly-extraction} are bounded inverse linear
isomorphisms,
\begin{equation}
 \mathsf H_\ell E_\ell=I_{\mathcal G_\ell},\qquad
 E_\ell\mathsf H_\ell=I_{\overline{\mathfrak B}_\ell},\qquad
 \lVert E_\ell\rVert\leq1,\qquad
 \lVert\mathsf H_\ell\rVert\leq3^{|V_\ell|}-1.
 \label{eq:rg-hoeffding-inverse-and-bounds}
\end{equation}
The latter bound is valid for every finite network but is not dimension-free.
Its exponential dependence is sharp in the worst case: for biased Rademacher
product coordinates and \(f(x)=\prod_i x_i\), the sum of component norms
approaches the bound at the corresponding product scale as the bias tends to
one. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The commuting conditional expectations give
\(P_{\ell,A}P_{\ell,B}=\mathbf1_{A=B}P_{\ell,A}\) and
\(\sum_{A\subseteq V_\ell}P_{\ell,A}=I\).  Hence nonempty components
recover \(g\), while their sum is \(f-C_{\ell,\varnothing}f\), which is
the class \([f]\).  The triangle inequality gives \(\lVert E_\ell\rVert
\leq1\).  Since \(P_{\ell,A}\) is an alternating sum of \(2^{|A|}\)
contractive coordinate expectations, its norm is at most \(2^{|A|}\).
Summing over nonempty \(A\) gives
\(\sum_{A\ne\varnothing}2^{|A|}=3^{|V_\ell|}-1\).  For independent
\(\{-1,1\}\)-coordinates with \(\Pr(X_i=1)=p\geq1/2\), set
\(n=|V_\ell|\) and \(f=\prod_iX_i\).  Direct expansion gives
\(\sum_{\varnothing\ne A\subseteq V_\ell}\lVert P_Af\rVert_\infty
=(4p-1)^n-(2p-1)^n\), which tends to \(3^n-1\) as \(p\uparrow1\).
\(\square\)

\paragraph{Gauge covariance and its necessary realization hypothesis.}
Let a gauge transformation at scale \(\ell\) be realized by a Borel
coordinate permutation \(\sigma\) and coordinate Borel isomorphisms whose
product map \(\vartheta_\ell\) sends \(\nu_\ell\) to
\(\widehat\nu_\ell\).  Write
\(\Theta_\ell f=f\circ\vartheta_\ell^{-1}\) and let
\(\overline\Theta_\ell\) be its action on the quotient by constants.
Fubini then gives
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_\ell C_{\ell,A}^{\nu}
 =C_{\ell,\sigma A}^{\widehat\nu}\Theta_\ell,\qquad
 \Theta_\ell P_{\ell,A}^{\nu}
 =P_{\ell,\sigma A}^{\widehat\nu}\Theta_\ell,\qquad
 \overline\Theta_\ell E_\ell
 =\widehat E_\ell\Theta_\ell^{\mathcal G},
 \qquad
 \Theta_\ell^{\mathcal G}\mathsf H_\ell
 =\widehat{\mathsf H}_\ell\overline\Theta_\ell.
 \label{eq:rg-hoeffding-gauge-covariance}
\end{equation}
Fubini proves the first identity and the rest follow by finite Mobius sums.
Consequently coordinate expectations and interaction components are
gauge-covariant under this componentwise/permutation realization.  Product
measure preservation alone is insufficient: a product-Haar-preserving shear
of \(\mathbb T^2\), for example \((x_1,x_2)\mapsto
(x_1,x_1+x_2)\), sends a singleton function of \(x_2\) to one depending on
both coordinates and mixes a singleton component into a pair component.  A
grading-intertwining hypothesis is therefore necessary for a claim that
hyperedge degree is gauge covariant; componentwise/permutation realization is
the declared sufficient hypothesis here, not the only logically possible one.

it must not be promoted to pointwise covariance of a selected reverse-kernel

\propositionheading{Radon--Nikodym covariance of the nonlinear interaction step}{prop:rg-interaction-rn-gauge-covariance}
At adjacent scales, suppose
\(\vartheta_\ell:\mathsf X_\ell\to\widehat{\mathsf X}_\ell\) and
\(\vartheta_{\ell+1}:\mathsf X_{\ell+1}\to
\widehat{\mathsf X}_{\ell+1}\) have the componentwise/permutation Borel
realizations above.  Transform the complete measure-pair data by
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\rho_\ell=(\vartheta_\ell)_\#\rho_\ell,\qquad
 \widehat m_\ell=(\vartheta_\ell)_\#m_\ell,\qquad
 \widehat\pi_\ell=(\vartheta_\ell)_\#\pi_\ell,
 \label{eq:rg-interaction-transformed-measure-pair}
\end{equation}
and suppose the transformed normalized kernel obeys
\begin{equation}
 \widehat K_\ell(\vartheta_\ell x,\vartheta_{\ell+1}B)
 =K_\ell(x,B)
 \label{eq:rg-interaction-kernel-gauge-covariance}
\end{equation}
for every Borel \(B\).
Then, as Radon--Nikodym equivalence classes,
\begin{align}
 \widehat Q_\ell(\Theta_\ell\varphi)
 &=\Theta_{\ell+1}Q_\ell(\varphi),
 \label{eq:rg-action-rn-gauge-covariance}\\
 \widehat U_\ell^{\Theta_\ell\varphi}\Theta_\ell h
 &=\Theta_{\ell+1}U_\ell^\varphi h.
 \label{eq:rg-tilted-derivative-gauge-covariance}
\end{align}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}\Theta_\ell^{\mathcal G}
 =\Theta_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}T_\ell^{\mathcal G}.
 \label{eq:rg-nonlinear-interaction-gauge-covariance}
\end{equation}
If the retained projections also intertwine,
\(\widehat R_k\Theta_k^{\mathcal G}
=\Theta_k^{\mathcal G}R_k\) for \(k=\ell,\ell+1\), then both residuals
are covariant:
\begin{align}
 \widehat r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}
   (\Theta_\ell^{\mathcal G}g)
 &=\Theta_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}(g),
 \label{eq:rg-coordinate-residual-gauge-covariance}\\
 \widehat{\overline r}_{\ell+1}^{Q}
   (\Theta_\ell^{\mathcal G}g)
 &=\overline\Theta_{\ell+1}\overline r_{\ell+1}^{Q}(g).
 \label{eq:rg-action-residual-gauge-covariance}
\end{align}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Kernel covariance gives
\[
 \widehat m_\ell\widehat K_\ell
 =(\vartheta_{\ell+1})_\#(m_\ell K_\ell),\qquad
 (e^{-\Theta_\ell\varphi}\widehat m_\ell)\widehat K_\ell
 =(\vartheta_{\ell+1})_\#((e^{-\varphi}m_\ell)K_\ell).
\]
The Radon--Nikodym derivative of two measures pushed through the same Borel
isomorphism is the transported original derivative.  Applying \(-\log\)
proves \eqref{eq:rg-action-rn-gauge-covariance} without selecting a
pointwise reverse conditional.  Differentiate this identity at an arbitrary
bounded \(\varphi\), using
\Cref{prop:rg-action-bounded-recentering}, to obtain
\eqref{eq:rg-tilted-derivative-gauge-covariance}.  Compose the first identity
with the assembly and extraction intertwinings in
\eqref{eq:rg-hoeffding-gauge-covariance} to prove
\eqref{eq:rg-nonlinear-interaction-gauge-covariance}.  Finally insert the
retained-projection intertwining into \(I-R_{\ell+1}\) and into the exact
residual identity \eqref{eq:rg-interaction-action-residual}; this proves the
two residual formulas. \(\square\)

 =\mathsf H_{\ell+1}\overline Q_\ell E_\ell:

 \label{eq:rg-exact-nonlinear-interaction-map}

It is exact as a change of the bounded action class, conditional on the

 \label{eq:rg-interaction-tilted-conditional-expectation}

 \label{eq:rg-nonlinear-interaction-derivative}

\corollaryheading{Temperedness of interaction comparison trivializations}{cor:rg-interaction-tempered}
Suppose the declared comparison isomorphisms
\(J_\ell:\mathcal G_\star\to\mathcal G_\ell\) obey the growth bound
\begin{equation}
 \log^+\lVert J_n\rVert+\log^+\lVert J_n^{-1}\rVert
 \leq c\left(1+\lvert V_n\rvert\right)
 \label{eq:rg-interaction-trivialization-growth}
\end{equation}
for a constant \(c\) independent of \(n\).  This bound holds for every
trivialization assembled from the maps of
\eqref{eq:rg-hoeffding-inverse-and-bounds} together with a bounded gauge

\(\lVert\mathsf H_\ell\rVert\leq3^{\lvert V_\ell\rvert}-1\).  Then the tempering
hypothesis \eqref{eq:rg-tempered-trivialization} of
\Cref{thm:rg-tempered-comparison} holds whenever
\(\lvert V_n\rvert/s_{n\leftarrow\ell}\to0\).  In particular it holds for every
blocking sequence whose vertex counts do not increase and whose block ratios
satisfy \(b_k\geq b>1\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Insert \eqref{eq:rg-interaction-trivialization-growth} into the left side of
\eqref{eq:rg-tempered-trivialization} and divide by \(s_{n\leftarrow\ell}\); the
constant term vanishes because \(s_{n\leftarrow\ell}\to\infty\).  If the vertex
counts do not increase then \(\lvert V_n\rvert\leq\lvert V_\ell\rvert\) while
\(s_{n\leftarrow\ell}\geq(n-\ell)\log b\to\infty\).  The stated operator bounds
are \eqref{eq:rg-hoeffding-inverse-and-bounds}. \(\square\)

This corollary is the point at which the sharp finite-size extraction bound of
\Cref{thm:rg-hoeffding-action-isomorphism} becomes a hypothesis of the mode
theory rather than a curiosity: the bound is exponential in
\(\lvert V_\ell\rvert\), so a scheme in which the network grows fast enough with
depth can violate \eqref{eq:rg-tempered-trivialization} and change the reported
exponents, exactly as in
\Cref{prop:rg-superexponential-distortion}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Let \(R_\ell:\mathcal G_\ell\to\mathcal G_\ell\) be a declared bounded

 &r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}(g_\ell)

 \label{eq:rg-interaction-action-residual}

 \subseteq\operatorname{Ran}R_{\ell+1}

 \label{eq:rg-interaction-exact-image-invariance}
\end{equation}

\section{Exact closure generates hyperedges}
\label{sec:rg-hypergraph-closure}

Suppose the complete nonnegative density of the law, including the baseline,
is factorized against a product or block-product reference.  Marginalizing an
internal variable multiplies every incident factor, including baseline
factors, and integrates their product.  The result is one factor on the union
of the remaining incident scopes.  Iterating this operation represents the
normalized law by generally unnormalized factors on the elimination cliques;
the inherited total normalizer, not each factor, normalizes their product.
Tonelli's theorem makes the resulting joint law independent of elimination
order even though the displayed factorization is scheme dependent.  For an
arbitrary correlated baseline
without such a factorization, retain the baseline as one global factor and use
the law-level pushforward instead.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

For a finite meta-agent set $\mathcal P$ and a finite-valued effective action,
choose an anchor $z^\circ$ and define
\begin{equation}
\Phi_A^c(z_A)
=\sum_{B\subseteq A}(-1)^{|A|-|B|}
H_o^c(z_B,z_{B^c}^\circ),
\qquad A\subseteq\mathcal P.
\label{eq:rg-mobius-potentials}
\end{equation}
Mobius inversion gives
\begin{equation}
H_o^c(z)=\sum_{A\subseteq\mathcal P}\Phi_A^c(z_A).
\label{eq:rg-full-hypergraph-action}
\end{equation}
Thus the full hypergraph family is exactly closed.  If $H_o^c$ is extended
valued, retaining it as one top-order factor gives exact closure without
forming undefined alternating sums.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

The anchor is a covariant background, not a coordinatewise constant under
frame changes.  Consequently each displayed factor obeys
$\Phi_A^c(gz_A;gz^\circ)=\Phi_A^c(z_A;z^\circ)$ when the action is
gauge invariant.  If an anchor is held fixed only in one coordinate chart,
gauge covariance is asserted only for the reconstructed sum
\eqref{eq:rg-full-hypergraph-action}.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

Pairwise closure is false in general.  Eliminating the center $s_0$ of an
Ising star produces
\begin{equation}
\sum_{s_0=\pm1}\exp\left[s_0\left(h_0+\sum_{r=1}^3J_rs_r\right)\right]
=2\cosh\left(h_0+\sum_{r=1}^3J_rs_r\right),
\label{eq:rg-hidden-star-factor}
\end{equation}
whose negative log has cubic coefficient
$2\operatorname{sech}^2(h_0)\tanh(h_0)J_1J_2J_3+O(J^5)$ when
$h_0J_1J_2J_3\ne0$; it is therefore nonzero for all sufficiently small
nonzero $J_r$ with $h_0\ne0$.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Fine--meta bridge kernels}
\label{sec:rg-cross-scale-kernels}

The posterior bridge
\begin{equation}
B_o(dy,dz)=\Pi_o(dy)C(y,dz)
\label{eq:rg-posterior-bridge}
\end{equation}
defines the full reverse kernel $R_o(z,dy)=B_o(dy\given z)$.  For fine agent
$j$ and meta-agent $I$, take the $(Y_j,Z_I)$ marginal and disintegrate it in
both directions:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{o,j}(dy_j)K_{I\leftarrow j}(y_j,dz_I)
=\Pi_{o,I}^c(dz_I)K_{j\leftarrow I}(z_I,dy_j).
\label{eq:rg-agent-meta-bayes-adjoint}
\end{equation}
These normalized kernels are the exact agent-to-meta and meta-to-agent
pair-marginal association, or bridge, kernels.  They are not thereby causal
generative interactions.  For bounded observables $f$ and $g$,
they obey
\begin{equation}
\int f(y_j)(K_{I\leftarrow j}g)(y_j)\Pi_{o,j}(dy_j)
=\int (K_{j\leftarrow I}f)(z_I)g(z_I)\Pi_{o,I}^c(dz_I).
\label{eq:rg-agent-meta-adjointness}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Equation~\eqref{eq:rg-agent-meta-bayes-adjoint} does not say that $Z_I$ alone
reconstructs the full fine posterior.  The exact full reverse generally reads
all meta variables.  A product baseline and blockwise coarse channel factorize
the baseline reverse, but a likelihood coupling blocks can destroy that
factorization in the posterior.  Posterior-local refinement additionally
requires the conditional-independence property
$Y_I\perp Z_{-I}\mid(Z_I,o)$, or another separately proved sufficient
condition.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

For a deterministic statistic $z=c(y)$, the observable pullback and posterior
conditional expectation are
\begin{equation}
(\mathsf P f)(y)=f(c(y)),
\qquad
(\mathsf Cg)(z)=\E_{\Pi_o}[g(Y)\given c(Y)=z].
\label{eq:rg-conditional-operators}
\end{equation}
They satisfy $\mathsf C\mathsf P=I$ in $L^p(\Pi_o^c)$, equivalently
$\Pi_o^c$-almost everywhere, and compose by the tower property along a fixed
nested filtration.  Values of a conditional kernel at unused coarse points
are immaterial.  For a randomized channel, the corresponding
operators remain adjoint but $\mathsf C\mathsf P=I$ is not automatic.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Gauge-covariant cross-scale operators}
\label{sec:rg-gauge-cross-scale}

For an arbitrary block partition $\mathcal P$, define the component-indexed
meta-agent set
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal P}
=\{\widehat I=(I,c):I\in\mathcal P,
c\in\pi_0(\Gamma_I)\}.
\label{eq:rg-component-meta-index}
\end{equation}
Choose a rooted spanning tree inside each connected component $\widehat I$ and
require successive trees to be nested.  From this point through the rooted
operator formulas, $I,J$ denote elements of $\widehat{\mathcal P}$ and
$r_I$ denotes their single component root.  The state of an original
disconnected block is the product over its component meta-agents; collapsing
that product to one root, row, or feature is an additional declared coarse
channel.  For channel $x\in\{b,m\}$, let
$\tau^x_{I\leftarrow i}$ be the ordered product of the channel-specific graph
links from $i$ to the root $r_I$.  Under the passive section rechoices
$a_i^b=a_i$ and $a_i^m=b_i$ of \Cref{eq:geo-local-reframing}, it transforms as
\begin{equation}
\tau^{x\prime}_{I\leftarrow i}
=(a^x_{r_I})^{-1}\tau^x_{I\leftarrow i}a_i^x.
\label{eq:rg-tree-transport-law}
\end{equation}
These are two passive coordinate rechoices in one principal $G$-bundle, not
an active $G\times G$ gauge symmetry.  For one active automorphism, the two
local functions instead obey $k_i^m=h_i^{-1}k_i^bh_i$.

Every non-tree edge is retained through the channel-typed based holonomy map
\begin{equation}
H_I^x:\pi_1(\Gamma_I,r_I)\longrightarrow G,
\qquad
H_I^{x\prime}(\gamma)
=(a^x_{r_I})^{-1}H_I^x(\gamma)a^x_{r_I}.
\label{eq:rg-full-holonomy-representation}
\end{equation}
For each marked boundary edge $e:j\to i$ from block $J$ to block $I$, retain
\begin{equation}
V_e^x=\tau^x_{I\leftarrow i}\Theta_e^x
(\tau^x_{J\leftarrow j})^{-1},
\qquad
V_e^{x\prime}=(a^x_{r_I})^{-1}V_e^xa^x_{r_J}.
\label{eq:rg-dressed-boundary-edge}
\end{equation}
The exact datum is the simultaneous root-framed equivariant state
$(\bar z_I^x,H_I^x,\{V_e^x\})$, or its one simultaneous root-gauge orbit;
separately quotienting each holonomy loses its orientation relative to the root
features and boundary legs.  For noncompact $G$, a naive conjugacy quotient
need not be standard Borel, so the measure tier retains the raw root-framed
$G$-space unless a proper-action or standard-Borel quotient theorem is
supplied.  Different trees change the presentation by Nielsen transformations;
nested trees with the compatibility below give strict composition, while
arbitrary choices give a root-gauge-equivariant isomorphism of presentations.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Full-law holonomy alternatives.}

\label{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-holonomy-channel}

\paragraph{Scope of the word ``holonomy'' in the blindness alternative.}
The name is inherited from
\Cref{thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal}, where the acting object is the genuine based-loop transport group

marginal-law sections. The full-law alternative above generalizes that statement by widening the
acting object to an \emph{arbitrary typed groupoid of bimeasurable isomorphisms}, and no connection
form, horizontal distribution, lift, loop, or curvature enters its hypotheses or its proof: the
argument is substitution of an equivariant channel into
\eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-generative} and \eqref{eq:rg-pointwise-parent-triple}. What is proved is
therefore \emph{covariance of the full parent laws under a declared groupoid action}, with
same-slice invariance on the isotropy subgroup of $(o,X)$; it is not a statement that a holonomy has
been computed or quotiented away. Loop transports are one admissible instance of that groupoid, which

from it. The retention alternative, by contrast, does carry literal holonomy data: the based holonomy

\section{Exact attention between meta-agents}
\label{sec:rg-meta-attention}

$\alpha_i(y)$ be a normalized receiver occupancy and define the joint marked

\theoremheading{Standard-Borel strong lumpability}{thm:rg-strong-lumpability}
Let $(\mathsf Y,\mathscr Y)$ and $(\mathsf Z,\mathscr Z)$ be standard Borel, let
$c:\mathsf Y\to\mathsf Z$ be Borel and surjective, and let $T$ be a Markov
kernel on $\mathsf Y$.  Consider
\begin{equation}
T(y,c^{-1}B)=T(y',c^{-1}B)
\quad\text{whenever }c(y)=c(y'),
\qquad\text{for every }B\in\mathscr Z.
\label{eq:rg-strong-lumpability}
\end{equation}
If a Markov kernel $T^c$ on $\mathsf Z$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
c_\#(\mu T)=(c_\#\mu)T^c
\qquad\text{for every probability }\mu\text{ on }\mathsf Y,
\label{eq:rg-lumped-kernel}
\end{equation}
then \eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability} holds and $T^c$ is unique.  Conversely, if
\eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability} holds and $c$ admits a Borel right inverse
$\varsigma:\mathsf Z\to\mathsf Y$ with $c\circ\varsigma=\operatorname{id}$, then
\begin{equation}
T^c(z,B)=T\bigl(\varsigma(z),c^{-1}B\bigr)
\label{eq:rg-lumped-kernel-formula}
\end{equation}
is a Markov kernel satisfying \eqref{eq:rg-lumped-kernel}.  A Borel right
inverse exists in the two cases used in this chapter: a countable coarse space,
and a product projection $\mathsf Y=\mathsf Z\times\mathsf Y'$ with $\mathsf Y'$
nonempty, for which one fixes any point of $\mathsf Y'$.  Without a measurable
selection, \eqref{eq:rg-lumped-kernel-formula} still defines the coarse
transition on the range of $c$ and the selection must be declared separately.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Assume \eqref{eq:rg-lumped-kernel} and take $\mu=\delta_y$.  Then
$c_\#(\delta_yT)(B)=T(y,c^{-1}B)$ while
$((c_\#\delta_y)T^c)(B)=T^c(c(y),B)$, so
\begin{equation}
T(y,c^{-1}B)=T^c(c(y),B)
\qquad\text{for every }y\in\mathsf Y,\ B\in\mathscr Z,
\label{eq:rg-lumpability-pointwise}
\end{equation}
whose right side depends on $y$ only through $c(y)$; this is
\eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability}.  Surjectivity of $c$ and
\eqref{eq:rg-lumpability-pointwise} determine $T^c$ at every $z$, so it is
unique.

Conversely assume \eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability} and a Borel $\varsigma$.  For
fixed $z$, the set map $B\mapsto T(\varsigma(z),c^{-1}B)$ is a probability
measure, because $c^{-1}$ commutes with countable disjoint unions and
$c^{-1}\mathsf Z=\mathsf Y$.  For fixed $B$, the map
$z\mapsto T(\varsigma(z),c^{-1}B)$ is the composition of the Borel map
$\varsigma$ with the measurable map $y\mapsto T(y,c^{-1}B)$, hence measurable.
So \eqref{eq:rg-lumped-kernel-formula} is a Markov kernel.  Since
$c(\varsigma(c(y)))=c(y)$, hypothesis \eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability} gives
$T^c(c(y),B)=T(y,c^{-1}B)$ for every $y$.  Therefore, for any probability
$\mu$,
\[
c_\#(\mu T)(B)
=\int T(y,c^{-1}B)\mu(dy)
=\int T^c(c(y),B)\mu(dy)
=\int T^c(z,B)(c_\#\mu)(dz),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:rg-lumped-kernel}.  $\square$

Strong lumpability is exactly the every-initial-law condition, and it is not
necessary for a single initial law.  Take $\mathsf Y=\{1,2,3\}$ with
$c(1)=c(2)=a$ and $c(3)=\beta$, and let $T$ send $1$ to $3$, send $2$ to $1$ or
$2$ with equal probability, and send $3$ to $1$ or $3$ with equal probability.
Then $T(1,\{1,2\})=0$ while $T(2,\{1,2\})=1$, so
\eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability} fails.  Started at $\delta_3$, however, the
chain never leaves $\{1,3\}$, on which $c$ is injective, so the coarse process is
a Markov chain with transitions $a\mapsto\beta$ surely and
$\beta\mapsto a$ or $\beta$ with equal probability.  Weak lumpability at a
selected initial law is therefore a strictly weaker statement, and it is the
only one available when \eqref{eq:rg-strong-lumpability} fails.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The finite-state form of the biconditional is classical and
is the lumpability criterion of \citet[Ch.~6]{KemenySnell1976}; that source is
cited only at its finite-state scope, and the standard-Borel statement above is
proved here. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Exact discrete projection-memory recurrence}{thm:rg-projection-memory}
Let $\mathsf X$ and $\mathsf W$ be vector spaces, let $T:\mathsf X\to\mathsf X$,
$\mathsf C:\mathsf X\to\mathsf W$, and $\mathsf P:\mathsf W\to\mathsf X$ be
linear with $\mathsf C\mathsf P=I_{\mathsf W}$, and set
\begin{equation}
\Pi^{\mathrm{res}}=\mathsf P\mathsf C,
\qquad
\mathsf Q=I-\Pi^{\mathrm{res}},
\label{eq:rg-resolved-projection}
\end{equation}
so that $\Pi^{\mathrm{res}}$ is idempotent.  For the fine recursion
$x_{n+1}=Tx_n$ write $w_n=\mathsf Cx_n$, so that
$\Pi^{\mathrm{res}}x_n=\mathsf Pw_n$.  Then for every $n\geq0$
\begin{equation}
\mathsf Qx_n
=(\mathsf QT\mathsf Q)^n\mathsf Qx_0
+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
(\mathsf QT\mathsf Q)^{n-1-k}\mathsf QT\mathsf Pw_k,
\label{eq:rg-unresolved-solution}
\end{equation}
and the resolved coordinate obeys the exact closed recurrence
\begin{equation}
w_{n+1}
=\mathsf CT\mathsf Pw_n
+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\mathsf CT\mathsf Q(\mathsf QT\mathsf Q)^{n-1-k}\mathsf QT\mathsf Pw_k
+\mathsf CT\mathsf Q(\mathsf QT\mathsf Q)^{n}\mathsf Qx_0 .
\label{eq:rg-memory-recurrence}
\end{equation}
The operators
\begin{equation}
\mathsf CT\mathsf Q(\mathsf QT\mathsf Q)^{m}\mathsf QT\mathsf P,
\qquad m\geq0,
\label{eq:rg-memory-operators}
\end{equation}
are the memory kernels and the last term of
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-recurrence} is the unresolved initial-state term.  Equation
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-recurrence} is exact; no closure or Markov assumption is
used. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Apply $\Pi^{\mathrm{res}}$ and $\mathsf Q$ to $x_{n+1}=Tx_n$ and use
$x_n=\Pi^{\mathrm{res}}x_n+\mathsf Qx_n=\mathsf Pw_n+\mathsf Qx_n$ together with
$\mathsf Q\mathsf Qx_n=\mathsf Qx_n$.  This gives the coupled system
\[
w_{n+1}=\mathsf CT\mathsf Pw_n+\mathsf CT\mathsf Q\,\mathsf Qx_n,
\qquad
\mathsf Qx_{n+1}=\mathsf QT\mathsf Q\,\mathsf Qx_n+\mathsf QT\mathsf Pw_n,
\]
where both lines used idempotence of $\mathsf Q$ to write
$\mathsf Q x_n=\mathsf Q(\mathsf Qx_n)$.  Solving the
second line by induction on $n$ gives
\eqref{eq:rg-unresolved-solution}: it is an identity at $n=0$, and applying
$\mathsf QT\mathsf Q$ to the case $n$ and adding
$\mathsf QT\mathsf Pw_n$ reproduces the case $n+1$ with the index shifted.
Substituting \eqref{eq:rg-unresolved-solution} into the first line gives
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-recurrence}. $\square$

\corollaryheading{Autonomy of the resolved recursion}{cor:rg-resolved-autonomy}
Let $\mathcal X_0$ be an admitted initial class.  The resolved recursion is
autonomous on $\mathcal X_0$, meaning $w_{n+1}=\mathsf CT\mathsf Pw_n$ for every
$n\geq0$ and every $x_0\in\mathcal X_0$, if and only if the total correction in
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-recurrence} vanishes for every such $n$ and $x_0$.  Two
sufficient conditions are that all memory kernels
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-operators} vanish and that
\begin{equation}
\mathsf CT\mathsf Q(\mathsf QT\mathsf Q)^{n}\mathsf Qx_0=0,
\qquad n\geq0,
\label{eq:rg-initial-noise-vanishing}
\end{equation}
throughout $\mathcal X_0$.  On the resolved class $\mathsf Qx_0=0$ the family
\eqref{eq:rg-initial-noise-vanishing} is vacuous, and there
$\mathsf QT\mathsf P=0$ is sufficient but not necessary: on
$\mathsf X=\R^2$ with $\mathsf W=\R$, $\mathsf C(x,y)=x$,
$\mathsf P(w)=(w,0)$, and $T(x,y)=(x,x)$, one has
$\mathsf QT\mathsf Pw=(0,w)\neq0$ for $w\neq0$ while
$\mathsf CT\mathsf Q=0$, so every memory kernel vanishes and the resolved
recursion $w_{n+1}=w_n$ is exactly autonomous. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The biconditional is \eqref{eq:rg-memory-recurrence} read as an identity.  The
two displayed families are exactly the two correction sums, so their vanishing
is sufficient.  For the witness, $\mathsf Q(x,y)=(0,y)$ and
$T(0,y)=(0,0)$ give $\mathsf CT\mathsf Q=0$, while
$T(w,0)=(w,w)$ gives $\mathsf QT\mathsf Pw=(0,w)$. $\square$

Recurrence \eqref{eq:rg-memory-recurrence} is proved here for the discrete
linear observable tier.  The projection-operator method of eliminating
unresolved coordinates in favor of a memory kernel and an initial-state term
originates with \citet{Nakajima1958} and \citet{Zwanzig1960}, who work in
continuous time on ensemble densities; those sources are cited as the historical
antecedent of the method and not as proofs of
\eqref{eq:rg-memory-recurrence}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Change of reference is an inhomogeneous law for the action beta}{prop:rg-action-beta-reference-change}
Fix one arrow with rescaled kernel $K_b$ and reverse conditional
$R_\rho(z,dy)$ as in \Cref{thm:rg-effective-action}.  Let $H$ be an action with
$m=e^{-H}\rho$ finite and positive, let $\Delta$ be bounded and measurable with
$\rho(e^{-\Delta})=1$, and set
\begin{equation}
\rho'=e^{-\Delta}\rho,
\qquad
H'=H-\Delta,
\qquad\text{so that}\qquad
e^{-H'}\rho'=e^{-H}\rho=m.
\label{eq:rg-reference-change-pair}
\end{equation}
Then $\rho'$ is again a probability reference for the same measure pair, and
$(\rho'K_b)$-almost everywhere
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_b^H[H';\rho']
=\mathcal R_b^H[H;\rho]-\mathcal R_b^H[\Delta;\rho].
\label{eq:rg-action-reference-change}
\end{equation}
On a declared finite-valued action vector space containing $H$, $\Delta$, and
their images, the discrete beta functional of
\eqref{eq:rg-discrete-beta-functional} therefore obeys the inhomogeneous law
\begin{equation}
\mathfrak B_b^H[H';\rho']
=\mathfrak B_b^H[H;\rho]-\mathfrak B_b^H[\Delta;\rho].
\label{eq:rg-beta-reference-change}
\end{equation}
The added term is not a linear reparameterization of the beta functional, and it
vanishes exactly when the reference-change increment is itself a fixed action,
$\mathcal R_b^H[\Delta;\rho]=\Delta$.  A beta functional is therefore a joint
statement about the action and the declared reference trajectory.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Since $\rho(e^{-\Delta})=1$ and $\Delta$ is bounded, $\rho'$ is a probability
measure equivalent to $\rho$, and \eqref{eq:rg-reference-change-pair} holds by
construction.  Both $\rho K_b$ and $\rho'K_b$ dominate $mK_b$, and the chain
rule for Radon--Nikodym derivatives gives
\[
\frac{d(mK_b)}{d(\rho'K_b)}
=\frac{d(mK_b)}{d(\rho K_b)}
\cdot\left[\frac{d(\rho'K_b)}{d(\rho K_b)}\right]^{-1}
\]
almost everywhere.  By \Cref{thm:rg-effective-action} applied to the pair
$(\rho,e^{-\Delta}\rho)$, the bracketed factor equals
$\int e^{-\Delta(y)}R_\rho(z,dy)=\exp(-\mathcal R_b^H[\Delta;\rho])$, which is
strictly positive and finite because $\Delta$ is bounded.  Taking $-\log$ of the
display and using \eqref{eq:rg-reference-dependent-action-map} twice gives
\eqref{eq:rg-action-reference-change}.  Subtracting
$H'=H-\Delta$, dividing by $\log b$, and regrouping gives
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-reference-change}.  The vanishing criterion is
$\mathcal R_b^H[\Delta;\rho]-\Delta=0$.  $\square$

\definitionheading{Typed discrete interaction beta}{def:rg-interaction-beta}
Let $\mathcal G_\star$ be a declared reference interaction space and let
\begin{equation}
J_\ell:\mathcal G_\star\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\mathcal G_\ell
\label{eq:rg-interaction-trivialization}
\end{equation}
be bounded isomorphisms with bounded inverses.  This is the inverse orientation
of the reference identifications $I_\ell$ of
\eqref{eq:rg-reference-cocycle}, so $J_\ell=I_\ell^{-1}$ whenever both are
declared.  The pulled exact interaction map and the exact discrete interaction
beta are
\begin{equation}
\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}
=J_{\ell+1}^{-1}T_\ell^{\mathcal G}J_\ell:
\mathcal G_\star\longrightarrow\mathcal G_\star,
\qquad
\beta_\ell^{\mathrm{ex}}(g)
=\frac{\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(g)-g}{\Delta s_\ell},
\qquad
\Delta s_\ell=\log b_\ell .
\label{eq:rg-interaction-beta}
\end{equation}
Both terms of the numerator lie in $\mathcal G_\star$; the subtraction
$T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(g)-g$ is not formed, because its two terms inhabit
$\mathcal G_{\ell+1}$ and $\mathcal G_\ell$.  The symbol
$\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}$ is not the retained projection $R_\ell$ of
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-coordinate-residual}, and no beta functional is written
before \eqref{eq:rg-interaction-trivialization} is declared.  If the coupling
object is a manifold rather than a Banach space, the difference must be replaced
by a declared chart difference, logarithm, or retraction into one tangent space.
\status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Exact, retained, and residual interaction beta}{prop:rg-retained-beta-residual}
With $\widehat R_\ell=J_\ell^{-1}R_\ell J_\ell$ for the bounded idempotent
retained projections of \eqref{eq:rg-interaction-coordinate-residual}, define for
$g\in\operatorname{Ran}\widehat R_\ell$
\begin{equation}
\beta_\ell^{\mathrm{ret}}(g)
=\frac{\widehat R_{\ell+1}\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(g)-g}{\Delta s_\ell},
\qquad
\delta\beta_\ell(g)
=\beta_\ell^{\mathrm{ex}}(g)-\beta_\ell^{\mathrm{ret}}(g).
\label{eq:rg-retained-beta}
\end{equation}
Then the omitted part of the flow is exactly the transported coordinate residual
divided by the log block step,
\begin{equation}
\delta\beta_\ell(g)
=\frac{(I-\widehat R_{\ell+1})\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(g)}{\Delta s_\ell}
=\frac{J_{\ell+1}^{-1}r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}(J_\ell g)}{\Delta s_\ell},
\label{eq:rg-beta-residual-identity}
\end{equation}
and its action-quotient partner is
\begin{equation}
\delta\overline\beta_{\ell+1}^{Q}(g)
=\frac{E_{\ell+1}r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}(J_\ell g)}{\Delta s_\ell},
\qquad
\mathsf H_{\ell+1}\delta\overline\beta_{\ell+1}^{Q}(g)
=\frac{r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}(J_\ell g)}{\Delta s_\ell}.
\label{eq:rg-beta-residual-quotient}
\end{equation}
Consequently $\delta\beta_\ell$ vanishes on the whole retained sector if and
only if the exact image of the retained sector is retained,
\begin{equation}
\delta\beta_\ell\big|_{\operatorname{Ran}\widehat R_\ell}=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(\operatorname{Ran}R_\ell)\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}R_{\ell+1},
\label{eq:rg-beta-exactness-criterion}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:rg-interaction-exact-image-invariance}.  Boundedness and
idempotence of $R_\ell$ do not imply this invariance, so a retained beta is
called exact only after \eqref{eq:rg-beta-exactness-criterion} is proved, and
otherwise carries $\delta\beta_\ell$ with the two-sided norm control of
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-residual-norm-control}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Subtracting the two displays in \eqref{eq:rg-retained-beta} cancels $g$ and
leaves $(I-\widehat R_{\ell+1})\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(g)/\Delta s_\ell$.
Substituting $\widehat R_{\ell+1}=J_{\ell+1}^{-1}R_{\ell+1}J_{\ell+1}$ and
$\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}=J_{\ell+1}^{-1}T_\ell^{\mathcal G}J_\ell$ gives
$J_{\ell+1}^{-1}(I-R_{\ell+1})T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(J_\ell g)/\Delta s_\ell$, which
is \eqref{eq:rg-beta-residual-identity} by the definition of
$r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}$.  Applying $E_{\ell+1}$ and
$\mathsf H_{\ell+1}$ and using the
inverse identities of \Cref{thm:rg-hoeffding-action-isomorphism} together with
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-action-residual} gives
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-residual-quotient}.  Since $J_{\ell+1}^{-1}$ is injective and
$\Delta s_\ell>0$, $\delta\beta_\ell(g)=0$ is equivalent to
$r_{\ell+1}^{\mathcal G}(J_\ell g)=0$, and $J_\ell$ maps
$\operatorname{Ran}\widehat R_\ell$ onto $\operatorname{Ran}R_\ell$; quantifying
over that sector and using
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-exact-image-invariance} gives
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-exactness-criterion}.  $\square$

A projected fixed point is not an exact fixed point.  On
$\mathcal G_\star=\R^2$ with $b=e$, take $R(x,y)=(x,0)$ and $T(x,y)=(x,x)$.  For
every retained input $g=(x,0)$ one has $\beta^{\mathrm{ret}}(g)=(0,0)$ while
$\beta^{\mathrm{ex}}(g)=(0,x)$, so the retained flow reports a line of fixed
points although the exact step generates an omitted component whenever
$x\neq0$. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The interaction beta is also scheme dependent, and its scheme dependence is
larger than the coupling reparameterization of
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-change}.  If $J_\ell'=J_\ell S_\ell$ for bounded isomorphisms
$S_\ell$ of $\mathcal G_\star$, then
$\widehat T_\ell'=S_{\ell+1}^{-1}\widehat T_\ell S_\ell$ and
\begin{equation}
\beta_\ell'(g)
=\frac{S_{\ell+1}^{-1}\widehat T_\ell^{\mathcal G}(S_\ell g)-g}{\Delta s_\ell},
\label{eq:rg-interaction-beta-scheme-change}
\end{equation}
which reduces to the usual pushforward relation only for a scale-independent
$S_\ell\equiv S$.  Even the identity native step acquires a nonzero beta in a
moving comparison frame: on $\mathcal G_\ell=\R$ with $T_\ell=\operatorname{id}$,
$b_\ell=e$, and $J_\ell u=a_\ell u$, formula \eqref{eq:rg-interaction-beta} gives
$\beta_\ell(g)=(a_\ell/a_{\ell+1}-1)g$, which is zero for $a_\ell\equiv1$ and
nonzero otherwise.  The comparison scheme is therefore part of the statement of
any reported beta function. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\definitionheading{Smooth scale bundle, scale connection, and continuous beta}{def:rg-scale-connection}
A continuous beta function is a construction on a separately declared tier and
is not obtained from a discrete sequence.  Declare an oriented one-dimensional
$C^1$ scale manifold $S$ with coordinate $s$, a $C^1$ Banach vector bundle
$\pi_S:\mathscr G\to S$ whose fibers are the coupling spaces, and a scale
connection, that is a covariant derivative
$\nabla^{\mathrm{scale}}$ on sections of $\mathscr G$ satisfying the usual
linearity and Leibniz rules.  For a $C^1$ running coupling section
$g(s)\in\mathscr G_s$, the continuous beta is
\begin{equation}
\beta^{\mathrm{scale}}(s)=\nabla^{\mathrm{scale}}_{\partial_s}g(s)
\in\mathscr G_s .
\label{eq:rg-covariant-scale-beta}
\end{equation}
In a local trivialization $I_s:\mathscr G_s\to\mathcal G_\star$ with connection
form $A_s\in\mathcal L(\mathcal G_\star)$ and $\widetilde g(s)=I_sg(s)$,
\begin{equation}
I_s\beta^{\mathrm{scale}}(s)
=\partial_s\widetilde g(s)+A_s\widetilde g(s),
\qquad
A'_s=S_sA_sS_s^{-1}-(\partial_sS_s)S_s^{-1}
\ \text{ under }I'_s=S_sI_s .
\label{eq:rg-scale-connection-form}
\end{equation}
A raw coordinate derivative does not obey that transformation law.  On the
trivial line bundle with $g(s)=1$ parallel in a frame with $A_s=0$, the frame
change $S_s=e^{s^2}$ gives raw derivative $2se^{s^2}\neq0$ while
$A_s'=-2s$ and the covariant derivative
$\partial_s(e^{s^2})-2se^{s^2}$ is zero.
The scale connection is a different object from the two contextual principal
connections $\omega_b,\omega_m$ of \Cref{def:rg-geometric-state}: the former has
base $S$ and compares coupling fibers at different resolutions, the latter have
base $\mathcal C_\ell$ at one fixed resolution and compare associated
statistical fibers along directions in $T\mathcal C_\ell$.  It is also distinct
from the inference-orbit parameter and the Fisher duration of
\Cref{ch:relational-inference}.  Nothing is proved by this declaration.
\status{DEFINITION}

One realization of that tier is the following density calculation.
Suppose that scale is a genuine continuous semigroup and that, relative
to one common dominating measure, the positive densities
$r_t,p_t\in\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal A^*)$ are differentiable in a topology
that permits pointwise ratios and the following dual pairings, with
$p_t=r_t\exp[-H_t]$, $\dot r_t=\mathcal A^*r_t$, and
$\dot p_t=\mathcal A^*p_t$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\dot H_t
=-\frac{\mathcal A^*(r_t\exp[-H_t])}
{r_t\exp[-H_t]}
+\frac{\mathcal A^*r_t}{r_t}}
\label{eq:rg-continuous-beta-functional}
\end{equation}
almost everywhere wherever the displayed ratios and their dual pairings are
defined.  If the reference is stationary, the second term vanishes.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Differentiate $H_t=-\log(p_t/r_t)$ and substitute the two forward equations.
$\square$

On a declared Banach action space with a Schauder basis
$\{\psi_A\}$ and continuous coordinate functionals
$\{\psi_A^*\}$, or a Hilbert space with a Riesz basis, suppose the expansion
$H_t=\sum_Ag_A(t)\psi_A$ converges and differentiation and coefficient
extraction can be interchanged.  The component beta functions are then the
coefficients of the right side of \eqref{eq:rg-continuous-beta-functional}:
\begin{equation}
\dot g_A=\left\langle\psi_A^*,
-\frac{\mathcal A^*(r_te^{-H_t})}{r_te^{-H_t}}
+\frac{\mathcal A^*r_t}{r_t}\right\rangle.
\label{eq:rg-component-beta}
\end{equation}
This is exact for the stated complete convergent basis.  A truncated basis
defines a projection and leaves an explicit residual.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Discrete endpoints do not determine a continuous beta}{prop:rg-continuous-beta-underdetermined}
Let a scale evolution family on the tier of
\Cref{def:rg-scale-connection} consist of maps
\begin{equation}
\mathsf V(s,t):\mathscr G_t\longrightarrow\mathscr G_s,
\qquad
\mathsf V(r,s)\circ\mathsf V(s,t)=\mathsf V(r,t),
\qquad
\mathsf V(t,t)=I_{\mathscr G_t},
\label{eq:rg-scale-evolution-family}
\end{equation}
which is the continuous analogue of \eqref{eq:rg-cocycle-composition} and is
again two-parameter.  Restriction to integer endpoints does not determine the
family, hence does not determine
\eqref{eq:rg-covariant-scale-beta}.  On $S=\R$ with the trivial line bundle and
zero connection form, the two families
\begin{equation}
\mathsf V^{(0)}(s,t)=1,
\qquad
\mathsf V^{(\epsilon)}(s,t)
=\exp\bigl\{\epsilon[\sin(2\pi s)-\sin(2\pi t)]\bigr\}
\label{eq:rg-scale-interpolation-witness}
\end{equation}
both satisfy \eqref{eq:rg-scale-evolution-family} and agree at every pair of
integer endpoints, while their generators are $0$ and
$2\pi\epsilon\cos(2\pi s)$.  The one-parameter semigroup form, in which
$\mathsf V(s,t)$ depends only on $s-t$, additionally requires a stationary
trivialization, a stationary connection, an autonomous vertical field, and a
global existence domain; it is not a consequence of
\eqref{eq:rg-scale-evolution-family}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Both displayed families satisfy the composition and identity laws by inspection,
because each is of the form $f(s)/f(t)$ with $f$ positive.  At integer $s$ and
$t$ the sine terms vanish, so
$\mathsf V^{(\epsilon)}(s,t)=1=\mathsf V^{(0)}(s,t)$.
Differentiating $\mathsf V^{(\epsilon)}(s,t)$ in $s$ at $s=t$ gives
$2\pi\epsilon\cos(2\pi t)$, which is not identically zero for
$\epsilon\neq0$. $\square$

The smooth scale base, the bundle, the interpolation, and the connection are
therefore additional declared input.  A discrete blocking sequence supplies
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-beta} and nothing more. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Attention flows primarily through the joint event law.  Along a differentiable

\label{eq:rg-attention-beta-event}
\end{equation}
For a differentiable single softmax row on a fixed strictly positive prior

At a support change or on $\{\alpha_I=0\}$, the joint law $\eta$ is the

\theoremheading{Exhaustive fixed-point equations}{thm:rg-fixed-point-equations}
A normalized fixed theory is exactly a rescaled invariant pair
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_b(\rho_*,m_*)=(\rho_*,m_*)
\quad\text{for every declared }b.
\label{eq:rg-fixed-measure-pair}
\end{equation}
On the dominated normalized tier this is equivalent to the conjunction
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_b^\rho\rho_*=\rho_*,
\qquad
\mathcal R_b^H[H_*;\rho_*]=H_*,
\label{eq:rg-fixed-action}
\end{equation}
almost everywhere with respect to the fixed reference.  If the evidence mass
is discarded and actions are considered only projectively, the weaker
fixed-ray equation is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R_b^H[H_*;\rho_*]=H_*+c_{\mathrm{ray}}(b).
\label{eq:rg-fixed-action-ray}
\end{equation}
It represents the normalized pair in \eqref{eq:rg-fixed-measure-pair} only when
the reference is also invariant and normalization fixes
$c_{\mathrm{ray}}(b)=0$.  A fixed
attention law is a fixed rescaled event measure $\eta_*$.  A fixed conditional
row alone does not make the attention law fixed: $\eta$ is fixed exactly when
its receiver marginal $\alpha$ is fixed and its disintegrated row $\beta$ is
fixed $\alpha$-almost everywhere.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The first equation is the definition of invariance on the common rescaled
measure space.  Invariance of its first component and taking the
Radon--Nikodym derivative of its second component give the two conditions in
\eqref{eq:rg-fixed-action}.  Quotienting by the action's additive gauge gives
\eqref{eq:rg-fixed-action-ray}, but restoring the tracked evidence mass removes
that freedom.  Disintegration of a fixed $\eta$ gives the final statement.
$\square$

At a fixed pair, additionally require $H_*$ to lie in the declared
finite-valued action Banach space.  Set
\begin{equation}
m_*(dy)=e^{-H_*(y)}\rho_*(dy),\qquad
M_*=m_*(\mathsf Y),\qquad
\pi_*=M_*^{-1}m_*,\qquad
\pi_*^c=\pi_*K_b,
\label{eq:rg-fixed-normalized-reverse-data}
\end{equation}
and fix the standard-Borel reverse-kernel version \(\Pi_*\) defined by
\begin{equation}
\pi_*(dy)K_b(y,dz)=\pi_*^c(dz)\Pi_*(z,dy).
\label{eq:rg-fixed-normalized-reverse-kernel}
\end{equation}
Also require
\begin{equation}
0<L_*^c(z)=\E_{\rho_*}[e^{-H_*}\given Z=z]<\infty
\quad \rho_*^c\text{-almost everywhere}.
\label{eq:rg-linearization-positive-likelihood}
\end{equation}
For bounded perturbations
$\varphi\in L^\infty(\pi_*)$, the first two variations of one exact
conditional-partition step are, \(\pi_*^c\)-almost everywhere,
\begin{equation}
D_H\mathcal R_b^H\big|_{(H_*,\rho_*)}[\varphi](z)
=\E_{\Pi_*(dy\given z)}\varphi(Y),
\qquad
D_H^2\mathcal R_b^H\big|_{(H_*,\rho_*)}[\varphi,\varphi](z)
=-\operatorname{Var}_{\Pi_*(dy\given z)}\varphi(Y),
\label{eq:rg-linearized-action}
\end{equation}
for the composite kernel \(K_b=C_bI_b\), which already includes the declared
rescaling.  The bounded-chart derivation of these two variations is
\Cref{thm:rg-bounded-action-calculus}; the displayed
conditional-expectation and covariance formulas are exact on that stated
domain. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\definitionheading{Typed invariant and fixed objects}{def:rg-typed-fixed-objects}

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\widehat F_\ell^{(\tau)}
=J_{\ell+1}^{-1}\circ F_\ell^{(\tau)}\circ J_\ell
:Y_\star^{(\tau)}\longrightarrow Y_\star^{(\tau)}.
\label{eq:rg-reference-step}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\widehat F_\ell^{(\tau)}(y_\star)=y_\star
\qquad\text{for every }\ell,
\label{eq:rg-reference-fixed-object}
\end{equation}

\widehat{\mathcal M}_\ell^{(\tau)}
=\widehat F_{\ell+p-1}^{(\tau)}\circ\cdots\circ
\widehat F_\ell^{(\tau)}
:Y_\star^{(\tau)}\longrightarrow Y_\star^{(\tau)},
\label{eq:rg-monodromy-map}

and a \emph{reference monodromy fixed object} satisfies

\[
y_{\star,j+1}
=\widehat F_{\ell+j}^{(\tau)}(y_{\star,j})
\quad(0\leq j<p),
\qquad
y_{\star,p}=y_{\star,0},
\]
entirely in \(Y_\star^{(\tau)}\); its points need not be fixed by any
one-step reference map.

\propositionheading{Fixedness does not transfer between tiers}{prop:rg-fixed-object-nonimplication}
None of the following implications holds, and each is refuted by a finite
witness.  A fixed normalized law does not give a fixed reference-dependent
action: on a two-point set hold $\mu=(1/2,1/2)$ fixed while the reference
alternates between $\rho_0=(1/4,3/4)$ and $\rho_1=(3/4,1/4)$, so that the
actions $H_i=-\log(d\mu/d\rho_i)$ are interchanged.  A fixed action class does
not give a fixed tracked pair: $H$ and $H+c$ are the same class, while
$e^{-H}\rho$ and $e^{-c}e^{-H}\rho$ have evidence masses differing by the factor
$e^{-c}$, which is why \eqref{eq:rg-fixed-action-ray} is strictly weaker than
\eqref{eq:rg-fixed-measure-pair} until mass preservation forces
$c_{\mathrm{ray}}(b)=0$.  A
fixed retained interaction does not give a fixed exact interaction: the witness
$R(x,y)=(x,0)$ and $T(x,y)=(x,x)$ of
\Cref{prop:rg-retained-beta-residual} has zero retained beta at every $(x,0)$
and nonzero exact residual for $x\neq0$.  A fixed law does not give a fixed
configuration: take the configuration manifold $S^1$ with antipodal step
$q\mapsto-q$ and a constant configuration-to-law extraction, so that the law is
fixed while the configuration step has no fixed point.  A fixed conditional
attention row does not give a fixed event law: holding $\beta$ fixed while the
receiver occupancy $\alpha_\ell$ alternates makes
$\eta_\ell=\alpha_\ell\beta$ alternate, which is the disintegration clause of
\Cref{thm:rg-fixed-point-equations}.  Finally a monodromy object is not a
one-step fixed object: the period-two maps $F_0(x)=x+1$ and $F_1(x)=x-1$ on $\R$
have monodromy $F_1\circ F_0=\operatorname{id}$, which fixes every point, while
neither one-step map has a fixed point. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Each clause is the displayed computation.  For the first,
$d\mu/d\rho_0=(2,2/3)$ and $d\mu/d\rho_1=(2/3,2)$ are distinct.  For the second,
$(e^{-c}e^{-H}\rho)(\mathsf Y)=e^{-c}m(\mathsf Y)$.  The third is
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-residual-identity} evaluated at $g=(x,0)$, which gives
$(0,x)$.  For the fourth, a constant extraction sends both $q$ and $-q$ to the
same law, while $q\mapsto-q$ has no fixed point on $S^1$.  The fifth is
$\eta_\ell=\alpha_\ell\beta$ with $\alpha_\ell$ nonconstant in $\ell$.  For the
sixth, $F_1(F_0(x))=x$ while $F_0(x)=x$ and $F_1(x)=x$ have no
solutions. $\square$

Two prohibitions follow.  First, relevance, marginality, and irrelevance are
properties of a mode relative to declared norms, a declared extensive
normalization, the declared block ratios, and the declared comparison maps, in
the sense of \Cref{def:rg-mode-exponents} and
\Cref{def:rg-hermite-relevance}; \Cref{prop:rg-superexponential-distortion} and
\Cref{cor:rg-interaction-tempered} show that changing the comparison data alone
can change the reported exponent.  Second, none of the objects above is a
physical law.  A fixed measure pair, a fixed action class, a fixed interaction
coordinate, a monodromy object, and a fixed configuration are mathematical
invariants of a declared scale diagram; identifying any of them with an
empirical regularity requires the separate apparatus listed at
\Cref{claim:physical-law-identification}, which this document does not supply.
\status{NOT-CLAIMED}

The equations characterize all fixed points without claiming that every model
class admits a closed-form enumeration.  The following exact sectors illustrate
them.  The identity channel fixes every law.  A terminal channel followed by
its one-point identification has the constant infrared fixed theory.  For
independent additive vector states and the block statistic
$b^{-1/\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^bY_i$, every strictly $\alpha$-stable baseline
with constant likelihood $m_o=Z(o)\rho$ gives a fixed measure pair; the
centered Gaussian is the $\alpha=2$ case.  At one fixed integer $b$ this
list is not exhaustive because $b$-semistable laws can also be fixed.
Exhaustiveness by strict stability requires invariance over all declared block
sizes (or a continuum of scales) with the usual centering and regularity
hypotheses.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

Flat graph connection data form an invariant sector under rooted contraction,
but become a fixed connection only after a self-similar graph identification
has been declared.  Uniform attention is fixed-form only when receiver
occupancies, block sizes, source labels, and self-edge conventions are also
self-similar.  One-hot rows are invariant boundary sectors of
\eqref{eq:rg-attention-beta-softmax}.  These qualifications prevent an
invariant form from being mislabeled as a complete fixed theory.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Along every exact coarse path, \eqref{eq:rg-vfe-chain-rule} makes the recognition
gap nonincreasing and the ELBO nondecreasing at fixed evidence.  This monotone
flow is information loss under resolution, not a proof of approach to a
nontrivial critical fixed point.  Attraction requires spectral control of
\eqref{eq:rg-linearized-action} on the declared common space.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\section[Closure theorem]{Closure theorem and infinite extensions}
\label{sec:rg-closure-theorem}

\theoremheading{Complete finite law-level gauge-VFE theory}{thm:rg-complete-effective-theory}
For every finite standard-Borel agent hypergraph, suppose the model supplies
the common principal bundle and its two associated statistical bundles;
normalized gauge-covariant baseline and interaction-record kernels with
positive finite evidence; channel-specific incidence and boundary links
$U^x,\Theta^x$ obeying
$U_{a\leftarrow i}^{x\prime}=(a_a^x)^{-1}U_{a\leftarrow i}^xa_i^x$
and the separate belief and model representations; normalized recognition laws
satisfying the stated absolute-continuity and log-integrability conditions;
structural coarse channels that are recognition independent and gauge
equivariant; globally gauge-equivariant jointly measurable versions of every
disintegration used for the displayed bridge kernels; pushed probability
references; component-rooted forests together with their simultaneous raw
root-framed holonomy maps, root features, and dressed boundary generators;
joint marked attention-event laws; every
induced effective hyperedge; full path laws or their exact memory kernels; and
rescaling kernels satisfying
\eqref{eq:rg-kernel-compatibility}.  Then the global and local ELBOs,
agent--meta bridge kernels, meta-agent interaction kernels, meta-attention,
effective likelihoods, measure-pair scale composition, and measure-pair fixed
equations defined in this and the preceding chapter are simultaneously
normalized, gauge covariant, and exact.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Normalization and the collective/local identities are
\Cref{prop:obs-interaction-normalization,thm:obs-collective-vfe,thm:obs-local-global-potential}.
The common-pushforward chain rule is \Cref{thm:rg-exact-coarse-vfe}; the
conditional-partition and tower identities are \Cref{thm:rg-effective-action}.
Mobius inversion or the retained top-order factor closes the hypergraph.
Regular disintegration gives an almost-everywhere version family of bridge
kernels, and the supplied equivariant-version hypothesis selects the displayed
globally covariant versions.  Rooted dressing gives the
separate gauge-covariant channel maps while the full holonomy representation
preserves the information lost by a tree presentation.  Pushforward and
disintegration of $\eta$ give exact attention.  Path pushforward supplies
dynamic closure, and the explicit common-space kernel identity types and
composes the measure-pair RG.  Each operation is closed in the stated
collection, so their finite composition is closed.  $\square$

The rescaling-kernel hypothesis deserves one caution the other hypotheses do
not need.  Every other premise is satisfied by the declared finite
constructions of this and the preceding chapter, but rescaling kernels
satisfying \eqref{eq:rg-kernel-compatibility} are, as of 2026-08-17, not
exhibited: the first declared rescaling construction fails the compatibility
law at order one on its pre-registered criterion, as recorded after
\eqref{eq:rg-kernel-compatibility}.  The theorem is unaffected as a
conditional statement, and every clause of it except scale composition
applies to the exhibited construction per level pair.  But its
scale-composition clause is currently satisfied by no exhibited instance, and
a reader instantiating the theorem must either supply a kernel family proved
closed under composition, which is a separate open design, or restate the
composed clauses in typed-cocycle form.  \status{NUMERICAL}

\corollaryheading{Exact analytic action, beta, and scaling tier}{cor:rg-complete-analytic-tier}
In addition to the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:rg-complete-effective-theory},
suppose the pushed measures admit the declared common dominators.  On the
linear cross-scale tier, additionally supply the vector features and linear
representations $R_x$, multiplicative weights and inter-root transports in
\eqref{eq:rg-linear-nested-compatibility}, the extra fine cross morphism
$\Phi_f$, and the full marked operator--feature kernels.  These hypotheses
make the fine--meta, meta--fine, meta--meta, cross-channel, and attention
operators exact and compositional on their stated domains; the Hom moments
also require \eqref{eq:rg-attention-bochner-domain}.  For the discrete beta
require a declared reference trajectory and a finite-valued action vector
space closed under the reference-dependent action map; a change of that
reference trajectory then obeys the inhomogeneous law
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-reference-change}.  For the discrete interaction beta require
in addition the comparison isomorphisms
\eqref{eq:rg-interaction-trivialization}, and report the residual
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-residual-identity} unless
\eqref{eq:rg-beta-exactness-criterion} is proved.  For the continuous beta
require the separately declared smooth scale bundle and scale connection of
\Cref{def:rg-scale-connection}, together with the generator-domain,
differentiability, positivity, and dual-pairing hypotheses stated before
\eqref{eq:rg-continuous-beta-functional}.  For component beta functions
require the stated Schauder or Riesz basis and interchange conditions.  For
action fixed points retain invariance of the reference component.  For
linearization require \eqref{eq:rg-linearization-positive-likelihood} and use
the bounded perturbations in $L^\infty(\pi_*)$ stated before
\eqref{eq:rg-linearized-action}.  Then the
effective actions, discrete and continuous beta functionals, component flows,
action fixed-point equations, and linearized scaling operators are exact on
their declared analytic domains.  \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The Radon--Nikodym formula gives the action.  Ordinary subtraction on the
finite action space gives the discrete beta; differentiation in the declared
generator domain gives the continuous beta.  Valid coefficient extraction
gives the component flow.  Invariance of both the reference and likelihood
ratio is equivalent to pair invariance, and dominated differentiation under
the conditional partition gives the first two variations.  $\square$

The theorem is uniform in the number and arrangement of agents; it is not a
verification on one fixed graph size.  For a countable agent or time index,
suppose instead that the normalized finite-cylinder joints form a projectively
consistent family on standard Borel coordinate spaces.  Kolmogorov extension
then supplies the full normalized joint \citep{Kallenberg2021}, and the stated
identities hold for every regular finite-cylinder observation and finite block.
Projective consistency alone does not make one prescribed infinite record
positive probability: an infinite sequence of independent positive-cost
binary successes can still be null.  Conditioning on an infinite record
therefore requires a chosen regular conditional at an almost-everywhere record
or a proved DLR, Doob-transform, or finite-volume posterior limit.  An
infinite-law KL pushforward additionally requires compatible
$Q\ll\Pi$ and an infinite coarse channel.  Existence and uniqueness of a
thermodynamic DLR state, convergence of free-energy densities, and interchange
of volume and RG limits remain the separate open obligations recorded in
\Cref{app:claim-ledger}.  \status{ESTABLISHED}
