\chapter{The Geometric Setting}
\label{ch:geometry}

The ambient geometry has one principal gauge bundle over the contextual base and two associated
statistical bundles, one for belief laws and one for model laws.  The two associated actions of
the same group may be inequivalent and may act on fibers of different dimensions.  Belief and
model frames may nevertheless be chosen separately as local sections of the common principal
bundle, and the two channels may carry different connections.  Cross-channel maps are additional
associated-bundle morphisms; neither a relative frame nor a shared structure group supplies such a
map by itself.  A product-gauge theory with two independent principal bundles is recorded only as
the optional extension in \Cref{sec:geo-product-extension}.

\section{The contextual base}
\label{sec:geo-base}

\definitionheading{Contextual base}{def:geo-context-base}
Let $\mathcal C$ be a finite-dimensional smooth, second countable, Hausdorff manifold whose points
$c$ are called \emph{contexts}. \status{DEFINITION}

This declaration fixes a type and proves nothing.  The base is neither the population index set
$V=\{1,\dots,N\}$ nor its interaction graph, neither latent sample space, and not physical
spacetime.  Its smooth structure supports curves and differential forms.  A connection on a
bundle over $\mathcal C$ defines transport in that bundle along curves in $\mathcal C$; it does
not, unless separately specified as a connection on $T\mathcal C$, define parallel transport of
tangent vectors of the base.  The finite design $D=\{c_a\}_{a=1}^{M}$ used later is a finite
subset of $\mathcal C$, not a continuum limit or a discretization theorem.

Throughout this development, $\mathcal C$ is fixed and timeless.  A curve in $\mathcal C$
orders contexts and does not by itself represent an agent's evolution.  Belief or model change
at a fixed context is a curve inside one associated-bundle fiber, while simultaneous change over
all contexts is a curve in a declared space of sections.  A trajectory whose projection moves in
$\mathcal C$ is a different construction and requires the horizontal--vertical distinction made
by a chosen connection.  These are typing declarations rather than physical conclusions.
\status{DEFINITION}

\section{One principal bundle and two associated statistical bundles}
\label{sec:geo-bundle}
\label{sec:geo-associated}

\definitionheading{Common principal gauge bundle}{def:geo-principal-systems}
Let
\begin{equation}
\pi:\mathscr P_G\longrightarrow\mathcal C
\label{eq:geo-principal-bundle}
\end{equation}
be a smooth principal right $G$-bundle for a Lie group $G$.  For each agent $i$, choose an open
support $\mathcal C_i\subseteq\mathcal C$ and two local principal-frame sections
\begin{equation}
u_i^b:\mathcal C_i\longrightarrow\mathscr P_G,
\qquad
u_i^m:\mathcal C_i\longrightarrow\mathscr P_G.
\label{eq:geo-frame-sections}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

The superscripts distinguish two choices of local section, not two principal bundles.  They may
be unequal.  Because each principal fiber is a right $G$-torsor, there is a unique smooth map
\begin{equation}
h_i:\mathcal C_i\longrightarrow G,
\qquad
u_i^m=u_i^b\cdot h_i,
\label{eq:geo-relative-frame}
\end{equation}
called the \emph{relative principal-frame field}.  Its existence uses only the common principal
bundle and is independent of all associated representations. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Let $(\mathsf K,\mathscr K)$ and $(\mathsf M,\mathscr M)$ be the belief and model sample fibers in
local coordinates, and declare normalized-law fibers
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B_b\subseteq\mathcal P(\mathsf K),
\qquad
\mathcal B_m\subseteq\mathcal P(\mathsf M).
\label{eq:geo-law-fibers}
\end{equation}
A law fiber is not a kernel fiber.  A generative transition belongs to a separately declared
collection of normalized Markov kernels between specified measurable spaces.  No density chart,
common dominating measure, affine structure, or Fisher metric follows from
\eqref{eq:geo-law-fibers}.

Suppose the same group acts measurably on the two sample fibers through representations
\begin{equation}
\rho_b:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf K),
\qquad
\rho_m:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(\mathsf M).
\label{eq:geo-representations}
\end{equation}
The representations need not be equivalent, faithful, or of equal dimension.  Denote their
induced actions on laws by
\begin{equation}
\widehat\rho_b(g)q=(\rho_b(g))_\#q,
\qquad
\widehat\rho_m(g)s=(\rho_m(g))_\#s.
\label{eq:geo-pushforward-actions}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

Thus $(\widehat\rho_b(g)q)(A)=q(\rho_b(g)^{-1}A)$, and similarly in the model channel.  The hats
distinguish actions on laws from actions on samples.  Multiplying a matrix into a
density is not the pushforward in \eqref{eq:geo-pushforward-actions}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\medskip
\hypothesisheading{Selected smooth tier}{hyp:geo-smooth-tier}
When differential statements are made, $\mathcal B_b$ and $\mathcal B_m$ are finite-dimensional
smooth parametrized-measure models, each is closed under its $G$-action, and
\begin{equation}
(g,q)\longmapsto(\rho_b(g))_\#q,
\qquad
(g,s)\longmapsto(\rho_m(g))_\#s
\label{eq:geo-smooth-actions}
\end{equation}
are smooth. \status{HYPOTHESIS}
\medskip

A finite-dimensional statistical interpretation additionally requires differentiability in
quadratic mean, square-integrable scores, and the integrability required by each tensor.  The
Fisher tensor is a Riemannian metric only when it is nondegenerate.  Stratified and
infinite-dimensional fibers require their own categories and are not included silently.

\definitionheading{Belief and model associated bundles}{def:geo-associated-bundles}
On invariant law fibers define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_b=\mathscr P_G\times_{\widehat\rho_b}\mathcal B_b,
\qquad
\mathcal E_m=\mathscr P_G\times_{\widehat\rho_m}\mathcal B_m,
\label{eq:geo-associated-bundles}
\end{equation}
using the quotient convention
\begin{equation}
(u\cdot g,\beta)\sim(u,g\cdot\beta).
\label{eq:geo-quotient-convention}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

At the general tier these are associated measurable bundles.  Under
\Cref{hyp:geo-smooth-tier} they are smooth associated bundles.  Smoothness of $\mathscr P_G$ alone does not
upgrade an arbitrary law fiber.

The field $h_i$ compares the two principal sections, even when $\rho_b$ and $\rho_m$ are
inequivalent.  It then produces two different represented operators,
$\widehat\rho_b(h_i)$ on $\mathcal B_b$ and $\widehat\rho_m(h_i)$ on $\mathcal B_m$.  It does not
canonically produce an operator from $\mathcal B_b$ to $\mathcal B_m$.  If ``frame'' instead means
an arbitrary basis of an associated vector fiber that is not induced from a section of this same
$\mathscr P_G$, the existence of a $G$-valued relative field is not guaranteed.  Moreover, when a
representation is nonfaithful, represented frame data alone determine at most a coset modulo its
kernel; the principal sections, rather than their represented images, are what make $h_i$ unique.

\section{Frame choices, gauge symmetry, and relative frames}
\label{sec:geo-frame-gauge}

Independent changes of the two local trivializations may be written
\begin{equation}
u_i^{b\prime}=u_i^b\cdot a_i,
\qquad
u_i^{m\prime}=u_i^m\cdot b_i,
\qquad a_i,b_i:\mathcal C_i\to G.
\label{eq:geo-local-reframing}
\end{equation}
They change associated coordinates by
$\beta_b'=\widehat\rho_b(a_i)^{-1}\beta_b$ and
$\beta_m'=\widehat\rho_m(b_i)^{-1}\beta_m$.  The unique relative field becomes
\begin{equation}
h_i'=a_i^{-1}h_i b_i.
\label{eq:geo-relative-frame-law}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

These two passive coordinate choices must not be confused with a product gauge symmetry.  The
principal gauge group of the ambient theory is the single group of $G$-equivariant automorphisms
of $\mathscr P_G$ over $\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal C}$, and each such automorphism acts simultaneously on
both associated bundles.  For such an automorphism $F$, define its local functions by
$F(u_i^b)=u_i^b\cdot k_i^b$ and $F(u_i^m)=u_i^m\cdot k_i^m$.  Equivariance and
\eqref{eq:geo-relative-frame} imply
\begin{equation}
k_i^m=h_i^{-1}k_i^b h_i.
\label{eq:geo-diagonal-gauge-functions}
\end{equation}
Thus the two local descriptions of one gauge transformation are related, not independent.  By
contrast, \eqref{eq:geo-local-reframing} simply permits two atlases to be replaced independently.
Applying one principal gauge automorphism to both sections leaves their relative principal element
unchanged, since equivariance gives $F(u_i^m)=F(u_i^b\cdot h_i)=F(u_i^b)\cdot h_i$.

\section{Cross-associated-bundle morphisms}
\label{sec:geo-cross-morphisms}

\definitionheading{Cross morphisms}{def:geo-cross-morphisms}
At the general measurable tier, declare fiber-preserving measurable maps
\begin{equation}
\Phi:\mathcal E_b\longrightarrow\mathcal E_m,
\qquad
\widetilde\Phi:\mathcal E_m\longrightarrow\mathcal E_b,
\qquad
\varpi_m\circ\Phi=\varpi_b,
\quad
\varpi_b\circ\widetilde\Phi=\varpi_m,
\label{eq:geo-cross-channel-map}
\end{equation}
where $\varpi_b$ and $\varpi_m$ are the associated-bundle projections.  Both maps cover
$\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal C}$. \status{DEFINITION}

These maps are extra structure.  They are not principal-bundle morphisms, gauge automorphisms,
same-channel transports, or assumed inverses.  Under \Cref{hyp:geo-smooth-tier}, differential
statements additionally assume that they are smooth.  In the frames $u_i^b,u_i^m$, let
$\phi_i$ and $\widetilde\phi_i$ denote their local fiber representatives.  The rechoices in
\eqref{eq:geo-local-reframing} give
\begin{align}
\phi_i'
&=\widehat\rho_m(b_i)^{-1}\circ\phi_i\circ\widehat\rho_b(a_i),
\label{eq:geo-phi-gauge-law}\\
\widetilde\phi_i'
&=\widehat\rho_b(a_i)^{-1}\circ\widetilde\phi_i\circ\widehat\rho_m(b_i).
\label{eq:geo-tildephi-gauge-law}
\end{align}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Write $\beta_m=\phi_i(\beta_b)$ and substitute
$\beta_b=\widehat\rho_b(a_i)\beta_b'$ and
$\beta_m'=\widehat\rho_m(b_i)^{-1}\beta_m$.  The second law follows by interchanging the
channels. \hfill$\square$

\propositionheading{Equivariant fiber maps induce cross morphisms}{prop:geo-intertwining-cross-map}
Suppose maps $\phi:\mathcal B_b\to\mathcal B_m$ and
$\widetilde\phi:\mathcal B_m\to\mathcal B_b$ satisfy, for every $g\in G$,
\begin{align}
\phi\circ\widehat\rho_b(g)&=\widehat\rho_m(g)\circ\phi,
\qquad
\widetilde\phi\circ\widehat\rho_m(g)=\widehat\rho_b(g)\circ\widetilde\phi.
\label{eq:geo-intertwining}
\end{align}
Then
\begin{equation}
[p,\beta_b]\longmapsto[p,\phi(\beta_b)],
\qquad
[p,\beta_m]\longmapsto[p,\widetilde\phi(\beta_m)]
\label{eq:geo-induced-cross-morphisms}
\end{equation}
are well-defined cross morphisms. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The quotient representatives $(p\cdot g,\beta)$ and $(p,g\cdot\beta)$ have the same image under
\eqref{eq:geo-induced-cross-morphisms} exactly when the corresponding equality in
\eqref{eq:geo-intertwining} holds. \hfill$\square$

Equivariance is sufficient for the representative-independent construction
\eqref{eq:geo-induced-cross-morphisms}.  A general bundle morphism is instead characterized by
local maps satisfying the overlap laws
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\phi_i\circ\widehat\rho_b(T_{ij}^b)
&=\widehat\rho_m(T_{ij}^m)\circ\phi_j,\\
\widetilde\phi_i\circ\widehat\rho_m(T_{ij}^m)
&=\widehat\rho_b(T_{ij}^b)\circ\widetilde\phi_j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:geo-cross-map-gluing}
\end{equation}
In any selected regularity category admitting the two mapping objects and the composition actions
displayed below, their local representatives first require conversion to one principal-frame
atlas.  In the belief atlas define
\begin{equation}
f_i:=\widehat\rho_m(h_i)\circ\phi_i,
\qquad
\widetilde f_i:=\widetilde\phi_i\circ\widehat\rho_m(h_i^{-1}).
\label{eq:geo-map-bundle-frame-conversion}
\end{equation}
Then $f_i$ is the local representative of a section of the associated map bundle with action
$f\mapsto\widehat\rho_m(g)\circ f\circ\widehat\rho_b(g)^{-1}$, and
$\widetilde f_i$ obeys the analogous law with the two channels interchanged.  Indeed,
\eqref{eq:geo-cross-map-gluing} and
$T_{ij}^m=h_i^{-1}T_{ij}^b h_j$ give
$f_i\widehat\rho_b(T_{ij}^b)=\widehat\rho_m(T_{ij}^b)f_j$ and
$\widetilde f_i\widehat\rho_m(T_{ij}^b)=\widehat\rho_b(T_{ij}^b)\widetilde f_j$.
Thus the compatible family is a map-bundle section only after the displayed conversion, and
$h_i$ does not replace the cross-fiber map.  For example, if $\Phi$ is induced by the first map in
\eqref{eq:geo-induced-cross-morphisms}, then
$\phi_i=\widehat\rho_m(h_i^{-1})\circ\phi$ and hence $f_i=\phi$.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Chosen connections, parallel transport, and covariant defects}
\label{sec:geo-three-transports}

\definitionheading{Two connections on the common principal bundle}{def:geo-connections}
Under \Cref{hyp:geo-smooth-tier}, choose
\begin{equation}
\omega_b,\omega_m\in\Omega^1(\mathscr P_G,\mathfrak g)
\label{eq:geo-principal-connections}
\end{equation}
as principal connections on $\mathscr P_G$.  They induce horizontal distributions $H^b,H^m$ on the two
associated bundles and, for every piecewise-smooth $\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathcal C$, parallel
transports
\begin{equation}
\Omega_\gamma:
(\mathcal E_b)_{\gamma(0)}\longrightarrow(\mathcal E_b)_{\gamma(1)},
\qquad
\widetilde\Omega_\gamma:
(\mathcal E_m)_{\gamma(0)}\longrightarrow(\mathcal E_m)_{\gamma(1)}.
\label{eq:geo-base-parallel-transports}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

Explicitly, $\omega_x$ selects the unique horizontal lift of $\gamma$ through a chosen point of
$(\mathscr P_G)_{\gamma(0)}$; carrying the associated-law coordinate along that lift defines the corresponding
map in \eqref{eq:geo-base-parallel-transports}.  This is transport \emph{over} the base curve, not
transport of vectors in $T\mathcal C$.  A tangent-vector transport would require a separately
chosen affine connection on $T\mathcal C$, unless $\mathscr P_G$ and its representation have explicitly been
identified with the frame bundle of $\mathcal C$.

The two connections need not agree.  Their difference $\omega_m-\omega_b$ is horizontal and
$\operatorname{Ad}$-equivariant, hence descends to an
$\operatorname{Ad}(\mathscr P_G)$-valued one-form on $\mathcal C$.  In an associated vector-bundle
realization, the induced horizontal distributions also define covariant derivatives
$\nabla^b,\nabla^m$.  No linear covariant derivative is assumed on a merely measurable law fiber.

Let $A_i^b=(u_i^b)^*\omega_b$ and $A_i^m=(u_i^m)^*\omega_m$.  Under
\eqref{eq:geo-local-reframing},
\begin{align}
A_i^{b\prime}
&=\operatorname{Ad}_{a_i^{-1}}A_i^b+a_i^{-1}da_i,
\label{eq:geo-local-connection-b}\\
A_i^{m\prime}
&=\operatorname{Ad}_{b_i^{-1}}A_i^m+b_i^{-1}db_i.
\label{eq:geo-local-connection-m}
\end{align}
If endpoint coordinates are changed by $a_0,a_1$ in the belief frame and $b_0,b_1$ in the model
frame, the represented transports obey
\begin{align}
\Omega_\gamma'
&=\widehat\rho_b(a_1)^{-1}\circ\Omega_\gamma\circ\widehat\rho_b(a_0),
\label{eq:geo-pt-gauge-b}\\
\widetilde\Omega_\gamma'
&=\widehat\rho_m(b_1)^{-1}\circ\widetilde\Omega_\gamma\circ\widehat\rho_m(b_0).
\label{eq:geo-pt-gauge-m}
\end{align}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

These are the standard connection and endpoint laws for the quotient convention
\eqref{eq:geo-quotient-convention} \citep{Kobayashi1963,Nakahara2003}.  They also show why a
same-point frame comparison and transport along a nonconstant curve are different objects.

\definitionheading{Covariant defects}{def:geo-covariant-defects}
Let $e\in\mathcal E_b$ and $f\in\mathcal E_m$ lie over $c\in\mathcal C$, and let
$X\in T_c\mathcal C$.  For smooth associated fibers, define the vertical-valued tensors
\begin{align}
(\mathcal D\Phi)_e(X)
&=T_e\Phi\bigl(H_e^bX\bigr)-H_{\Phi(e)}^mX,
\label{eq:geo-nonlinear-defect-phi}\\
(\mathcal D\widetilde\Phi)_f(X)
&=T_f\widetilde\Phi\bigl(H_f^mX\bigr)-H_{\widetilde\Phi(f)}^bX.
\label{eq:geo-nonlinear-defect-tildephi}
\end{align}
\status{DEFINITION}

Each difference is vertical because its two terms project to the same $X$.  The maps are parallel
exactly when these defects vanish, equivalently when every base curve satisfies
\begin{align}
\widetilde\Omega_\gamma\circ\Phi_{\gamma(0)}
&=\Phi_{\gamma(1)}\circ\Omega_\gamma,
\label{eq:geo-phi-parallelness}\\
\Omega_\gamma\circ\widetilde\Phi_{\gamma(0)}
&=\widetilde\Phi_{\gamma(1)}\circ\widetilde\Omega_\gamma.
\label{eq:geo-tildephi-parallelness}
\end{align}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
Vanishing defect says that the image of every horizontal lift is horizontal.  Integration along
$\gamma$ gives the two commutative squares, and differentiation at the initial point gives the
converse. \hfill$\square$

Only in a fiberwise-linear associated-vector-bundle specialization do the defects reduce to
\begin{equation}
D\Phi=\nabla^m\circ\Phi-\Phi\circ\nabla^b,
\qquad
D\widetilde\Phi=\nabla^b\circ\widetilde\Phi-\widetilde\Phi\circ\nabla^m.
\label{eq:geo-linear-covariant-defects}
\end{equation}
Write $V_b,V_m$ for the two representation spaces in this linear specialization.  The
old-to-new coordinate maps induced by \eqref{eq:geo-local-reframing} are, by definition,
\begin{equation}
R_i^b:=\rho_b(a_i)^{-1}:V_b\longrightarrow V_b,
\qquad
R_i^m:=\rho_m(b_i)^{-1}:V_m\longrightarrow V_m,
\label{eq:geo-linear-coordinate-maps}
\end{equation}
so that $v_b'=R_i^bv_b$ and $v_m'=R_i^mv_m$.  These are invertible coordinate maps, not
cross-channel morphisms or coarse restriction operators.  In these coordinates the defect
matrices obey
\begin{equation}
(D\Phi)_i'=R_i^m(D\Phi)_i(R_i^b)^{-1},
\qquad
(D\widetilde\Phi)_i'=R_i^b(D\widetilde\Phi)_i(R_i^m)^{-1}.
\label{eq:geo-defect-gauge-laws}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The matrix laws are not asserted for nonlinear law manifolds. The connections
in \eqref{eq:geo-principal-connections} are chosen data; no curvature or
transport is inferred from the agent frames. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\section{Agents and finite-moment pushforward}
\label{sec:geo-agents}

\definitionheading{Agent}{def:geo-agent}
Agent $i$ is the section-bearing object
\begin{equation}
\mathcal A^i=\bigl(\mathcal C_i;q_i,s_i,u_i^b,u_i^m\bigr),
\label{eq:geo-agent-object}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
q_i\in\Gamma(\mathcal C_i,\mathcal E_b|_{\mathcal C_i}),
\qquad
s_i\in\Gamma(\mathcal C_i,\mathcal E_m|_{\mathcal C_i}).
\label{eq:geo-agent-sections}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

The sections are smooth only in the selected smooth tier.  A sample
\begin{equation}
Y(c)=\bigl((k_1(c),m_1(c)),\ldots,(k_N(c),m_N(c))\bigr)
\label{eq:geo-population-sample}
\end{equation}
is a random element of a product of sample fibers, not a tuple of agents.  The continuum sections
above and the finite recognition law introduced later are separately declared objects; their
compatibility is a hypothesis in the probability chapter.

\propositionheading{Finite-second-moment congruence}{prop:geo-moment-pushforward}
Let a belief law $q$ on $\R^K$ have finite mean $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma$, and let
$R=\rho_b(g)$ be invertible and linear for $g\in G$. Then
\begin{equation}
\mu'=R\mu,
\qquad
\Sigma'=R\Sigma R^\top.
\label{eq:geo-moment-pushforward}
\end{equation}
If $\Sigma\succ0$ and $J=\Sigma^{-1}$, the pushed law has precision
\begin{equation}
J'=(\Sigma')^{-1}=R^{-\top}JR^{-1}.
\label{eq:geo-precision-inverse-congruence}
\end{equation}
The model channel has the same statement in its own dimension. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
For $X\sim q$, linearity gives $\mathbb E[RX]=R\mu$ and
$\mathbb E[(RX-R\mu)(RX-R\mu)^\top]=R\Sigma R^\top$.  Inverting the latter identity gives
\eqref{eq:geo-precision-inverse-congruence}. \hfill$\square$

\section{Pointwise frame comparisons}
\label{sec:geo-regime-one}

Fix $c\in\mathcal C_i\cap\mathcal C_j$.  Freeness and transitivity give unique elements of the
same group $G$ such that
\begin{equation}
u_j^b(c)=u_i^b(c)\cdot T_{ij}^b(c),
\qquad
u_j^m(c)=u_i^m(c)\cdot T_{ij}^m(c).
\label{eq:geo-frame-comparison}
\end{equation}
They act through $\rho_b(T_{ij}^b)$ and $\rho_m(T_{ij}^m)$ in the two associated fibers.  Under
\eqref{eq:geo-local-reframing},
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}^{b\prime}=a_i^{-1}T_{ij}^b a_j,
\qquad
T_{ij}^{m\prime}=b_i^{-1}T_{ij}^m b_j.
\label{eq:geo-regime-one-gauge-law}
\end{equation}
Moreover, the relative fields force
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}^m=h_i^{-1}T_{ij}^b h_j.
\label{eq:geo-frame-comparison-relation}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The transformation law follows by substitution and uniqueness in the principal fiber.  The two
expressions
$u_j^m=u_j^b h_j=u_i^bT_{ij}^b h_j$ and
$u_j^m=u_i^mT_{ij}^m=u_i^b h_iT_{ij}^m$ give
\eqref{eq:geo-frame-comparison-relation}. \hfill$\square$

The comparisons are same-point changes of coordinates.  They involve no curve and no connection.
The two group elements are related by \eqref{eq:geo-frame-comparison-relation}, while their
represented actions can be inequivalent.

\section{The \v{C}ech picture and bundle topology}
\label{sec:geo-cech}

Let $\mathcal C_0=\bigcup_i\mathcal C_i$, and let $\mathcal N$ be the nerve of this open cover:
\begin{equation}
\{i_0,\ldots,i_r\}\in\mathcal N
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal C_{i_0\cdots i_r}=\bigcap_{a=0}^r\mathcal C_{i_a}\neq\varnothing.
\label{eq:geo-nerve}
\end{equation}
For either frame atlas $x\in\{b,m\}$, the maps $T_{ij}^x:\mathcal C_{ij}\to G$ obey
\begin{equation}
T_{ii}^x=e_G,
\qquad
T_{ji}^x=(T_{ij}^x)^{-1},
\qquad
T_{ij}^xT_{jl}^x=T_{il}^x.
\label{eq:geo-cech-cocycle}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
On a triple overlap, composing the unique change from $l$ to $j$ with the unique change from $j$
to $i$ gives the unique change from $l$ to $i$. \hfill$\square$

Let $\mathcal G$ be the sheaf of smooth $G$-valued maps.  There is one principal bundle and hence
one nonabelian \v{C}ech class:
\begin{equation}
[T^b]=[T^m]=[\mathscr P_G|_{\mathcal C_0}]
\in\check H^1(\{\mathcal C_i\};\mathcal G).
\label{eq:geo-cech-class}
\end{equation}
Indeed, \eqref{eq:geo-frame-comparison-relation} is exactly the coboundary equivalence between
the two cocycles.  The class is distinguished exactly when $\mathscr P_G|_{\mathcal C_0}$ is trivial,
equivalently when, for either atlas, there are smooth $U_i^x:\mathcal C_i\to G$ such that
\begin{equation}
T_{ij}^x=U_i^x(U_j^x)^{-1}.
\label{eq:geo-coboundary-form}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
If \eqref{eq:geo-coboundary-form} holds, the rechoice $u_i^{x\prime}=u_i^xU_i^x$ makes all
transitions identity, so the sections glue to a global section.  A global section trivializes a
principal bundle.  Conversely, a global trivialization supplies the $U_i^x$.  This is the standard
global-section characterization of a trivial principal bundle
\citep{Steenrod1951,Husemoller1994,Kobayashi1963}. \hfill$\square$

The single principal class does not make the two associated bundles isomorphic.  Inequivalent
representations can send the same principal cocycle to nonisomorphic associated bundles and to
different induced characteristic data.

\medskip
\hypothesisheading{Common principal trivialization}{hyp:geo-common-trivializations}
Whenever global principal-frame coordinates are used, declare $\mathscr P_G|_{\mathcal C_0}$ trivial and
choose one reference section $\sigma_0$ such that
\begin{equation}
u_i^b=\sigma_0\cdot(U_i^b)^{-1},
\qquad
u_i^m=\sigma_0\cdot(U_i^m)^{-1},
\qquad
h_i=U_i^b(U_i^m)^{-1}.
\label{eq:geo-frame-field}
\end{equation}
\status{HYPOTHESIS}
\medskip

This is one topological hypothesis, not two channelwise hypotheses.  The general theory retains
local frames and their common \v{C}ech class.  A global section of an associated bundle does not
in general trivialize $\mathscr P_G$: if an associated fiber contains a $G$-fixed point $\beta_0$, then
$c\mapsto[p,\beta_0]$ is well defined for any $\mathscr P_G$. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Channel-specific graph links and trivializing subfamilies}
\label{sec:geo-regime-two}
\label{sec:geo-trivializing}

\definitionheading{Typed graph links}{def:geo-graph-links}
Let $\mathfrak I=(V,E,\bar{\ })$ be a finite interaction multigraph declared independently of
$\mathcal C$.  Its oriented edge copies have a fixed-point-free reversal involution, and parallel
copies are allowed.  For $e:j\to i$, declare
\begin{equation}
\Theta_e^b,\Theta_e^m\in G,
\qquad
\Theta_{\bar e}^x=(\Theta_e^x)^{-1}.
\label{eq:geo-regime-two-links}
\end{equation}
Under independently changed channel coordinates at the vertices,
\begin{equation}
\Theta_e^{b\prime}=(a_i^b)^{-1}\Theta_e^b a_j^b,
\qquad
\Theta_e^{m\prime}=(a_i^m)^{-1}\Theta_e^m a_j^m.
\label{eq:geo-regime-two-gauge-law}
\end{equation}
Here the $a_i^x\in G$ are passive vertex-coordinate choices.  They are induced by smooth frame
rechoices only after vertices are anchored at contexts $c_i\in\mathcal C_i$, in which case
$a_i^b=a_i(c_i)$ and $a_i^m=b_i(c_i)$.  Allowing two coordinate descriptions of two graph-link
data sets does not enlarge the principal gauge group to $G\times G$.  In the old-to-new
sample-coordinate convention of \Cref{sec:gen-gauge}, the contextual elements denoted there by
$h_i^x$ satisfy $h_i^x=(a_i^x)^{-1}$.  The superscripted $h_i^x$ is a discrete contextual
coordinate re-expression and is unrelated to the relative principal-frame field $h_i$ of
\eqref{eq:geo-relative-frame}. \status{DEFINITION}

The links are channel-specific data.  Neither is determined by the other, by $h_i$, by $\Phi$ or
$\widetilde\Phi$, by pointwise frame comparison, or by a principal connection.  For a closed
edge-labeled walk $\gamma=(e_0,\ldots,e_{r-1})$, where $e_a:i_{a+1}\to i_a$ and $i_r=i_0$, define
\begin{equation}
H^x(\gamma)=\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}\Theta_{e_a}^x.
\label{eq:geo-link-holonomy}
\end{equation}
Its conjugacy class and the condition $H^x(\gamma)=e_G$ are invariant under the corresponding
coordinate changes. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Trivializing criterion}{prop:geo-trivializing-criterion}
Fix a channel $x$, a subfamily $F\subseteq V$, and a $G$-valued comparison assignment $\Xi_e^x$
on its internal oriented edge copies.  The following are equivalent:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\exists V_i^x\in G\ (i\in F)
\quad
\Xi_e^x=V_i^x(V_j^x)^{-1}
\quad\text{for every internal }e:j\to i,\\
\prod_{a=0}^{r-1}\Xi_{e_a}^x=e_G
\quad\text{for every closed internal walk}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:geo-trivializing-criterion}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
The first condition telescopes around every closed walk.  Conversely, choose a base vertex in each
connected component and define $V_i^x$ by an ordered path product.  Two choices differ by a closed
walk and hence agree.  Appending $e:j\to i$ gives the required edge factorization. \hfill$\square$

A subfamily is \emph{jointly trivializing} when the criterion holds separately for both link data
sets.  For $T^x$ evaluated at one common context the criterion is automatic by the \v{C}ech
cocycle law.  Its nontrivial graph content concerns the independently declared $\Theta^x$.
Pointwise graph triviality is weaker than triviality of $\mathscr P_G$, which requires smooth overlap data on
the entire cover.

\medskip
\hypothesisheading{Graph links realized by base transport}{hyp:geo-graph-base-transport}
Choose contexts $c_i\in\mathcal C_i$ and, for every $e:j\to i$, a piecewise-smooth curve
$\gamma_e$ from $c_j$ to $c_i$.  In the chosen endpoint frames impose
\begin{equation}
\widehat\rho_b(\Theta_e^b)=\Omega_{\gamma_e},
\qquad
\widehat\rho_m(\Theta_e^m)=\widetilde\Omega_{\gamma_e}.
\label{eq:geo-link-pt-hypothesis}
\end{equation}
\status{HYPOTHESIS}
\medskip

Without the curve assignment and these equalities, graph holonomy and base-connection holonomy are
unrelated.  Under the hypothesis, represented graph-loop products equal the corresponding
parallel transports around concatenated base loops.  This is a connection-holonomy statement, not
a claim of nontrivial principal-bundle topology.

\section{Flat graph links and residuals}
\label{sec:geo-flatness}

\medskip
\hypothesisheading{Pointwise flat-link specialization}{hyp:geo-flat-links}
At one common context, declare separately for the two link data sets that
\begin{equation}
\Theta_e^b=T_{ij}^b=U_i^b(U_j^b)^{-1},
\qquad
\Theta_e^m=T_{ij}^m=U_i^m(U_j^m)^{-1},
\qquad e:j\to i.
\label{eq:geo-flat-regime}
\end{equation}
\status{HYPOTHESIS}
\medskip

This excludes represented graph holonomy in either channel.  It does not assert that either
principal connection is flat or that the two channel frames coincide.  Let $\mu_i$ be a
belief-channel vector coordinate, set $z_i=\rho_b(U_i^b)^{-1}\mu_i$, and suppress $\rho_b$ only in
the defining representation.  Then
\begin{equation}
\mu_i-\rho_b(\Theta_e^b)\mu_j
=\rho_b(U_i^b)(z_i-z_j).
\label{eq:geo-trivialized-residual}
\end{equation}
For an independent graph link,
\begin{equation}
\mu_i-\rho_b(\Theta_e^b)\mu_j
=\rho_b(U_i^b)(z_i-H_e^bz_j),
\qquad
H_e^b=\rho_b(U_i^b)^{-1}\rho_b(\Theta_e^b)\rho_b(U_j^b).
\label{eq:geo-regime-two-residual}
\end{equation}
More generally, if $\Theta_e^b=V_i^b(V_j^b)^{-1}$ on a belief-trivializing subfamily and
$z_i=\rho_b(V_i^b)^{-1}\mu_i$, then
\begin{equation}
\mu_i-\rho_b(\Theta_e^b)\mu_j
=\rho_b(V_i^b)(z_i-z_j).
\label{eq:geo-subfamily-trivialized-residual}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

These identities follow by substitution.  Belief-link trivialization says nothing about model
links, either cross morphism, or equality of the belief and model frames.

\section{Common-frame and common-connection specializations}
\label{sec:geo-diagonal}

The one-principal-bundle construction is already the ambient theory.  A \emph{common-frame}
specialization makes the stronger declaration
\begin{equation}
u_i^b=u_i^m=u_i,
\qquad h_i=e_G,
\qquad T_{ij}^b=T_{ij}^m=T_{ij}.
\label{eq:geo-diagonal-principal}
\end{equation}
Even then the represented frame changes generally differ:
choose a common old-to-new sample-coordinate re-expression $g_i:\mathcal C_i\to G$, so that the
corresponding principal-section rechoice is $u_i'=u_i\cdot g_i^{-1}$.  Then
\begin{equation}
R_i^b=\rho_b(g_i),
\qquad
R_i^m=\rho_m(g_i).
\label{eq:geo-represented-frame-change}
\end{equation}
Thus $R_i^b$ and $R_i^m$ have the same principal source $g_i$ but act within different
representation spaces; neither is a map between the belief and model fibers.
An independent \emph{equal-connection} specialization declares $\omega_b=\omega_m=\omega$.  The
underlying principal holonomy is then common, while its actions on belief and model laws remain
$\widehat\rho_b$ and $\widehat\rho_m$.  A further common-link specialization declares
$\Theta_e^b=\Theta_e^m=\Theta_e\in G$.  None of these three stronger hypotheses follows from the
others. \status{HYPOTHESIS}

The cross maps remain extra equivariant structure governed by
\eqref{eq:geo-intertwining}; they are not forced to be inverses or parallel.  Thus
inequivalent representations do not obstruct a common principal frame.  In a linear
associated-vector-bundle specialization they may obstruct a nonzero sample-fiber intertwiner.
Law-level equivariance is weaker: an equivariant Markov kernel may induce an equivariant map on
laws without supplying a linear map between the sample fibers.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Optional product-gauge extension}
\label{sec:geo-product-extension}

If the application genuinely requires independently nonisomorphic principal bundles or two
independent gauge symmetries, replace the ambient $\mathscr P_G$ by
\begin{equation}
\pi_b:P_b\to\mathcal C,
\qquad
\pi_m:P_m\to\mathcal C,
\qquad
Q=P_b\times_{\mathcal C}P_m,
\label{eq:geo-product-principal-extension}
\end{equation}
where $Q$ is a principal $G_b\times G_m$-bundle.  The belief and model associated bundles are then
formed using the two projections. \status{DEFINITION}

There is no canonical relative field valued in one common group between frames in
$P_b$ and $P_m$ without additional comparison data.  When $G_b=G_m=G$, sufficient principal-level
data are a chosen principal $G$-bundle isomorphism
$B:P_b\to P_m$ over $\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal C}$ satisfying
$B(p\cdot g)=B(p)\cdot g$.  For possibly different groups, the corresponding declared data are a
homomorphism $\kappa:G_b\to G_m$ and a bundle map $B:P_b\to P_m$ over
$\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal C}$ satisfying
$B(p\cdot g_b)=B(p)\cdot\kappa(g_b)$.  The latter data alone do not create a canonical relative
field valued in one common group: no such common group or inverse identification has been declared.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

In a category with the required mapping object, a belief-to-model morphism is a section of
\begin{equation}
Q\times_{G_b\times G_m}\operatorname{Map}(\mathcal B_b,\mathcal B_m),
\qquad
(g_b,g_m)\cdot f
=\widehat\rho_m(g_m)\circ f\circ\widehat\rho_b(g_b)^{-1}.
\label{eq:geo-product-cross-map-bundle}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

A constant fiber map descends only if it is fixed by every independent pair.  Given the
$\kappa$-equivariant map $B$ above, a fiber map
$\phi:\mathcal B_b\to\mathcal B_m$ satisfying
$\phi\circ\widehat\rho_b(g_b)
=\widehat\rho_m(\kappa(g_b))\circ\phi$ induces a compatible cross morphism.
These principal and fiber data are sufficient, but a general mapping-bundle section need not arise
that way.  Thus the product bundle supplies neither a relative field nor either cross morphism
automatically.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

This extension carries additional topology and an independent product gauge freedom.  It should
therefore be invoked only when those degrees of freedom are intended, not merely because belief
and model use different associated representations of one group.
