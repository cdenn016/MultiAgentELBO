\chapter{Multivariate-Gaussian Coarse-Graining}
\label{ch:gaussian-coarsegraining}

This chapter realizes the three operations of
\Cref{ch:coarsegraining} in the multivariate-Gaussian interaction family. It
does not repeat the Markov or graph-exponential theory. Its claims concern
hard identification, Gaussian marginalization and recognition restrictions,
all at finite dimension. Unless a channel index is displayed, the symbols
\(K,\Theta,\Lambda,L,A,W\) in this chapter denote the belief/recognition
channel. A model-channel realization is separate and uses its own
\(K_m,\Theta^m,\Lambda_m,L_m,A_m,W_m\); no equality of the two channels is
implied. \status{DEFINITION}

Within the connection-Laplacian realization,
\Cref{prop:cg-kernel-holonomy} applies only to invertible represented
transports with inverse reverse transports and positive-definite represented
internal weights. Under those hypotheses, trivial represented internal
holonomy is the criterion for a connected cluster to retain a full
\(K\)-dimensional, state-independent structural fixed section; nontrivial
holonomy may still leave a lower-dimensional fixed sector.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Marginal similarity is not identified with this structural criterion.
\status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\section{Hard aggregation of the interaction precision}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-aggregation}

Assume the declared interaction family of \Cref{ch:gaussian},
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_{ii}=A_i+\sum_{j\neq i}W_{ij},\qquad
\Lambda_{ij}=-W_{ij}\quad(i\neq j),
\qquad
A_i\succeq0,\quad W_{ij}=W_{ji}\succeq0.
\label{eq:cg-interaction-family}
\end{equation}
In flat trivialized coordinates its energy is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E(z)
=\sum_i z_i^\top A_i z_i
+\sum_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^\top W_{ij}(z_i-z_j).
\label{eq:cg-trivialized-energy}
\end{equation}
\status{HYPOTHESIS}

This form is a Gaussian subfamily, not a consequence of an arbitrary
linear-Gaussian directed model. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

For a hard partition \(\mathcal P\), let
\[
S=\widehat S\otimes I_K,\qquad
\widehat S_{iI}=\mathbf1_{\{i\in I\}},\qquad
\Lambda_{\rm c}=S^\top\Lambda S.
\]
The columns of \(S\) are orthogonal but not normalized:
\begin{equation}
S^\top S=\operatorname{diag}(n_I)\otimes I_K.
\label{eq:cg-aggregation-matrix}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Exact Gaussian aggregation}{prop:cg-gaussian-aggregation}
For every hard partition,
\begin{equation}
(\Lambda_{\rm c})_{IJ}
=-\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\j\in J}}W_{ij}\quad(I\neq J),
\qquad
(\Lambda_{\rm c})_{II}
=\sum_{i\in I}A_i
+\sum_{\substack{i\in I\\j\notin I}}W_{ij}.
\label{eq:cg-coarse-blocks}
\end{equation}
Thus \(A_I=\sum_{i\in I}A_i\),
\(W_{IJ}=\sum_{i\in I,j\in J}W_{ij}\), and every internal edge is
annihilated. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Substitute one coordinate \(z_I\) for every \(z_i\) in a cluster. An internal
edge contributes zero. A cut edge contributes its difference energy between
\(z_I\) and \(z_J\), and self terms add. Finite sums preserve positive
semidefiniteness; independently,
\(\Lambda_{\rm c}\succeq0\) follows by congruence. \(\square\)

This is the Galerkin coarse operator with a piecewise-constant prolongator,
not a new numerical construction \citep{VanekMandelBrezina1996,Lee2024AMGSystems}.
The parameter map is exact for hard identification. A separately fitted
coupling is outside that exact trace construction, while learned or soft
pooling is a different approximate model. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\section{Kron reduction: a counterexample and the closed cone}
\label{sec:cg-kron}

Gaussian marginalization eliminates nodes by a Schur complement. It is not
the aggregation above and is not closed on the unrestricted matrix-weighted
interaction family.

\propositionheading{Unrestricted Kron reduction leaves the family}{prop:cg-kron-leaves-family}
Take \(N=3\), \(K=2\), \(A_1=A_2=A_3=I_2\), and
\begin{equation}
W_{12}=0,\qquad
W_{13}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
W_{23}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:cg-kron-witness}
\end{equation}
Then the assembled \(\Lambda\) is positive definite. Eliminating node \(3\)
manufactures
\begin{equation}
-\left(\Lambda^{\rm Kron}\right)_{12}
=W_{13}\Lambda_{33}^{-1}W_{23}
=\frac1{19}\begin{pmatrix}9&3\\4&14\end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:cg-kron-asymmetric}
\end{equation}
which is not symmetric and hence is not a positive-semidefinite interaction
weight. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
\(\Lambda_{33}=\begin{psmallmatrix}4&1\\1&5\end{psmallmatrix}\) has
determinant \(19\), and direct multiplication gives
\eqref{eq:cg-kron-asymmetric}. The leading principal minors of the full
precision are \(2,6,18,48,102,233\), so Sylvester's criterion gives
\(\Lambda\succ0\). The full Schur complement is symmetric; its two mirrored
off-diagonal blocks are transposes. Membership in
\eqref{eq:cg-interaction-family} additionally requires each individual block
to be symmetric, which fails. \(\square\)

The scalar loopy-Laplacian family is closed under every defined elimination
\citep{DorflerBullo2013Kron}. The matrix analog is closed on a larger
subfamily than common orthogonal diagonalization.

\definitionheading{Congruence-diagonal cone}{def:cg-congruence-diagonal-cone}
For \(H\in\GL(K)\), define
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C_H
=\{HDH^\top:D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_K),\ d_a\geq0\}.
\label{eq:cg-congruence-diagonal-cone}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

\theoremheading{Congruence-diagonal Kron closure}{thm:cg-congruence-diagonal-kron}
If every \(A_i\) and \(W_{ij}\) lies in one fixed \(\mathcal C_H\), then
every defined node-block Schur complement remains in the interaction family
with all reduced parameters in \(\mathcal C_H\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Factor
\begin{equation}
\Lambda=(I_N\otimes H)\Lambda_D(I_N\otimes H^\top),
\label{eq:cg-kron-congruence-factor}
\end{equation}
where, after grouping coordinates by channel, \(\Lambda_D\) is a direct sum
of \(K\) scalar loopy Laplacians. For retained nodes \(R\) and eliminated
nodes \(E\),
\begin{align}
\operatorname{Sc}_E(\Lambda)
&=(I_R\otimes H)\Big[
(\Lambda_D)_{RR}
-(\Lambda_D)_{RE}(\Lambda_D)_{EE}^{-1}(\Lambda_D)_{ER}
\Big](I_R\otimes H^\top).
\label{eq:cg-kron-congruence-schur}
\end{align}
The middle operator eliminates channel by channel. Each scalar Schur
complement is another loopy Laplacian: its off-diagonals remain nonpositive
because the eliminated block is a nonsingular \(M\)-matrix, and its row sums
remain nonnegative. Reassembling the channels gives reduced self terms and
weights in \(\mathcal C_H\). \(\square\)

This family strictly contains the common-orthogonal-eigenbasis family.
For
\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
X=H\operatorname{diag}(1,2)H^\top,\quad
Y=H\operatorname{diag}(3,1)H^\top,
\]
one has \(XY-YX=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-5\\5&0\end{psmallmatrix}\neq0\),
so \(X,Y\) cannot be simultaneously orthogonally diagonalized, while they
belong to the same \(\mathcal C_H\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\theoremheading{Qualified maximality}{thm:cg-qualified-maximality}
Let \(\mathcal C\subseteq\PSD^K\) be a closed convex cone with an SPD order
unit \(M\in\operatorname{relint}\mathcal C\). Assume that anchors and incident
edge coefficients range independently over \(\mathcal C\), arbitrary positive
edge scaling is allowed, every defined one-node elimination must remain in
the family, and every manufactured off-diagonal block must be minus a
symmetric member of \(\mathcal C\). Then
\(\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal C_H\) for some \(H\), and a maximal cone under
these assumptions equals \(\mathcal C_H\). \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For \(X,Y\in\mathcal C\), relative interior gives an
\(\varepsilon>0\) such that
\[
A_e=M-\varepsilon(X+Y)\in\mathcal C.
\]
Use \(\varepsilon X,\varepsilon Y\) as the two incident weights and \(M\)
as retained anchors. The eliminated diagonal is \(M\), and the manufactured
coupling is \(\varepsilon^2XM^{-1}Y\). Its required symmetry forces
\[
XM^{-1}Y=YM^{-1}X.
\]
After whitening by \(M^{-1/2}\), every pair in the whitened cone commutes.
Commuting real symmetric matrices are simultaneously orthogonally
diagonalizable, so
\[
\mathcal C\subseteq
\{M^{1/2}ODO^\top M^{1/2}:D\succeq0\text{ diagonal}\}
=\mathcal C_H.
\]
The preceding theorem proves that \(\mathcal C_H\) itself is closed, yielding
maximality. \(\square\)

The assumptions exclude correlated or nonconvex admissible sets, cones
without an SPD order unit, parameter-dependent constraints, variable
congruence charts, and nonflat link data. Classification in those domains
remains open. \status{OPEN}

\section{Nonflat links and effective support}
\label{sec:cg-effective-support}

For a graph edge \(e=(i,j)\), write the twisted energy as
\begin{equation}
(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j).
\label{eq:cg-twisted-pair}
\end{equation}
An internal edge under hard identification contributes
\begin{equation}
(I-\Theta_e)^\top W_e(I-\Theta_e),
\label{eq:cg-internal-residual}
\end{equation}
which vanishes exactly when
\begin{equation}
W_e^{1/2}(I-\Theta_e)=0.
\label{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Positive-definite \(W_e\) forces \(\Theta_e=I\); a singular weight may hide a
nonidentity twist. \status{ESTABLISHED}

For \(N_e=\ker W_e\) and quotient map \(q_e:\R^K\to\R^K/N_e\),
\begin{equation}
W_e^{1/2}(I-\Theta_e)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q_e\Theta_e=q_e.
\label{eq:cg-edge-quotient}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

This is edge-local. A single common quotient
\(\R^K/N\) on which every induced weight is positive definite exists exactly
when
\begin{equation}
\ker W_e=N,\qquad \Theta_eN=N
\quad\text{for every relevant edge}.
\label{eq:cg-common-quotient}
\end{equation}
On that quotient ordinary group-valued holonomy is defined. When the kernels
differ, there is no canonical product of the edge-local quotients and no
single ``represented support.'' \status{ESTABLISHED}

A triangle makes the boundary exact:
\[
\begin{aligned}
W_{12}&=\operatorname{diag}(1,0),&
\Theta_{12}&=\operatorname{diag}(1,2),\\
W_{23}&=\operatorname{diag}(0,1),&
\Theta_{23}&=\operatorname{diag}(2,1),\\
W_{31}&=I,&\Theta_{31}&=I.
\end{aligned}
\]
Every edge satisfies \eqref{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}, yet the loop product
is \(2I\), fixing no nonzero vector. The local kernels differ, so no common
effective quotient exists. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Cut links and rectangular sheaf closure}
\label{sec:cg-sheaf-closure}

For positive-definite cut weights between two already trivialized clusters,
define
\[
C=\sum W_e,\qquad B=\sum W_e\Theta_e,\qquad
A=\sum\Theta_e^\top W_e\Theta_e.
\]
One group-valued coarse pair reproduces the cut exactly if and only if all
cut twists coincide. Indeed, with \(\bar\Theta=C^{-1}B\),
\begin{equation}
\Delta=A-B^\top C^{-1}B
=\sum_e(\Theta_e-\bar\Theta)^\top W_e
(\Theta_e-\bar\Theta)\succeq0,
\label{eq:cg-cut-excess}
\end{equation}
and \(\Delta=0\) exactly under common twists. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The weighted mean has the correct gauge law but can be singular; equal weights
with twists \(I_2\) and \(-I_2\) give \(\bar\Theta=0\).
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The exact closed category is larger than one invertible link.

\theoremheading{Rectangular endpoint-map closure}{thm:cg-rectangular-endpoint-closure}
Choose a minimal factor
\[
W_e=C_e^\top C_e,\qquad C_e:\R^K\to E_e .
\]
Then
\begin{equation}
(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)
=\|R_{i,e}z_i-R_{j,e}z_j\|_{E_e}^2,
\qquad
R_{i,e}=C_e,\quad R_{j,e}=C_e\Theta_e.
\label{eq:cg-sheaf-factor}
\end{equation}
For partial injections \(S_{iI}:V_I\to\R^K\), the coarse endpoint maps are
\begin{equation}
R^c_{I,e}=R_{i,e}S_{iI},\qquad
R^c_{J,e}=R_{j,e}S_{jJ}.
\label{eq:cg-sheaf-coarse-maps}
\end{equation}
They reproduce \(S^\top\Lambda S\) exactly, parallel cut edges compress
losslessly by the direct sum of their \(E_e\), and nested blocking is
associative. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Expanding \eqref{eq:cg-sheaf-factor} gives the original pair form.
Precomposition gives \eqref{eq:cg-sheaf-coarse-maps}; its cross block is
\[
-(R^c_{I,e})^\top R^c_{J,e}
=-S_{iI}^\top W_e\Theta_eS_{jJ},
\]
and the diagonal blocks agree by the same expansion. Vertical stacking in
\(\bigoplus_eE_e\) sums the energies without loss. A second injection \(T\)
gives \((RS)T=R(ST)\), proving repeated closure. \(\square\)

This is the degree-zero cellular-sheaf Laplacian category
\citep{HansenGhrist2019}. Vertex spaces may have unequal dimensions and
automorphism groups \(\GL(V_I)\); edge basis changes act on both endpoint
maps and the edge metric. It is gauge covariant and strictly larger than the
fixed-\(K\) group-link family. Open questions are canonical minimal
compression, characterization of compression to one invertible transporter
and one SPD weight, and compatibility with normalized laws and RG rescaling.
\status{OPEN}

\section{Partial fixed-section aggregation}
\label{sec:cg-partial}

For each undirected internal edge, choose one orientation \(e=(i,j)\), so
\(\Theta_e\) transports from \(j\) to \(i\). Assume
\(\Theta_e\in\GL(K)\), \(\Theta_{\bar e}=\Theta_e^{-1}\), and
\(W_e\succ0\). Positive definiteness is the nondegeneracy assumption used
below. \status{HYPOTHESIS}

The cluster connection Laplacian \(L_I\) is defined by
\begin{equation}
z^\top L_Iz
=\sum_{e=(i,j)\in E_I}
(z_i-\Theta_ez_j)^\top W_e(z_i-\Theta_ez_j).
\label{eq:cg-connection-laplacian-energy}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

With semidefinite weights, the exact condition remains the edgewise constraint
in \eqref{eq:cg-weighted-invisibility}, and the resulting effective-support
problem is not replaced by the following proposition. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Let \((I_\alpha)_\alpha\) be the connected components of the internal graph.
Choose a root \(r_\alpha\) and a spanning tree in each component. For a tree
path from \(r_\alpha\) to \(v\), let \(P_{v\leftarrow r_\alpha}\) be the
ordered product of its represented transports. Let
\(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}\) be the group of represented transport
products around all closed walks based at \(r_\alpha\), and define
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha})
=\{u\in\R^K:Hu=u\text{ for every }
H\in\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}\}.
\label{eq:cg-holonomy-fixed-space}
\end{equation}
\status{DEFINITION}

\propositionheading{Kernel--holonomy fixed-space isomorphism}{prop:cg-kernel-holonomy}
On every connected component \(I_\alpha\), root evaluation is a linear
isomorphism
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{ev}_{r_\alpha}:\ker L_{I_\alpha}
\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}),
\qquad z\longmapsto z_{r_\alpha}.
\label{eq:cg-kernel-holonomy-isomorphism}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}
\ker L_I\cong
\bigoplus_\alpha\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}),
\qquad
f_I:=\dim\ker L_I
=\sum_\alpha\dim\operatorname{Fix}
(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}).
\label{eq:cg-fixed-rank}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
By \eqref{eq:cg-connection-laplacian-energy} and \(W_e\succ0\), a vector is
in \(\ker L_{I_\alpha}\) exactly when
\(z_i=\Theta_ez_j\) on every edge. Such a parallel section is determined by
its root value, so root evaluation is injective. Transporting its value
around any rooted closed walk returns the same value, placing its image in
\(\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha})\).

Conversely, take
\(u\in\operatorname{Fix}(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha})\) and define
\(z_v=P_{v\leftarrow r_\alpha}u\) along the spanning tree. Tree-edge
constraints hold by construction. For a non-tree edge \(e=(i,j)\), the tree
path from \(r_\alpha\) to \(j\), followed by \(e\), followed by the reverse
tree path from \(i\) to \(r_\alpha\), has holonomy
\begin{equation}
H_e=P_{r_\alpha\leftarrow i}\Theta_e
P_{j\leftarrow r_\alpha}.
\label{eq:cg-fundamental-cycle-holonomy}
\end{equation}
Since \(H_eu=u\) and
\(P_{r_\alpha\leftarrow i}=P_{i\leftarrow r_\alpha}^{-1}\), multiplication
by \(P_{i\leftarrow r_\alpha}\) gives
\(\Theta_ez_j=z_i\). Thus every edge constraint holds, proving surjectivity.
The full Laplacian is the direct sum over connected components, which yields
\eqref{eq:cg-fixed-rank}. \(\square\)

For a connected cluster, \(f_I=K\) exactly when every represented holonomy
element is the identity. This is the full fixed sector. The case
\(0<f_I<K\) is a partial fixed sector, while \(f_I=0\) leaves no retained
cluster degree. A nonfaithful representation can make represented holonomy
trivial even when principal holonomy is not. \status{ESTABLISHED}

This rank is a state-independent structural admissibility criterion. It assigns no
partition cost, singletons remain admissible, and it does not require the
constituent belief means to coincide. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

Define the abstract fixed fiber
\begin{equation}
\mathsf F_I
=\bigoplus_\alpha\operatorname{Fix}
(\operatorname{Hol}_{r_\alpha}).
\label{eq:cg-abstract-fixed-fiber}
\end{equation}
After choosing bases, let
\(\iota_I:\mathsf F_I\hookrightarrow(\R^K)^I\) be the full-column matrix
that extends each root value by the spanning-tree construction in the proof.
Its range is independent of those choices and equals \(\ker L_I\). For a
partition \(\mathcal P\), put
\(\iota_{\mathcal P}=\operatorname{blkdiag}_{I\in\mathcal P}\iota_I\).
Then
\begin{equation}
L_I\iota_I=0,\qquad
\Lambda_{\rm c}=\iota_{\mathcal P}^\top\Lambda
\iota_{\mathcal P}\succeq0,\qquad
\Lambda_{\rm c}\succ0
\Longleftrightarrow
\operatorname{range}(\iota_{\mathcal P})\cap\ker\Lambda=\{0\}.
\label{eq:cg-partial-properness}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Internal edges vanish, while cut edges become the
rectangular endpoint data of \eqref{eq:cg-sheaf-coarse-maps}. An ordinary
smooth vector bundle over varying parameters additionally requires locally
constant \(f_I\); rank jumps produce strata rather than a smooth bundle.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\propositionheading{Nested fixed sections compose exactly}{prop:cg-nested-sections-compose}
Let a larger cluster \(A\) be a union of first-level clusters, and let
\begin{equation}
\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}
=\operatorname{blkdiag}_{I\subset A}\iota_I,
\qquad
L_A^{(1)}
=\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}^\top L_A
\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}.
\label{eq:cg-nested-first-compression}
\end{equation}
Then the fine and first-level kernels obey
\begin{equation}
\ker L_A
=\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}\ker L_A^{(1)}.
\label{eq:cg-nested-fixed-sections}
\end{equation}
If
\(\iota_A^{(2)}:\mathsf F_A\hookrightarrow
\bigoplus_{I\subset A}\mathsf F_I\) has range \(\ker L_A^{(1)}\), then the
fine-level fixed-section injection is
\begin{equation}
\iota_A^{\rm fine}
=\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}\iota_A^{(2)}.
\label{eq:cg-nested-injection-composition}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Every zero mode of \(L_A\) has zero energy in each positive-semidefinite
edge summand. Its restriction to every first-level cluster therefore belongs
to \(\ker L_I\), so it lies in
\(\operatorname{range}(\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P})\). Write it as
\(\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}y\). Since \(L_A\succeq0\),
\begin{equation}
y\in\ker L_A^{(1)}
\Longleftrightarrow
y^\top L_A^{(1)}y=0
\Longleftrightarrow
\iota_{A\leftarrow\mathcal P}y\in\ker L_A.
\label{eq:cg-nested-kernel-equivalence}
\end{equation}
This proves \eqref{eq:cg-nested-fixed-sections}; substituting the range of
\(\iota_A^{(2)}\) gives \eqref{eq:cg-nested-injection-composition}. Matrix
composition is associative, so the same statement iterates through further
levels. \(\square\)

Thus the vertex fixed spaces and sheaf endpoint maps are exactly functorial
under nested partitions. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Probability normalization, rescaling, and minimal group-link compression remain
separate obligations. \status{OPEN}

\subsection{Constant-metric Gaussian relaxation}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-fixed-relaxation}

\propositionheading{Relaxation onto the fixed sector}{prop:cg-gaussian-fixed-relaxation}
Let \(R=R^\top\succ0\) and \(L=L^\top\succeq0\) be constant matrices on
\(\R^d\). If the columns of \(N_0\) form a basis of \(\ker L\), define the
\(R\)-orthogonal projector
\begin{equation}
\Pi_0^R=N_0(N_0^\top RN_0)^{-1}N_0^\top R,
\label{eq:cg-r-projector}
\end{equation}
and set \(\Pi_0^R=0\) when \(\ker L=\{0\}\). The flow
\begin{equation}
R\dot z=-Lz,\qquad z(0)=z_0,
\label{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow}
\end{equation}
converges to \(\Pi_0^Rz_0\). If the positive spectrum is nonempty and
\begin{equation}
\lambda_+
=\lambda_{\min}^{+}(R^{-1/2}LR^{-1/2}),
\label{eq:cg-transverse-rate}
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
\|z(t)-\Pi_0^Rz_0\|_R
\leq e^{-\lambda_+t}
\|z_0-\Pi_0^Rz_0\|_R.
\label{eq:cg-transverse-decay}
\end{equation}
For \(\mathcal E(z)=\tfrac12z^\top Lz\), the exact dissipation identity is
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal E(z(t))
=-\dot z^\top R\dot z
=-z^\top LR^{-1}Lz\leq0.
\label{eq:cg-fixed-flow-dissipation}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Set \(y=R^{1/2}z\) and
\(A=R^{-1/2}LR^{-1/2}\succeq0\). Then
\(\dot y=-Ay\). The spectral theorem gives convergence to the Euclidean
projection of \(y_0\) onto \(\ker A=R^{1/2}\ker L\), with transverse norm
decaying at the smallest positive eigenvalue of \(A\). Transforming back
gives \eqref{eq:cg-r-projector} and \eqref{eq:cg-transverse-decay}. Direct
differentiation gives \eqref{eq:cg-fixed-flow-dissipation}. \(\square\)

For a constant passive reframing \(z'=Tz\), \(T\in\GL(d)\), covariance of
the equation requires the congruence laws
\begin{equation}
R'=T^{-\top}RT^{-1},\qquad
L'=T^{-\top}LT^{-1},\qquad
\Pi_0^{R'}=T\Pi_0^RT^{-1}.
\label{eq:cg-flow-congruence}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The choice \(R=I_d\) is therefore equivariant only
under orthogonal reframings. If \(T=T(t)\), the ordinary derivative acquires
the term \(\dot TT^{-1}z'\). Covariance requires a time covariant derivative;
when the original temporal connection is zero, it is
\begin{equation}
D_t'z'=\dot z'-\dot TT^{-1}z'=T\dot z,
\qquad R'D_t'z'=-L'z'.
\label{eq:cg-time-covariant-flow}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The information-geometric identification is exact on the fixed-covariance
Gaussian mean family
\(q_z=\mathcal N(z,R^{-1})\). Its mean Fisher matrix is \(R\), and
\begin{equation}
\KL(q_z\Vert q_w)
=\frac12(z-w)^\top R(z-w).
\label{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-kl}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow} is exactly the Fisher natural-gradient
flow of \(\mathcal E\) on that mean family. For a general regular statistical
family, only the local expansion
\begin{equation}
\KL(q_\theta\Vert q_{\theta+d\theta})
=\frac12d\theta^\top F(\theta)d\theta
+o(\|d\theta\|^2),
\label{eq:cg-local-fisher-kl}
\end{equation}
is available under the standard regularity hypotheses
\citep{AmariNagaoka2000}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Freezing
\(F(\theta_0)=R\) yields a local linearization. The development does not
identify a generic, state-dependent Fisher flow with the constant global
projector and rate above. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

The solution of \eqref{eq:cg-constant-metric-flow} is a parameterized
dynamical trajectory.  Away from stationary points, its oriented
unparameterized orbit is a history in the sense of
\Cref{def:hist-oriented-history}, and its Fisher arclength is invariant under
orientation-preserving reparameterization by
\Cref{thm:hist-fisher-clock-invariance}. The exponential rate and the
dissipation per unit \(t\) are properties of this chosen parameterization, not
of the unparameterized history. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Here \(t\) is a declared flow parameter; it is neither a coordinate on the
timeless contextual base, an RG-depth index, nor physical time. No
physical-time identification is claimed. \status{NOT-CLAIMED}

\subsection{Gaussian marginal-law specialization}
\label{sec:cg-gaussian-marginal-law}

Specialize \Cref{thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal} after transporting every
constituent marginal to one root. For this specialization, declare the
admissible parent family to be the full nondegenerate Gaussian family.
Trivial represented holonomy then makes the holonomy-fixed admissible
subfamily equal to that declared parent; it does not itself select the parent
family. Write the transported marginals as
\(q_i^{(r)}=\mathcal N(m_i,C_i)\), with \(C_i\succ0\), and choose weights
\(a_i>0\) satisfying \(\sum_i a_i=1\). \status{HYPOTHESIS}

\propositionheading{Full-Gaussian forward-KL barycenter}{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}
The unique minimizer of
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_I(m,C)
=\sum_i a_i\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(m_i,C_i)
\Vert\mathcal N(m,C)\bigr),
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-objective}
\end{equation}
matches the first two moments:
\begin{equation}
\bar m=\sum_i a_im_i,\qquad
\bar C=\sum_i a_i\left[C_i+(m_i-\bar m)(m_i-\bar m)^\top\right].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments}
\end{equation}
Its minimized score is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_I^\star
=\frac12\left[
\log\det\bar C-\sum_i a_i\log\det C_i
\right].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-score}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The Gaussian KL formula gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
2\mathcal D_I(m,C)
&=\Tr(C^{-1}M(m))-K+\log\det C
-\sum_i a_i\log\det C_i,\\
M(m)&=\sum_i a_i\left[C_i+(m_i-m)(m_i-m)^\top\right].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-expansion}
\end{equation}
Completing the weighted square gives
\(M(m)=\bar C+(m-\bar m)(m-\bar m)^\top\), so \(m=\bar m\) is the unique
minimizer. At that value,
\(\Tr(C^{-1}\bar C)-\log\det(C^{-1}\bar C)\geq K\), with equality exactly
when \(C=\bar C\). Substitution proves
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-score}. \(\square\)

Under nontrivial represented holonomy, the moment-matched Gaussian need not
belong to the holonomy-fixed parent family. \status{ESTABLISHED}

The
applicable optimization is then the constrained one in
\Cref{thm:cg-holonomy-kl-marginal}. The development does not claim that
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments} removes that constraint.
\status{NOT-CLAIMED}

For an explicit witness, let \(K=2\),
\(\mathcal H=\{I_2,-I_2\}\subset\mathrm{SO}(2)\), and take the one-law
ensemble \(\mathcal N(u,I_2)\) with \(u\neq0\). Its unconstrained
moment-matched Gaussian is itself and is not \(\mathcal H\)-invariant. The
Haar-constrained optimizer below is instead
\(\mathcal N(0,I_2+uu^\top)\), with score
\(\tfrac12\log(1+\lVert u\rVert^2)>0\). Thus the holonomy constraint can be
active even when there is no pairwise disagreement among the transported
constituents.

\corollaryheading{Compact-closure holonomy Gaussian barycenter}{cor:cg-compact-holonomy-barycenter}
Let \(\mathcal H\subset\GL(K)\) be a represented holonomy group whose closure
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\) is compact in \(\GL(K)\), and let
\(\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\) be normalized Haar probability measure on
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\). A nondegenerate Gaussian is
\(\mathcal H\)-invariant exactly when it is
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\)-invariant. Define
\begin{equation}
a=\sum_i a_im_i,\qquad
M_2=\sum_i a_i(C_i+m_im_i^\top),
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-raw-moments}
\end{equation}
and their Haar projections
\begin{equation}
a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}
=\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}}ha
d\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}(h),\qquad
M_{2,\overline{\mathcal H}}
=\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}}hM_2h^\top
d\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}(h),\qquad
C_{\overline{\mathcal H}}
=M_{2,\overline{\mathcal H}}
-a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}^\top.
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-moments}
\end{equation}
Then \(C_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\succ0\), and
\(\mathcal N(a_{\overline{\mathcal H}},C_{\overline{\mathcal H}})\) is the unique minimizer of
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-objective} over all
\(\mathcal H\)-invariant nondegenerate Gaussians. Its score retains the
log-determinant form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D_{I,\overline{\mathcal H}}^\star
=\frac12\left[
\log\det C_{\overline{\mathcal H}}-\sum_i a_i\log\det C_i
\right].
\label{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-score}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
The stabilizer of a Gaussian under the continuous \(\GL(K)\) pushforward
action is closed. Hence invariance under \(\mathcal H\) is equivalent to
invariance under \(\overline{\mathcal H}\). For every such invariant
candidate \(q\), invariance of KL under a common invertible pushforward gives
\begin{equation}
\sum_i a_i\KL(q_i^{(r)}\Vert q)
=\int_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\sum_i a_i
\KL(h_\#q_i^{(r)}\Vert q)d\mu_{\overline{\mathcal H}}(h).
\label{eq:cg-haar-objective-average}
\end{equation}
The orbit-averaged source ensemble on the right has first moment
\(a_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\) and second moment
\(M_{2,\overline{\mathcal H}}\). Haar invariance makes both moments
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\)-invariant, so its moment-matched Gaussian
is an admissible candidate. Its covariance is positive definite because it
contains the Haar average of the positive-definite matrices \(hC_ih^\top\).
The calculation in
\Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter} therefore proves uniqueness.
Every element of the compact matrix group
\(\overline{\mathcal H}\) has \(\lvert\det h\rvert=1\), so
\(\log\det(hC_ih^\top)=\log\det C_i\), which yields
\eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-haar-score}. \(\square\)

Compactness cannot be dropped from this averaging formula. If the represented
holonomy contains \(2I_K\), an invariant Gaussian would require
\(m=2m\) and \(C=4C\); hence \(m=0\) and \(C=0\), contradicting
nondegeneracy. The generated expanding holonomy group therefore admits no
invariant SPD Gaussian. \status{ESTABLISHED}

There is a separate exact identity when every transported constituent and
the parent are restricted to one fixed covariance \(C_0\succ0\). Define the
symmetrized convention
\begin{equation}
D_{\rm SKL}(p,q)
:=\frac12\left[\KL(p\Vert q)+\KL(q\Vert p)\right].
\label{eq:cg-symmetric-kl-convention}
\end{equation}
For \(q_i^{(r)}=\mathcal N(m_i,C_0)\) and
\(\bar q_{C_0}=\mathcal N(\bar m,C_0)\), the fixed-covariance barycenter
score satisfies
\begin{equation}
\sum_i a_i\KL(q_i^{(r)}\Vert\bar q_{C_0})
=\sum_{i<j}a_ia_jD_{\rm SKL}(q_i^{(r)},q_j^{(r)})
=\frac12\sum_{i,j}a_ia_jD_{\rm SKL}(q_i^{(r)},q_j^{(r)}).
\label{eq:cg-fixed-covariance-pairwise-identity}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

This follows from the weighted variance identity in the \(C_0^{-1}\) inner
product. It is not the free-covariance optimization of
\Cref{prop:cg-gaussian-forward-kl-barycenter}, whose covariance includes the
between-mean term in \eqref{eq:cg-gaussian-barycenter-moments}.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

Ordinary pairwise KL between untransported local marginals is not a
cross-fiber quantity. After choosing a path, a second path can change the
transported law by a loop-holonomy action. Stabilization by that action
guarantees path independence; without it the two KL values need not agree.
For example, using the holonomy \(H\) in
\eqref{eq:cg-isotropic-holonomy-counterexample} and
\(q_i=q_j=\mathcal N(e_2,I_3)\), the identity path gives KL zero, whereas a
path differing by \(H\) gives KL equal to \(2\). Moreover, an unpenalized sum
of nonnegative within-cluster pairwise KL values is minimized by the
singleton partition, where it vanishes. It therefore supplies no
nondegenerate partition selector by itself. It also contains only marginal
information and does not test the common recovery-kernel condition for joint
recovery in \Cref{sec:cg-kl-recovery}. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Two elementary examples separate the structural and marginal statements.
On a two-vertex tree with identity transport and \(W=I_K\), represented
holonomy is trivial, yet the marginals
\(\mathcal N(-ae_1,I_K)\) and \(\mathcal N(ae_1,I_K)\) have transported
divergence
\begin{equation}
\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(-ae_1,I_K)\Vert
\mathcal N(ae_1,I_K)\bigr)=2a^2,
\label{eq:cg-trivial-holonomy-large-kl}
\end{equation}
which is arbitrarily large. Conversely, take a triangle with \(K=3\), unit
edge weights, identity transports on two edges, and closing transport
\begin{equation}
H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)\in\operatorname{SO}(3).
\label{eq:cg-isotropic-holonomy-counterexample}
\end{equation}
Its represented holonomy is nontrivial and
\(\operatorname{Fix}(H)=\operatorname{span}(e_1)\), so the structural fixed
sector has \(f_I=1<K\). Nevertheless, \(H\) preserves
\(\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2I_3)\); assigning that marginal at every vertex makes
all transported marginal KL values zero. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Transported KL therefore measures marginal occupation of the available law
modes, not the dimension of the state-independent structural fixed section.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Quotient volume for hard membership}
\label{sec:cg-quotient-volume}

Let \(P=\widehat S\in\{0,1\}^{n\times m}\), with nonempty cluster sizes
\(s_1,\ldots,s_m\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(s_i)\),
\(s=(s_i)\), and \(n=\sum_i s_i\). For a positive-semidefinite matrix,
\(\operatorname{pdet}\) denotes the product of its positive eigenvalues.
On the translation quotient, the metric
induced from the fine Euclidean quotient is
\begin{equation}
H=D-\frac{ss^\top}{n},\qquad
\ker H=\operatorname{span}(\mathbf1_m),\qquad
\operatorname{pdet}H=\frac{m\prod_i s_i}{n}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-metric}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
For a coarse class \([x]\),
\[
\min_a\|Px+a\mathbf1_n\|^2
=x^\top\left(D-\frac{ss^\top}{n}\right)x.
\]
Deleting row and column \(r\), the matrix determinant lemma gives the
principal cofactor \(\prod_i s_i/n\). Since a rank-\((m-1)\) Laplacian with
kernel \(\mathbf1_m\) has pseudodeterminant \(m\) times any principal
cofactor, \eqref{eq:cg-quotient-metric} follows. \(\square\)

For \(K\) channels, standard quotient Lebesgue measure and fine-induced
Hausdorff measure are related by
\begin{equation}
\mu_{\rm ind}=J_{\mathcal P}\mu_{\rm std},\qquad
J_{\mathcal P}
=\left(\frac{m\prod_i s_i}{n}\right)^{K/2}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-jacobian}
\end{equation}
If
\[
\operatorname{range}(S)\cap\ker\Lambda
=\mathbf1_n\otimes\R^K,
\]
the quotient Gaussian is proper and its generalized determinant satisfies
\begin{equation}
\det_{H\otimes I_K}(\Lambda_{\rm c})
:=\frac{\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\rm c}}
{(\operatorname{pdet}H)^K}
=\frac{\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\rm c}}{J_{\mathcal P}^2}.
\label{eq:cg-quotient-generalized-determinant}
\end{equation}
This is the determinant of the precision relative to the quotient metric
\(H\otimes I_K\). For the common unnormalized factor
\(\exp(-x^\top\Lambda_{\rm c}x/2)\), let \(d_q=(m-1)K\). Its normalizers are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Z_{\rm std}
&=(2\pi)^{d_q/2}
(\operatorname{pdet}\Lambda_{\rm c})^{-1/2},\\
Z_{\rm ind}
&=(2\pi)^{d_q/2}
\det_{H\otimes I_K}(\Lambda_{\rm c})^{-1/2}
=J_{\mathcal P}Z_{\rm std}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-quotient-normalizers}
\end{equation}
The factor cancels from the normalized law because the measure changes by
the same amount. \status{ESTABLISHED}

Raw partition functions differ by \(\log J_{\mathcal P}\), so a partition
score needs a fixed volume convention, an exact correction, or a compensating
prior. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{Recognition-side costs}
\label{sec:cg-restriction-costs}

Let \(B\in\R^{NK\times mK}\) have orthonormal columns spanning
\(\operatorname{range}S\), let
\(B_\perp\in\R^{NK\times r}\), \(r=NK-mK\), complete it so that
\([B\ B_\perp]\) is orthogonal, and write
\(p=\mathcal N(\mu,\Lambda^{-1})\). These definitions apply throughout this
section.

The admissible mean-only family leaves covariance unrestricted. For
\(q=\mathcal N(\nu,\Sigma)\), \(\nu\in\operatorname{range}S\), its exact
optimized cost is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{\rm tie}
=\inf_{\nu\in\operatorname{range}S,\ \Sigma\succ0}
\KL\bigl(\mathcal N(\nu,\Sigma)\Vert p\bigr)
=\frac12m_\perp^\top
\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1}m_\perp,
\qquad
m_\perp=B_\perp^\top\mu.
\label{eq:cg-mean-tie-cost}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
Optimizing the Gaussian KL over covariance gives
\(\Lambda^{-1}\), leaving
\(\frac12(\nu-\mu)^\top\Lambda(\nu-\mu)\). Minimizing over
\(\nu=Ba\) gives the \(\Lambda\)-projection of \(\mu\). In the orthogonal
basis \([B\ B_\perp]\), its remaining block is the Schur complement
\[
\Lambda_{22}-\Lambda_{21}\Lambda_{11}^{-1}\Lambda_{12}
=\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1}.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:cg-mean-tie-cost}. \(\square\)

A recognition law supported exactly on \(\operatorname{range}S\) is singular
against \(p\), so its relative entropy to \(p\) is \(+\infty\). The following
declared full-rank regularization makes the divergence and every finite term
explicit. Let \(\nu_\star\) be the mean optimizer above and set
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_\varepsilon
=B(B^\top\Lambda B)^{-1}B^\top
+\varepsilon B_\perp B_\perp^\top,
\qquad
q_\varepsilon=\mathcal N(\nu_\star,\Sigma_\varepsilon),
\qquad \varepsilon>0.
\label{eq:cg-epsilon-regularized-law}
\end{equation}
Then, exactly,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal G_\varepsilon
:=\KL(q_\varepsilon\Vert p)
={}&\mathcal G_{\rm tie}
+\frac{r}{2}\log\frac1\varepsilon-\frac r2
+\frac12\log\det(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp)\\
&+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
\Tr(B_\perp^\top\Lambda B_\perp).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cg-epsilon-divergence}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Proof.}
In the orthogonal basis \([B\ B_\perp]\), the covariance in
\eqref{eq:cg-epsilon-regularized-law} has determinant
\(\det(B^\top\Lambda B)^{-1}\varepsilon^r\), while
\[
\Tr(\Lambda\Sigma_\varepsilon)
=mK+\varepsilon\Tr(B_\perp^\top\Lambda B_\perp).
\]
The Gaussian KL formula and the optimized mean term give all terms in
\eqref{eq:cg-epsilon-divergence} after using
\[
\det\Lambda
=\det(B^\top\Lambda B)
\det\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1},
\]
which is the block determinant identity. \(\square\)

Thus \(\mathcal G_\varepsilon\to+\infty\) as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\): identification is a generative construction, not
a finite recognition restriction.

The marginal precision in \eqref{eq:cg-mean-tie-cost} obeys
\begin{equation}
\left(B_\perp^\top\Lambda^{-1}B_\perp\right)^{-1}
\preceq B_\perp^\top\Lambda B_\perp,
\label{eq:cg-loewner}
\end{equation}
with equality exactly when
\(B^\top\Lambda B_\perp=0\). Using the restriction precision instead
therefore overstates the cost except under \(\Lambda\)-orthogonality.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

If only covariance factorization across a partition is imposed, the exact
Gaussian optimum has gap
\begin{equation}
\mathcal G_{\rm fact}
=\frac12\left[
\sum_I\log\det\Lambda_{II}-\log\det\Lambda
\right].
\label{eq:cg-factorization-gap}
\end{equation}
\status{ESTABLISHED}

The mean-tie cost is nonnegative and vanishes at the
finest partition; the factorization gap decreases under merging by Fischer's
determinant inequality and vanishes at the coarsest partition. The quotient
volume is convention dependent across partitions. Therefore these terms do
not supply a nondegenerate intrinsic scale without an additional coefficient
or a different nonmonotone functional. Whether such a selector exists remains
open. \status{OPEN}

The corrected cross-inventory boundary is the general theorem of
\Cref{sec:cg-elbo}: a common evidence-preserving Markov pushforward makes
ELBOs comparable and ordered. The hard energy trace analyzed here is not such
a pushforward unless a separate theorem proves it, so its cross-partition
difference can mix evidence and tightness. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\section{The frame sector}
\label{sec:cg-frame}

If a coarse Gaussian \((U_I,\mu_I,\Sigma_I)\) is sent to constituent \(i\)
through \(U_iU_I^{-1}\), then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal N(U_iU_I^{-1}\mu_I,
U_iU_I^{-1}\Sigma_IU_I^{-\top}U_i^\top)
=\mathcal N(U_i\bar m,U_i\bar S U_i^\top),
\label{eq:cg-frame-cancellation}
\end{equation}
with \(\bar m=U_I^{-1}\mu_I\) and
\(\bar S=U_I^{-1}\Sigma_IU_I^{-\top}\).
Thus the coarse frame cancels. With faithful representation the level set is
exactly one gauge orbit; without faithfulness only unidentifiability follows.
\status{ESTABLISHED}

For \(K\geq2\), \(n\geq2\), no map
\(F:\GL^+(K)^n\to\GL^+(K)\) is both left equivariant and permutation
symmetric. Choose an order-\(n\) rotation \(v\in\mathrm{SO}(K)\) and inputs
\((I,v,\ldots,v^{n-1})\). Left multiplication by \(v\) cyclically permutes
the tuple. Symmetry keeps \(F\) fixed, while equivariance sends it to \(vF\);
hence \((I-v)F=0\), making \(F\) singular. \status{ESTABLISHED}

\paragraph{Physicist summary.}
Hard Gaussian aggregation is exact Galerkin restriction: internal springs
disappear and cut springs add. Gaussian marginalization is a different
operation and mixes matrix channels; it fails in general but closes exactly
when all parameters decouple in one congruence metric. Nonflat or
rank-changing links close in the larger sheaf category of endpoint
measurement ports. With positive-definite represented internal weights,
holonomy determines the available parallel modes: trivial holonomy retains
the full \(K\)-dimensional sector, while nontrivial holonomy can retain only a
partial sector or none. When the infimum is attained, transported KL measures
whether the marginals occupy an available mode; without attainment, a zero
infimum establishes only approximability. It is not a structural-holonomy
test or a partition selector, and joint recovery remains separate. Quotient
volumes, mean tying, and determinant gaps are explicit, but none selects an
intrinsic blocking scale by itself.
