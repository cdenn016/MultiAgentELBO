## RG-v2 recursive coarse-agent closure design

Added the approved Phase 2 design for a static two-parent recursive closure witness. The design starts from four fine agents in two declared blocks, constructs two ordinary next-level agent mechanisms and one exact combined record from the pushed generative joint, declares a bijection from the fine observation support to the compound-record support, supplies identity access on that reconstructed support and an all-observation Bayesian update table, and requires the unchanged Release 1 population and inference constructors to reproduce coarse recognition, evidence, and posterior for all sixteen positive-evidence observations.

The specification keeps generative construction independent of access, update, recognition, selector, realized observation, posterior, `PopulationInference`, and `AggregateDatum`; preserves `AggregateDatum` as an unmodified terminal Release 1 type; requires at least one exact violation of a fully declared sparse-record factorization alongside the dense exact fallback; and fixes the root-local module, config, artifact, metric, replay, regression, and countercontrol boundaries for implementation planning. The construction proves static closure only in the Release 1 combined-state `AgentDatum` family; Chapter 4's separated belief/model factorization, observation-dependent recognition-family descent, autonomous dynamics, lumpability, scale composition, fixed points, universality, and continuum claims remain open.

Three independent design reviews checked the live code surface, the mathematical obligations, and the experiment contract. Their blocking findings were incorporated before finalization: the observation supports are related by an explicit typed bijection; access and Bayesian update data have an explicit inference-free constructor and live descent control; update data reside in the interface artifact rather than the generative artifact; sparse factorization is mechanically identifiable; and the executable non-lumpable dynamics control was removed from this static phase.
