# Investigation: does the missing thermodynamic limit damage the theory in `main.tex`?

Date: 2026-08-16
Author model: Claude Opus 5. Investigators: four domain agents (philosophy-of-science, information-geometry/rigorous-RG, gauge-theory, variational). Cross-model verifier: Fable 5.

## The trigger

A paragraph in the Codex-revised `physicists_companion.tex` reads:

> The finite exact scale theory supplies normalized kernels, pushed measure pairs, a bounded-action calculus with an exact conditional-variance loss, full Hoeffding interaction coordinates, and a typed projection residual. It supplies no thermodynamic limit. An infinite-volume limit still requires projectively consistent cylinder laws, boundary conditions or a DLR specification, tightness in a selected topology, convergence of free-energy densities, and compatible limits of the connection and cross-morphism components. A joint limit in system size and RG depth, together with basin independence and blocking-scheme robustness, is separately open, and a finite congruence identity or one matched exponent does not settle it.

The question put to this investigation was whether that constitutes a major problem for the theory.

## Verdict

It is not a problem, and it is not a finding. The paragraph is the manuscript's own declared open problem, transcribed from `main.tex` into the companion document. Separately, and more importantly, no asserted result in the manuscript depends on the limit that is missing.

## Provenance: the paragraph originates in `main.tex`

The text is a near-verbatim concatenation of two entries in the manuscript's own claim ledger. `Theory/appendix_claim_ledger.tex:243-255` is titled "Infinite-volume RG limit (open)" and states that "None of these finite-step theorems supplies a thermodynamic limit. An infinite-volume limit still requires projectively consistent cylinder laws, boundary conditions or a DLR specification, tightness in the selected state topology, convergence of free-energy densities, and compatible limits of the connection and cross-morphism components," carrying `\status{OPEN}`. Lines 257-261, titled "Two-index limits and universality (open)," close with "A finite congruence identity or one matched exponent is insufficient," also `\status{OPEN}`. Since `main.tex:152` inputs that appendix, this text ships inside `main.tex`.

In the companion the paragraph sits inside a `\begin{boundarybox}` environment whose title is literally "Boundary of the result" (`physicists_companion.tex:71-77`), one of five such boxes used throughout the document to fence scope. It is expository furniture, not a defect report. Codex's own review note (`docs/reviews/2026-08-16-physicists-companion-review.md`) lists what it changed, namely an attention normalization correction, a three-level split of the thermodynamic interpretation, a typed split of the meta-agent stages, and a status-table correction. It never claims to have discovered a thermodynamic-limit problem.

## Nothing asserted depends on the missing limit

The substantive question is whether the open status is honestly propagated, or whether some `ESTABLISHED` result silently requires infinite volume. Four independent sweeps found none.

The manuscript's vocabulary discipline is unusually strict. The phrase "renormalization group" appears nowhere in the body of any of the twenty-two chapters; chapter titles read "General Renormalization" and "Exact Renormalization of Agent Networks," and `01_introduction.tex:74-77` explains the restraint, noting that without comparison or rescaling maps "there is only a changing-space coarse diagram, not a fixed point problem." The word "universality" occurs six times and every occurrence is a denial, including `10_renormalization.tex:422`, "Equality of one exponent is not equality of universality classes." The relevant, marginal, and irrelevant trichotomy is declared definitional at first use and tagged `\status{DEFINITION}` (`07_general_renormalization.tex:708-713`), classified "relative to the declared norms ... not an intrinsic property of the scale sequence," and `prop:rg-superexponential-distortion` then exhibits a comparison trivialization that changes the reported exponent, converting the declaration into a proved warning. The Oseledets multiplicative-ergodic upgrade is refused outright as `\status{NOT-CLAIMED}` with its missing hypotheses enumerated.

The finite RG mathematics was recomputed rather than read. The Hermite spectrum \(\lambda_k = b^{1-k/2}\) was derived from the manuscript's own conventions, with a SymPy residual of exactly zero and Monte-Carlo agreement at \(n = 4\times10^6\) for \(b = 2, 3, 5\) within one standard error at every \(k\); this is the standard Gaussian block-spin result, Jona-Lasinio's \(2^{1-k/2}\) at \(b=2\), correctly generalized to arbitrary integer \(b\). The standard Gaussian is an exact fixed point of the declared blocking map, with no approximation and no limit, since \(b^{-1/2}\sum_i X_i \sim N(0,1)\) for independent standard normal summands, and `thm:rg-score-pushforward-defect` establishes that the Hermite operator genuinely is the derivative at that fixed point rather than an ansatz. The exponents require no limit at all: \(\upsilon = 1 - k/2\) holds for every finite \(n > \ell\), so the asymptotic value coincides with the one-step value without any convergence argument.

The manuscript proves, rather than assumes, that the two-index limits fail to commute. `prop:rg-noncommuting-limits` (`07_general_renormalization.tex:927-990`) was verified at every step: the Galerkin quotient residual is exactly zero, and the spectral counting function \(U_\infty(\lambda) = \pi^{-1}\arccos(1-\lambda/2)\) matched direct eigenvalue counting with zero mismatches across six values of \(m\) and two hundred values of \(\lambda\). The joint limit is open because the limits genuinely differ, not because the question was never asked.

The core variational results are finite-\(N\) exact. `05b_local_collective_elbo.tex:19-20` fixes \(V\) finite at the outset, and `:84-86` proves normalization "for every finite hypergraph, including cyclic ones." The local-global potential identity is the measure-theoretic KL chain rule with two cancellations, one of a common constant and one of \(-\log Z_B(b)\) pointwise in \(b\) before any expectation is taken, so no integrability of \(\log Z_B\) is used in the theorem. The cavity caveat familiar from statistical physics does not apply, because no cavity approximation is made: the field is the exact conditional \(\Pi_{o,B}(\cdot\mid b)\), retaining full dependence on the outside configuration rather than a product surrogate. A grep for `cavity`, `bethe`, `belief propagation`, and `replica` across the whole Theory tree returns no hits, and the manuscript denies the mean-field assumption four times, including `05b:455`, "mean field is thus a special case of the ledger, not a premise of it." The identity verifies symbolically to exactly zero for free symbols and numerically to \(2.8\times10^{-17}\) on a four-cycle with a correlated non-product baseline.

No free-energy density exists anywhere in the core chapters. A search for extensivity and intensivity language across all seven returns two hits, both arguing that a quantity is not decomposable per agent. Every functional is a total, never a ratio to \(|V|\). This is the cleanest single answer to the charge: there is no intensive quantity to converge, so no subadditivity or Fekete argument is owed. The only two occurrences of "convergence of free-energy densities" in the entire tree sit inside `\status{OPEN}` lists of what is not supplied.

The gauge apparatus is entirely finite-graph. The link variables obey the Wilson-Wegner law \(\Theta_e \mapsto g_i \Theta_e g_j^{-1}\), consistent with reversal to \(3\times10^{-15}\); holonomy telescopes to \(H' = a_{i_0}^{-1} H a_{i_0}\), making the conjugacy class, \(\det(I-H)\), and \(\operatorname{tr} H\) gauge invariant to within \(1.5\times10^{-15}\). The reciprocal-fold no-go determinant \(\det J = \det(I-H)^2/(\det R_e \det R_f)\) follows from a Schur complement on a \(2K \times 2K\) matrix, with relative errors at the \(10^{-15}\) level across random draws, and the \(K=1\) witness is symbolically exact. The blocking map is gauge-equivariant, verified on both flat and non-flat link data at \(2.6\times10^{-14}\) and \(6.8\times10^{-14}\); the fine gauge cancels edge by edge and only the root gauge survives. There is no equivariance failure. Every gauge-geometric claim in the manuscript is a finite-graph statement, and none invokes the open limit.

## Findings worth acting on

Nothing below is load-bearing. Each is a one-clause or one-sentence repair.

The introduction understates the manuscript's own debts. `01_introduction.tex:143-144` lists only infinite-hierarchy attraction, basin uniqueness, and blocking-scheme independence, dropping the volume axis and the continuum axis entirely, and the chapter carries no cross-reference to `app:claim-ledger` at all. A reader following the "physicist's fast path" can finish believing the RG programme owes less than it does. One inserted sentence naming the thermodynamic and continuum obligations, plus a `\Cref{app:claim-ledger}` pointer at line 157, closes this. Severity medium.

The Gibbs-Bogoliubov-Feynman identification is never made. A search for `bogoliubov`, `feynman`, and `GBF` across the Theory tree returns zero hits, yet the correspondence is exact: \(F_{\mathrm{true}} \le F_0 + \langle H - H_0\rangle_0\) with the slack identified precisely as \(\mathrm{KL}(Q\|\Pi_o)\), symbolic residual zero. Naming it costs one sentence after `eq:obs-collective-energy-form` and places the central result inside a finite-system variational principle a century old. This is the shortest available answer to the charge. Severity medium.

Two finiteness guards are missing. `prop:restrict-unrelated-joint-elbo` (`07_restrictions.tex:369-372`) performs the same subtraction that `prop:restrict-principle` explicitly guards with "if both divergence infima below are finite" (`05_elbo.tex:317`), but omits the hypothesis; if both families are posterior-singular, a case the manuscript itself constructs, the display becomes \((-\infty) - (-\infty)\), which is exactly the form the manuscript refuses to write at `05_elbo.tex:188`. Separately, `05b:679` treats the outside-averaged local VFE as a standalone finite object, but the cited theorem controls only the KL term; \(\mathbb E|\log Z_B|\) is unconstrained, so the quantity can be \(+\infty - \infty\). Chapter 5d imposes exactly the needed hypothesis at `05d:398-404`; chapter 5b does not. Both conclusions survive. Severity medium each.

The continuous-beta underdetermination proposition does not cover one-parameter groups. `prop:rg-continuous-beta-underdetermined` is proved, with cocycle residual exactly zero, but both witnesses have the form \(f(s)/f(t)\), that is, two-parameter evolution families. A referee who insists a physical RG flow is a one-parameter semigroup can call the proposition inapplicable, and in the scalar case would be right. The gap closes with a rotation witness: on \(\mathbb R^2\) with \(A_k = 2\pi k J\), the one-parameter groups \(e^{(s-t)A_k}\) agree at every integer endpoint pair to \(8.6\times10^{-16}\) while their generators are plainly distinguishable, so underdetermination survives the semigroup restriction once the coupling space has dimension at least two. Severity medium.

The trivializing criterion quantifies over an infinite family. `prop:geo-trivializing-criterion` (`02_geometry.tex:597-616`) requires trivial product "for every closed internal walk," a countably infinite condition, and never reduces it to a finite certificate, even though the reduction appears inside proofs elsewhere on a different graph class (`06_gaussian.tex:148`, `09_coarsegraining.tex:411-423`). Perturbing one non-tree edge on a graph with \(b_1 = 3\) broke exactly one fundamental-cycle holonomy and left the other two at \(2.7\times10^{-14}\), confirming that \(b_1\) checks decide the criterion. A spanning-tree corollary makes it decidable. Severity medium.

The blocking map depends on the declared spanning tree, and the disclosure covers only the \(\pi_1\) presentation. Under nontrivial internal holonomy, two admissible trees give coarse states not related by any element of \(\mathrm{GL}(K)\), with a best-fit residual of 0.60 against \(2.6\times10^{-14}\) in the flat case. Root change is covariant; tree change is not. The manuscript's own no-go at `09_coarsegraining.tex:1044-1049` makes the ambiguity structural rather than removable, so the fix is disclosure at `07b:1853` rather than repair. Severity medium.

The connection defect has an exact ordered cocycle across scales, verified independently, but the cross-morphism defect has no stated composition law, even though `07_general_renormalization.tex:1026` names both as obligations. Adding the horizontal-pasting identity gives both components a finite composition law before any limit is discussed. Severity medium.

Lower-severity items: `main.tex:21` asserts "renormalization group" in `pdfkeywords`, the sole occurrence of the phrase in the manuscript, undoing the body's deliberate restraint in the field that feeds search indices; `appendix_claim_ledger.tex:212-215` cites the author's own workflow tokens `EVIDENCE_VERIFIED` and `COMPLETE_AFFIRMATIVE` as if they were an independent audit; `07b:3051-3066` bundles an `ESTABLISHED` tag over a paragraph that ends by listing open obligations, breaking the one-tag-one-statement discipline at the closing paragraph of the RG development; `07b:1074` calls \(b^2/(b-1)\) the Hilbert-Schmidt norm when it is the squared norm, and the operator is in fact trace class; \(\mathscr L_{b,\rho}\) at `07b:1158` is used without definition; the based holonomy map is an anti-homomorphism under the manuscript's own product convention yet is called a representation; `eq:geo-link-holonomy`'s invariance claim is tagged `ESTABLISHED` with no proof and no basepoint statement; the star-schedule geometric rate is exact at every finite \(n\) but approaches unity as the constituent count grows, which the text does not record; and the exact block minimizer is a kernel in the outside configuration, so `05b:384` reads, in isolation, as licensing an agent-local update that a block cannot perform.

## Bounding the charge

The reviewer's paragraph is correct on its face and reaches only the RG chapters, `07_general_renormalization.tex` and `07b_agent_network_rg.tex`, where the gap is already recorded `OPEN`. Even there the extensivity statements are exact finite-\(b\) identities rather than limits. `03_probability.tex:483` already states that "Every evidence-bound claim in this document is accordingly a finite-design claim, and none of them is asserted in a continuum limit."

Scope was confirmed directly: `main.tex` contains no reference to `PIFB2` or `grand_canonical_meta_agent_formation`, so both are standalone documents outside the manuscript and the twenty-three inputs listed above are the whole of it.

## The framing finding

The verifier's separate adversarial pass reached the conclusion that matters most for how this work is received. The title, which reads "...and Renormalization," the PDF subject "exact agent-network renormalization," and the general framing sell renormalization, while the body delivers finite-step exact scale structure with no flow to a fixed point, no universality, and no continuum or thermodynamic limit. A hostile referee would say the title writes a check the theorems do not cash. The defense is unusually strong, since `01_introduction.tex:136-137` states that "The RG conclusions are deliberately narrower. Rescaling is declared rather than derived," and the epistemic contract, the per-paragraph status tags, and two dedicated `OPEN` ledger entries post the conversion rate openly. The one place the framing outruns the fencing is the word "Renormalization" in the title, unmodified by "finite" or "exact." The subtitle's "Exact Effective Scale Theory" is the accurate name and could carry more of the title's weight.

This converges with the substantive assessment of the gap. What is missing is universality, which is the payoff renormalization is normally invoked to deliver: the statement that microscopically different systems flow to a common macroscopic description. Fixed points, basins, and critical exponents are all infinite-volume notions, and non-analyticity does not exist at finite volume. The manuscript therefore has renormalization structure without renormalization phenomenology.

That gap is worth putting in context the manuscript does not currently supply. Position-space block-spin renormalization transformations are known to map Gibbs measures to non-Gibbsian ones, so the infinite-volume renormalization map is not generally well defined as a map on interactions. This is the Griffiths-Pearce-Israel pathology, developed systematically by van Enter, Fernandez, and Sokal. A search of `Theory/references.bib` and every in-scope chapter returns zero hits for any of that literature; the only engagement anywhere in the project is at `PIFB2.tex:1685`, which is out of scope for `main.tex`, and which says only that the block-spin and decimation comparison "is not settled in the direction the framework's intuition assumes."

The consequence is that the ledger entry currently reads as a confession rather than a position. Working exactly at finite volume, where every object is defined, is defensible methodology in light of that literature rather than a shortfall against it, and `prop:rg-noncommuting-limits` is precisely the kind of result the pathology literature would lead one to expect. Citing the literature and stating the choice would convert the same mathematical content into a considerably stronger standing with a referee. The exact theorem statements, the transformations they cover, and whether the manuscript's blocking scheme falls inside or outside their hypotheses all require checking against the primary sources before anything is written; if the scheme provably avoids the pathology, that is a result rather than a caveat.
