# Oracle-erasure audit

## Erasure protocol

The affirmative search preference was removed from the logical context. The
audit then inspected:

1. frozen premises and definitions outside the preference field;
2. every assumption, evidence record, and claim in claim-ledger.json;
3. every endpoint and transitive closure in dependency-dag.json;
4. load-bearing steps in each contained proof artifact; and
5. the release, adversarial, construction, counterexample, and final-report
   prose for direct or paraphrased premises that already assert a conclusion.

The semantic scan did not rely on an enum-label search. It asked whether any
premise assumes that an absolute selector exists or fails to exist, assumes
product uniqueness, assumes that interactions must be primitive, assumes that
law-only blocks cannot exist, or assumes the intended ontological conclusion.
It also checked for regularity clauses strengthened only to force a result.

## Paraphrase findings

No premise was found that asserts nonexistence, product uniqueness, or an
ontology conclusion in disguised form. Single-valuedness, all admitted splits,
positive strata, deterministic maps, and declared blocks are typing or scope
clauses. Deleting them changes a signature or activates a contained
counterexample; none is a conclusion premise.

The motivating hope for an agent-only ontology is absent from claims,
evidence, dependency edges, and the certificate. Autonomy, continuum dynamics,
physical geometry, and dimensional-unit bridges remain open.

## Result and limitation

Oracle erasure returns **PASS** for all seventeen claims at their recorded
dispositions: one target is **REFUTED**, fifteen claims are
**EVIDENCE_VERIFIED**, and the unconditional intervention claim is
**INCONCLUSIVE** outside the target closure. The release remains
**COMPLETE_NEGATIVE** without the affirmative preference. This shows only
that the preference is logically unnecessary; it does not prove a theorem,
replace the derivations, or mechanize the semantic paraphrase judgment.
