# Compact and GL group program

Let (G\le GL(K,\mathbb R)) be closed and compact. Average any Euclidean inner product over
normalized Haar measure:

[
\langle v,w\rangle_G=\int_G\langle gv,gw\rangle_0\,d\mu_H(g).
]

This is positive and (G)-invariant. In an orthonormal basis for it, (G\subseteq O(K)).
Consequently the first theorem can cover arbitrary compact linear subgroups, not just (SO(K)).
If orientation preservation is part of the representation, the group lies in (SO(K)).

For full (GL(K)), polar decomposition exposes an SPD scale/shear sector. It may be scientifically
useful, but raw positive Frobenius curvature is not invariant under conjugation. For
(H=I+\epsilon E_{12}) and (g=\operatorname{diag}(t,1)),

[
\|g^{-1}(H-I)g\|_F^2=\epsilon^2/t^2.
]

A transforming SPD metric (M^g=g^TMg) permits the algebraic dressing

[
W_M(H)=\operatorname{Tr}[M^{-1}(H-I)^TM(H-I)],
]

but existence and normalization further require nondegeneracy of (M), control of (D_AM), a
gauge quotient or slice, and a finite reference law. Full (GL(K)) has no normalized Haar measure.
Any “complexity growth” must be formulated as a gauge-invariant observable before it is physical.

Primary mathematical source: compact-subgroup invariant inner product by Haar averaging, as in
Landesman's *Notes on Lie Groups*.
