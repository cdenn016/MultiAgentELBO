# Oracle-erasure audit

## Method

The affirmative search preference was removed from the logical context. The
audit then read the frozen target, every assumption used by an ancestor claim,
the dependency graph, the direct derivation, the independent reconstruction,
and the counterexample proofs. It searched semantically for any premise that
asserted a desired conclusion, any regularity hypothesis strengthened only to
force the answer, and any proof step justified by expected success rather than
by a typed definition, calculation, or permitted theorem.

The audited closure consists of `target` and these ancestors:

```text
OIE-CONTEXTUAL-CONGRUENCE
OIE-TERMINAL-QUOTIENT
OIE-FINITE-MINIMALITY
OIE-COMPACT-QUOTIENT
OIE-SOFT-MONOID
OIE-BSC-SOFT-SEPARATION
OIE-RANDOMIZED-MONOID
OIE-RANDOMIZED-RANK
OIE-HARD-NONISOMORPHISM
OIE-RANDOMIZED-NONISOMORPHISM
OIE-BOREL-RESPONSE
OIE-FELLER-QUOTIENT
OIE-CIRCLE-PALETTE
OIE-CIRCLE-BLACKWELL
OIE-CIRCLE-SOFT-INCLUSION
```

## Premise-by-premise semantic scan

The response-system assumptions provide only a total monoid multiplication,
identity, response map, and explicit response-compatible quotient triples.
They do not assume contextual equivalence is a congruence, largest, terminal,
or minimum.

The compact assumptions provide compact metrizability, joint continuity,
Hausdorff response space, response continuity, and an explicit quotient-map
hypothesis for compared triples. They do not assume the contextual relation is
closed, the quotient metrizable, or quotient multiplication continuous; those
facts are derived through the countable signature and quotient-map arguments.

The finite soft assumptions provide normalized local kernels and compact
palettes in their actual evaluation topology. They do not assume response
continuity, target-face separation, or experiment nonisomorphism. Normalization
and continuity are derived from topological-order summation and finite
polynomials; separation is derived from total variation.

The randomized assumptions provide independent selector sampling, affine
response, convolution, and the fixed released hard class semantics. They do
not assume hard or randomized nonisomorphism. Rank follows from the displayed
exact minor. The same matrix's response columns independently reproduce the
hard obstruction: the two-element boundary-flip orbit of the `L_1`
`do(E=0)` response is absent from the complete `L_2` hard response image.
Only after this calculation does the affine-vertex restriction yield the
randomized nonisomorphism.

The standard-Borel assumptions provide declared pointwise normalized kernels
and joint evaluation measurability. They do not assume a smooth quotient or
pointwise uniqueness of observational conditionals. The measurable response is
derived by finite kernel recursion, while the nonsmooth and null-version
witnesses prevent either hidden strengthening.

The compact-Feller assumptions state the topology and weak-continuity input
needed for iterated integration. They do not assume the resulting retained-law
response or contextual quotient is continuous. Those conclusions follow from
the compact kernel-integration lemma and the previously derived quotient
theorem.

The circle assumptions provide Haar law, heat semigroup action, Fourier
eigenvalues, and the declared palette. They do not assume strict Blackwell
order or strict response-set inclusion. Both strictness statements are forced
by first-mode Markov contraction. The explicit inequality `0<rho<t-s` is a
genuine witness condition, not a disguised conclusion.

The comparison-category restrictions are fixed data, not outcome assumptions.
They say what counts as the same experiment: typed invertible state maps, one
compatible protocol map, one global response map, ordered external roles, and
heat intertwiners where applicable. Counterexamples show that erasing the
target, reversing time, allowing protocol-dependent response maps, or allowing
arbitrary latent coordinate changes defines a different question.

## Recomputed closure after erasure

Starting only from the typed premises, the algebraic relation proof yields the
largest congruence; its kernel inclusion yields the terminal factor; finiteness
yields the cardinality corollary. The countable signature yields the compact
topological quotient. Finite kernel algebra yields the soft monoid and BSC
diameter obstruction. Convolution plus the exact determinant and affine-vertex
restriction yields randomized nonisomorphism. Kernel recursion yields the
Borel response; compact Feller integration yields the compact quotient. The
heat semigroup and Fourier contraction yield passive equality, strict
Blackwell order, and strict soft inclusion.

No node loses evidence after removal of the preference. The closure therefore
passes oracle erasure. This result shows only that the preference was logically
unnecessary; it is not an additional proof of the theorem.
