# Collective parity lift and joint Fisher proof

## 1. Construction, positivity, and normalization

Let \(\Omega=\{0,1\}^6\), let

\[
P_\theta(x)=\prod_{i=1}^6\theta_i^{x_i}(1-\theta_i)^{1-x_i},\qquad
D(\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^6\theta_i(1-\theta_i),\qquad
\chi(x)=(-1)^{\sum_i x_i},
\]

and fix a scalar \(\kappa\) with \(|\kappa|<1\). Define

\[
Q_{\theta,\kappa}(x)=P_\theta(x)+\kappa\chi(x)D(\theta).\tag{1}
\]

For the bitwise complement \(\bar x\), division by the positive product atom
gives

\[
\frac{D(\theta)}{P_\theta(x)}
=\prod_{i:x_i=0}\theta_i\prod_{i:x_i=1}(1-\theta_i)
=P_\theta(\bar x).
\]

Therefore

\[
Q_{\theta,\kappa}(x)
=P_\theta(x)\left[1+\kappa\chi(x)P_\theta(\bar x)\right].\tag{2}
\]

On the open cube, \(0<P_\theta(\bar x)<1\); hence the bracket is strictly
larger than \(1-|\kappa|>0\). Every one of the 64 atoms is strictly positive.
Also \(\sum_xP_\theta(x)=1\) and \(\sum_x\chi(x)=0\), so
\(\sum_xQ_{\theta,\kappa}(x)=1\).

## 2. All 63 proper marginals

Let \(S\subsetneq\{1,\ldots,6\}\), including \(S=\varnothing\), and fix
\(x_S\). Choose one omitted coordinate \(j\notin S\). In the perturbation
sum over \(x_{S^c}\), terms differing only in \(x_j\) have opposite parity
and equal magnitude \(D(\theta)\), so they cancel. Consequently

\[
\sum_{x_{S^c}}Q_{\theta,\kappa}(x)
=\sum_{x_{S^c}}P_\theta(x)
=\prod_{i\in S}\theta_i^{x_i}(1-\theta_i)^{1-x_i}.\tag{3}
\]

This proves every proper marginal is the corresponding product marginal.
There are \(\sum_{r=0}^5\binom6r=63\) proper subsets. In particular, all
two-bit block marginals and all singleton marginals forget \(\kappa\). The
declared section data, however, are the six singleton Bernoulli marginals
grouped into three agent pairs; the theorem does not silently replace them
with three arbitrary two-bit joint sections.

The interior simplex has dimension 63. Fixing the six singleton Bernoulli
marginals imposes six independent affine constraints, so the associated
Fréchet fiber has dimension \(63-6=57\). Equation (1) supplies only one
one-dimensional parity direction inside that fiber. It is not a classification
or canonical selection of the other 56 directions.

## 3. Smooth right inverse, immersion, and Fisher rank

Let \(E:\Delta_{63}^{\circ}\to(0,1)^6\) extract singleton expectations,
\(E_i(q)=\sum_xx_iq(x)\), and let \(L_\kappa(\theta)=Q_{\theta,\kappa}\).
Equation (3) gives

\[
E\circ L_\kappa=\operatorname{id}_{(0,1)^6}.\tag{4}
\]

The map \(L_\kappa\) is polynomial and hence smooth. Differentiating (4)
gives \(dE\,dL_\kappa=I_6\), so \(dL_\kappa\) is injective everywhere and
\(L_\kappa\) is a rank-six immersion.

The categorical Fisher form on the interior simplex tangent space is
\(\langle u,v\rangle_q=\sum_xu(x)v(x)/q(x)\), which is positive definite on
the zero-sum tangent space. Its pullback by the immersion is therefore
positive definite, has rank six, and has zero radical. For a secondary smooth
section \(s:B\to(0,1)^6\), the further pullback has

\[
\operatorname{rad}(s^*G_\kappa)=\ker ds,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(s^*G_\kappa)=\operatorname{rank}ds.\tag{5}
\]

A vector-bundle quotient requires constant rank of \(ds\). A quotient
manifold metric additionally requires an involutive radical distribution, a
regular leaf space, and basicness of the tensor along the leaves. None follows
merely from (4).

## 4. Exact finite symmetry boundary

Complement a coordinate set \(J\) in both \(x\) and the corresponding
parameters. The product law and \(D\) transform covariantly, while

\[
\chi(C_Jx)=(-1)^{|J|}\chi(x).\tag{6}
\]

Thus fixed scalar \(\kappa\) gives equivariance under every even complement,
including the eight transformations generated by complementing one or more
of the declared pairs \(\{1,2\},\{3,4\},\{5,6\}\). Permutations of agent
pairs are admitted only when the paired agent/channel types are identified.
The toy formula is also accidentally symmetric under within-pair coordinate
swaps, but those swaps exchange the belief/model channel labels and are not a
universal typed gauge symmetry. The executable enumerates only the 48-element
subgroup that preserves the declared partition into three unordered pairs
(pair permutations combined with within-pair swaps). It does not claim to
count every accidental coordinate symmetry; the full coordinate permutation
group \(S_6\) has 720 elements.

For an odd complement and nonzero scalar \(\kappa\), (6) reverses the
perturbation and

\[
(C_J)_*Q_{\theta,\kappa}=Q_{C_J\theta,-\kappa}\ne
Q_{C_J\theta,\kappa}.\tag{7}
\]

Odd-flip equivariance is recovered only by declaring \(\kappa\) a
pseudoscalar that transforms as \(\kappa\mapsto-\kappa\). The six singleton
sections do not force that declaration.

## 5. General joint-score residual formula

Let \(q_\theta\) be any strictly positive smooth joint family whose singleton
marginal of \(X_i\) is \(\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\theta_i)\). Define

\[
J_i=\partial_i\log q_\theta(X),\qquad
s_i=\frac{X_i-\theta_i}{\theta_i(1-\theta_i)},\qquad
R_i=J_i-s_i.
\]

No sign may be discarded in the general identity:

\[
G_{\rm joint}
=\mathbb E[ss^{\mathsf T}]
+\mathbb E[sR^{\mathsf T}]
+\mathbb E[Rs^{\mathsf T}]
+\mathbb E[RR^{\mathsf T}].\tag{8}
\]

Equivalently, relative to the product marginal metric
\(G_{\rm prod}=\operatorname{diag}([\theta_i(1-\theta_i)]^{-1})\),

\[
G_{\rm joint}-G_{\rm prod}
=\bigl(\mathbb E[ss^{\mathsf T}]-G_{\rm prod}\bigr)
+\mathbb E[sR^{\mathsf T}]
+\mathbb E[Rs^{\mathsf T}]
+\mathbb E[RR^{\mathsf T}].\tag{9}
\]

Only the last term is automatically positive semidefinite. The other three
terms are signed and can make the total correction indefinite.

For the six-bit parity family, (3) gives pairwise independence, hence
\(\mathbb E[s_is_j]=0\) for \(i\ne j\) and
\(\mathbb E[s_i^2]=[\theta_i(1-\theta_i)]^{-1}\). Differentiating the
singleton score expectation through the finite sum gives

\[
\mathbb E[s_iJ_j]=\delta_{ij}[\theta_i(1-\theta_i)]^{-1}.\tag{10}
\]

Subtracting \(\mathbb E[s_is_j]\) proves
\(\mathbb E[s_iR_j]=0\) for all \(i,j\). Thus, and only after these two
family-specific facts,

\[
G_{\rm joint}=G_{\rm prod}+\mathbb E[RR^{\mathsf T}].\tag{11}
\]

## 6. Positive-definite parity residual for nonzero kappa

Write \(h_x=D/P_\theta(x)=P_\theta(\bar x)>0\). From (2),

\[
R_i(x)=\frac{\kappa\chi(x)h_x}{1+\kappa\chi(x)h_x}\,u_i(x_i),\qquad
u_i(0)=\frac1{\theta_i},\quad
u_i(1)=-\frac1{1-\theta_i}.\tag{12}
\]

If \(\kappa\ne0\), the scalar prefactor in (12) is nonzero for every atom. If
\(a^{\mathsf T}R(x)=0\) for every \(x\), then
\(\sum_ia_iu_i(x_i)=0\) for every binary \(x\). Comparing two states that
differ only in coordinate \(j\) gives

\[
a_j\left(-\frac1{1-\theta_j}-\frac1{\theta_j}\right)=0,
\]

so every \(a_j=0\). The six residual score functions are linearly independent.
Because every \(Q_{\theta,\kappa}(x)>0\), their Gram matrix
\(\mathbb E[RR^{\mathsf T}]\) is positive definite for every nonzero
\(\kappa\). At \(\kappa=0\), it vanishes.

## 7. Exact center

At \(\theta_i=1/2\), put \(c=\kappa/64\). Then

\[
Q_{\theta,\kappa}(x)=\frac{1+c\chi(x)}{64},\qquad
J_i(x)=\frac{s_i(x_i)}{1+c\chi(x)},\qquad s_i=\pm2.
\]

Half the states have each parity. Four unused bits cancel every off-diagonal
sum, and direct summation yields

\[
G_{\rm joint}=\frac4{1-c^2}I_6,\qquad
\mathbb E[RR^{\mathsf T}]=\frac{4c^2}{1-c^2}I_6.\tag{13}
\]

For \(\kappa=1/2\), \(c=1/128\), so

\[
G_{\rm joint}=\frac{65536}{16383}I_6,\qquad
\mathbb E[RR^{\mathsf T}]=\frac4{16383}I_6.\tag{14}
\]

## 8. Why the PSD correction is family-specific

For the older two-bit lift at \(\theta=(1/2,1/2)\) and \(\kappa=1/2\), the law
is \((9,7,7,9)/32\) and its Fisher matrix is

\[
G_2=\frac1{63}
\begin{pmatrix}256&-32\\-32&256\end{pmatrix}.\tag{15}
\]

Relative to \(4I_2\), the eigenvalues are \(-4/9\) and \(4/7\): the
correction is indefinite. There, the two singleton variables are correlated,
so \(\mathbb E[ss^{\mathsf T}]-4I_2\) and the cross terms in (9) do not
vanish. Explicitly, with \(c=1/8\), the residual Gram matrix has diagonal
\(4/63\) and off-diagonal \(-1/126\), while each signed cross matrix has zero
diagonal and off-diagonal \(-1/2\), and
\(\mathbb E[ss^{\mathsf T}]-4I_2\) has off-diagonal \(1/2\). Their sum is
exactly (15) minus \(4I_2\). Thus (11), not merely the definition of a
residual, is the six-bit parity-family theorem.
