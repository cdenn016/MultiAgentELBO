# The rescaling laboratory in figures

Regenerate with `PYTHONPATH=src python run_rescaling_figures.py` (about two minutes; every
panel except figure 1 and the story map is recomputed live from the validated declared seed,
which the script checks against the published invariants before drawing anything). Figure 1
carries the audited fixed-point numbers of amendments 3–4 as recorded constants; their
provenance is the 2026-08-18 audit (`docs/audits/2026-08-18-rescaling-lab-audit.md`), which
reproduced every one of them. Measurement declarations live in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-08-17-rescaling-map-design.md` (amendments 3–14); results
authority is `docs/STATUS.md` §16.

**Figure 1 — Passive versus regenerated.** (A) One-step pair retention versus boundary
thickness: sublinear growth saturating below one, so thicker boundaries cannot rescue the
passive channel (M-bundle). (B) The passive fixed structures are factorized (pair sup machine
zero) while the regenerated composites converge to interacting fixed structures above their
own one-step injection floor (M-regen; sustained-over-injected 1.246). Horizontal glue is
re-bound per level, not inherited.

**Figure 2 — Capacity in two instruments.** (A) Sup-norm retention with 9-state versus
27-label root-framed sector parents; dotted marks are the retired values measured with the
gauge-variant charge (audit F8, amendment 8). (B) The same comparison in law units: boundary
mutual-information retention, bounded by one as a data-processing theorem (amendment 9). The
gray dashes show the square-law prediction R_sup²: it lands on the measured bar at k = 1
(single cut edge) and overshoots at k = 3, where coherent aggregation inflates the sup norm —
the deflation of the sector gain from +34% to +10% is the same effect.

**Figure 3 — The coupling-scale sweep (M-bind, amendment 11).** (A) The boundary-information
ceiling grows as λ² across four decades. (B) Retention is flat: transmission is a property of
the Bayes kernel, not of the signal. (C) The partition posterior's class masses do not move —
no formation transition on this ray; the singleton preference is structural.

**Figure 4 — Environmental anchors (M-anchor, amendment 12).** Partition class masses versus
anchor strength for three pinning patterns. Uniform anchors flip the modal class to direct
collapse (A); shared-pair anchors pull the anchor-aligned ratio-2 blocking up eightfold
without crossing (B); the distinct control anti-binds (C). Condensation follows shared
evidence, and the posterior selects the placement matching the environment.

**Figure 5 — Process versus landscape (M-flow, amendment 12).** Quenched (frozen-flow
coordinate update, amendment 10) against annealed (exact joint marginal, the target of the
coupled dynamics) class masses. On the bare instance the annealed object makes direct modal
where the quenched one prefers singletons — the Jensen gap between the two declared theory
objects is qualitative, not a correction. Shared-pair anchors restore the singleton verdict.

**Figure 6 — Saturation and inertia (amendments 13–14).** (A) The aligned placement's mass
saturates by κ_env = 32: pinning saturates before condensing. (B) Finite-inertia shared
environments beat private duplicates ninefold at zero inertia, but the aligned mass rises
monotonically to the pinned limit — alignment and correlation are competing channels, and
alignment wins at this seed. (C) The induced pair coupling (the correlation channel) vanishes
as the environment freezes: a point-mass environment aligns but cannot correlate.

**Figure 7 — The story map.** The measurement chain of 2026-08-17/18 with one-line verdicts:
blue for passive/quenched negatives, green for the restorations (regeneration, environment,
process), orange for the corrections the lab imposed on itself (the retired charge, the
point-mass limit, the competing channels).
